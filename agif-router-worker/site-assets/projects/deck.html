What can you build in 3 weeks?

No grades!!

Group size is bounded by our presentation day; if we’re going to give each one its due, should be no more than 5-6 groups.

Aim for a minimum viable project to dive deep on by the 24th, and present it on the 25th.

What counts as a project?

Something difficult enough to force collaboration, and not necessarily a written output.

Each of you has a domain where you know more than the rest of us. Try starting from there. And be ambitious.

Three weeks isn’t much time!

But better a minimum viable product for an ambitious idea than polished miniaturism.

These are just suggestions; make what you want!

Build

So many cool things can be built with agents now. What can you make?

Evaluate

An eval for your own domain of expertise, with some governance relevance. Vibe-code from scratch, or come up with a specification and some pilot studies.

Write

A constitution or model specification. Or a case for a post-AGI institution. Vulnerability-scan, obstacle-map, initiate the launch sequence.

Legislate

A piece of model legislation, or a regulatory approach to a problem that current law does not reach.

Organise

A pitch for an AGI governance organisation that should exist.

SF Government Graph

Michael Adams at CivLab. Live platform.

Participation

Consult

“Consult is a new internal tool offering civil service departments high-quality, assured AI topic modelling capabilities for consultation processing.”

The UK government’s Incubator for AI, within DSIT. Operational system, with a published algorithmic transparency record.

Law & administration

STARA

“STARA can dramatically reduce the time for discerning the law.”

An automated system capable of performing accurate “statutory surveys”—compilations of all provisions relevant to a particular legal issue, together with detailed annotations and reasoning.

Faiz Surani, Lindsey Gailmard, Allison Casasola, Varun Magesh, Emily Robitschek and Daniel Ho at Stanford RegLab. Closed beta, plus an enacted San Francisco ordinance.
