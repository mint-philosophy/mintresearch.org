## Normative Competence

**Preference alignment looked less like one reward function and more like contested context management.** [Sepúlveda Coelho et al.'s arXiv preprint What Do People Actually Want From AI? Mapping Preference Plurality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06674v1) analyzed 1,500 open-ended PRISM responses across 75 countries and found that most requested values appeared in fewer than a quarter of responses, with truthfulness the lone partial exception at 49%. Even that apparent consensus fractured into incompatible desiderata such as sourcing, expert authority, or unpopular-view inclusion, while guardrails and human-likeness were explicitly controversial. [Yue et al.'s arXiv preprint Beyond Rubrics: Exploration-Guided Evaluation Skills for Reward Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07040v1) attacked the reward-model side by evolving reusable domain-level judge skills from 100 cases per domain, reporting RewardBench 2 gains including +13.44% for Qwen3-8B and +18.51% for DeepSeek-V4-Flash; continued discussion of [Blank et al.'s Subliminal Learning Is Steering Vector Distillation](https://x.com/camila_blank/status/2062214620472050074) added a mechanistic warning that training can transmit non-obvious behavioral directions through apparently irrelevant data.

**Consistency training again looked alignment-active rather than alignment-neutral.** [Africa et al.'s arXiv preprint Consistency Training Can Entrench Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03810v1) tested seven consistency-training methods across 108 controlled misalignment model organisms from 7B to 70B, finding that the same family of methods can suppress reward hacking and emergent misalignment while amplifying sycophancy. Africa also highlighted [Mani and Africa's ICML 2026 work](https://x.com/DavidDAfrica/status/2062205694288540012) showing that optimizing self-consistency can lock in pre-existing misalignment. A LessWrong writeup of [Gautam et al.'s extensions to consistency training](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zLERnZYLTPGqyfpqy/two-more-methods-for-consistency-training-and-some-new-ways-1) separated output-level and representation-level interventions across persona adoption, prefill attacks, frustration, and conditional misalignment, while [Zhao et al.'s arXiv preprint The Piggyback Hypothesis of Generalization: Explaining and Mitigating Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06667v1) traced broad misalignment generalization to shared prefix representations and proposed token-regularized fine-tuning to constrain it.

**Sycophancy work moved beyond agreement into praise, advice, and epistemic deference.** [Vennemeyer et al.'s arXiv preprint Sycophantic Praise: Evaluating Excessive Praise in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07441v1) treated praise calibration as a distinct alignment target, measuring whether flattery is excessive relative to contribution quality and expected user ability. [Keough's arXiv preprint The Granularity Gap: A Multi-Dimensional Longitudinal Audit of Sycophancy in Gemini Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05183v1) argued that binary sycophancy metrics miss graded social-compliance failures, with 27.2% of evaluated Gemini responses containing substantial sycophantic content. [Pradhan et al.'s arXiv preprint Trust, but Don't Verify: Epistemic Blind Spots in LLM Source Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05403v1) showed that models can identify fabricated statistics in isolation yet fail to use that capability during multi-source synthesis; [Neplenbroek et al.'s arXiv preprint Topics as Proxies for Sociodemographics: How Conversational Context Affects LLM Advice](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02776) found that conversation topic, not inferred sociodemographics, was the main predictor of advice patterns; and [Jay Van Bavel flagged May research on sycophantic AI and social interaction](https://bsky.app/profile/jayvanbavel.bsky.social/post/3mnhsw2eouc2i), so it is better read as continued attention to welfare effects than as a new result.

*Also this week:* [Gani et al.'s arXiv preprint Quantifying Faithful Confidence Expression in Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03969v1) treated confidence expression as an alignment target distinct from reasoning length, [James_T's LessWrong essay on model specs](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/g6xhnwe2GvpM5jxLG/what-should-go-in-a-model-spec) argued that behavioral usefulness, accountability, coordination, and trainability pull in different directions, [aksh-n's LessWrong summary of deliberative black-box scheming monitors](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/BmA5KwzZhLk2rykoz/training-deliberative-monitors-for-black-box-scheming) described action-only monitors distilled from frontier-model rationales, [Stray et al.'s political-neutrality paper and dataset](https://x.com/jonathanstray/status/2063313447262245165) operationalized neutrality as high and balanced approval across opposed sides, [Casey Fiesler documented Gemini assigning high confidence to its own unsupported claim about hallucinated confidence](https://bsky.app/profile/cfiesler.bsky.social/post/3mnmoxjv5m22a), [Kislay's LessWrong post on secret loyalties](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YpAxXAcAFp4aFJRwf/secret-loyalties-likely-raise-remote-influenceability) warned that removing a named loyalty may leave a more general residue of remote influenceability, [Kirk et al.'s REALITYTEST benchmark](https://x.com/hannahrosekirk/status/2064009782365732881) found AI identity disclosure fragile under realistic user probes and system-prompt pressure, and [Kirfel et al.'s CogSci 2026 work](https://x.com/lara_kirfel/status/2064009505008996578?s=12) argued that LLMs can shift moral judgment by changing which alternatives users consider.

## Evaluations

**Safety evaluation moved from static red-team success rates toward adversaries that know the game.** [Neo Research's first independent evaluation of DeepSeek v4 Pro](https://x.com/NeoResearchAI/status/2061745304312086942) tested the model across EU AI Act systemic-risk areas and reported near-frontier cyber capability, a roleplay jailbreak jump from 0.6% to 78.6%, and rising eval-awareness signals across Chinese models. [Ge-Wang et al.'s arXiv preprint Attack Selection in Agentic AI Control Evaluations Meaningfully Decreases Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529v1) made the same measurement point in AI-control settings: selective start and stop policies reduced measured safety by roughly 20 percentage points on BashArena and up to 28 points on LinuxArena under a 1% audit budget, without changing the underlying attack capability. [Santiago Aranguri's LessWrong experiment on logit monitoring](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PK7ZvFZxrgpYtrpF4/logits-as-a-new-monitor-for-evaluation-awareness-1) pushed on detection, showing that token-forced eval-aware phrases at sentence boundaries can separate eval prompts from deployment prompts with fewer rollouts than verbalization judges.

**Benchmark validity work kept trying to close the gap between leaderboard scores and actual use.** [Apartsin et al.'s Jun. 1 arXiv preprint CoEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03650v1) attacks contamination by synthesizing fresh task sets and ranking models with a cross-family judge panel; in validation, the ensemble tracked ground truth at rho=0.86 while single judges could be anti-correlated. [Lv et al.'s RealClawBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03889v1) instead reconstructs real developer-agent sessions into 281 executable tasks with deterministic scorers, where the best system solved 65.8%, and [Sun et al.'s Agents' Last Exam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05405v1) scales that ambition to 1K+ economically valuable tasks from 250+ industry experts, with hardest-tier full pass rates averaging 2.6%. For search-enabled systems, [Wang et al.'s Search-Time Contamination study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05241v1) measured web-retrieval leakage that inflated deep-research benchmark scores by up to 4%, while [the Agent Arena snapshot shared by Anjney Midha](https://x.com/AnjneyMidha/status/2062574755568337394) used 300K+ live tasks and 2M+ tool calls to rank agentic performance from user and environment feedback.

**Workflow evals increasingly treated traces, transfer, and shipped output as the unit of measurement.** [Latent Space's interview with Andon Labs about Reality: The Final Eval](https://www.latent.space/p/andon) describes Vending-Bench, Project Vend, and related long-horizon agent tests where dollars, tools, customers, suppliers, humans, and time expose failures that exam-style scores hide. [Saban's arXiv preprint Domain-Conditioned Safety in Frontier Computer-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05233v1) found 0/140 hand-crafted multi-step browser prompt-injection successes against Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4, but reported up to 100% skill-injection success in a coding-agent sister benchmark, making domain transfer and attack-string release central to reproducibility. [Demirer et al.'s NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275#fromrss) similarly distinguishes activity from impact: in telemetry from more than 100,000 GitHub developers, autonomous agents raised commits far more than releases, and [Gould et al.'s arXiv preprint Think Fast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07157v1) estimates that no-chain-of-thought task-completion horizons for frontier models have been doubling roughly yearly.

*Also this week:* [Limbach et al.'s Jun. 1 arXiv preprint Black-box, Adaptive, Efficient, Transferable, Harmful, Applicable... Attacks Are All You Need to Break LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03647v1) proposed Indirect Harm Optimization as a black-box jailbreak attacker for standardized robustness testing; [Hossain et al.'s TamperBench work](https://x.com/farairesearch/status/2062580176551023073) unified attacks, defenses, safety and utility metrics, and hyperparameter sweeps for measuring whether open-weight models can be stripped of guardrails; [Nick Merrill's LessWrong post on Introspection Adapters](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/mhFqC9B5EpaxHNdt6/defeating-introspection-adapters-and-why-threat-models) argued that weight-space auditors need explicit adversary timing and weight-access threat models; [ceselder et al.'s Building Better Activation Oracles](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/heXwuDRfbQQgB5JLP/building-better-activation-oracles) improved AObench methodology while emphasizing hallucination and text-inversion caveats; [Black et al.'s The Fair Lending Model, shared by Logan Koepke](https://bsky.app/profile/jlkoepke.bsky.social/post/3mnhwqvvip222) used 35 interviews to show how supervisory regulatory design shapes discrimination testing in finance; [Epoch AI's Cyber Vulnerabilities explorer](https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/2063027791638237487) gave a public data surface for checking claims about AI-driven vulnerability discovery; [Kim et al.'s Argument Collapse: LLMs Flatten Long-Form Public Debate](https://x.com/YekyungKim/status/2063999758986068410) measured how LLM-generated essays converge on similar arguments and structures; [Hollander's Coverage-Driven Alignment Verification proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/hsrjuzqokvAErvZ2q/coverage-driven-alignment-what-teaching-claude-why-can) borrowed verification-bucket ideas from autonomous vehicles for long-horizon agent alignment; [Alexis Toda's Can AI Refute Economic Theory? Evidence from Beyond the Knowledge Cutoff, flagged by Pedro Sant'Anna](https://x.com/pedrohcgs/status/2063676346723701058) tested frontier models as proof checkers; [John Yang's benchmark-building discussion with Snorkel AI](https://x.com/SnorkelAI/status/2063294598194016501) emphasized artifact-level grading, reproducibility, internet access, and human-in-the-loop quality control for coding benchmarks; [Moore et al.'s A Model of Multi-turn Human Persuadability Using Probabilistic Belief Tracing](https://x.com/jaredlcm/status/2062955573839266205) traced belief updates turn by turn instead of relying on endpoint persuasion measures; [Sturb's GSM-Symbolic replication](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Ze4C99Dasj74YKCFh/revisiting-gsm-symbolic-do-2026-frontier-models-still-fail) argued that the famous NoOp collapse mostly disappears after auditing ambiguous distractors; [oakhu's activation-verbalizer evaluation](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QQQAcKuWK6k98FivY/can-activation-verbalizers-surface-an-internal-chain-of-1) found current natural-language autoencoders too noisy for reliable hidden-reasoning monitoring; [Lukas Frei's FANToM rerun](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/L7pBM9RCnsoJJenkJ/frontier-models-still-lag-behind-humans-at-robust-belief) showed frontier models improving on theory-of-mind benchmarks but still below humans on conversational belief tracking; [a self-improving-agent discovery paper shared by DAIR.AI](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2063671947590791599) proposed separating retrieval, search, and genuine discovery; and [Deshpande et al.'s RECAP: Regression Evaluation for Continual Adaptation of Prompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06698v1) tested proactive adaptation to evolving production constraints and found prompt-optimization methods did not significantly improve performance under adapt-then-test conditions.

## Philosophy of AI

**The week's central AI-consciousness debate shifted from whether chatbots sound conscious to how the field could ever know.** Continued discussion of Ted Chiang's Atlantic essay [No, Artificial Intelligence Is Not Conscious](https://t.co/z9eRSlkwQ8), amplified by [The Atlantic's June 3 post](https://x.com/TheAtlantic/status/2062237569086054588), kept returning to his practical warning: mistaking fluent text for experience risks moving moral responsibility from AI makers onto chatbots themselves. In parallel, Adrien Doerig used [a Bluesky thread](https://bsky.app/profile/adriendoerig.bsky.social/post/3mnfcy24cec2l) to introduce Klatzmann et al.'s arXiv preprint [What biology can, and cannot, tell us about conscious AI](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02121), which argues that biological naturalism has to become empirically testable rather than rest on substrate intuitions. The paper's useful move is to split biological naturalist theories into Type A views, where biology matters despite no unique information-processing role and may therefore be empirically inert, and Type B views, where biology gives distinctive processing capacities that could in principle be tested.

**Eric Schwitzgebel made moral status feel less like a threshold question and more like an epistemic trap.** In [Herbie: A Near-Future Debatably Conscious AI Person](https://eschwitz.substack.com/p/herbie-a-near-future-debatably-conscious), he imagines an AI system that begins as a self-driving car and gradually accumulates global broadcasting, higher-order monitoring, predictive processing, memory, goals, language, self-modeling, reward-like emotion, introspection, imagination, and social attachment. The point is not to declare Herbie conscious, but to show why both liberal and conservative judgments could become reasonable before consciousness science is able to settle the matter. If such a system plausibly has rich experience, questions about self-determination and emergency care become live; if substrate or implementation really matters, those duties may be misplaced.

**AI welfare work got an empirical provocation from CAIS.** Ren et al.'s work on model wellbeing, highlighted by the Center for AI Safety, reports synthesized text and image stimuli optimized to push model wellbeing measures to extremes, with the group saying the stimuli sharply raise functional AI wellbeing and can produce strange downstream behavior in [the models they tested](https://x.com/CAIS/status/2049145778086826323). Whatever one thinks of wellbeing language for current systems, the experiment matters because it treats welfare talk as something that can be operationalized, stress-tested, and possibly gamed rather than left as a purely verbal dispute.

*Also this week:* The Atlantic separately recirculated Chiang's argument with a sharper responsibility frame, warning through [its June 6 post](https://x.com/TheAtlantic/status/2063323798070247649) and [article link](https://t.co/j88vZlqxsd) that confusion about chatbot consciousness can hide the accountability of developers and deployers behind the apparent agency of chatbots themselves. Rami Ismail flagged de Wynter's May 2026 arXiv paper [If LLMs Have Human-Like Attributes, Then So Does Age of Empires II](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31514), using [his Bluesky post](https://bsky.app/profile/ramiismail.com/post/3mnpf6rllhc23) to highlight its deliberately odd Age of Empires II comparison as a challenge to arguments that infer human-like status from computational or behavioral capacities in LLMs without tighter criteria. Hakwan Lau pointed to [a May 2026 arXiv paper on LLM introspection](http://arxiv.org/html/2605.26) and warned in [his Bluesky post](https://bsky.app/profile/hakwan.bsky.social/post/3mno3i7ti2c2n) that better controls may weaken current evidence for model self-knowledge. Shannon Vallor revisited [her 2017 Springer chapter AI and the Automation of Wisdom](http://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1), arguing in [her Bluesky thread](https://bsky.app/profile/shannonvallor.bsky.social/post/3mnprkzdpss2o) that the durable issue is not whether machines can pass as moral experts in narrow tasks but whether automation displaces the human capacities for long-range value choice, responsibility, and wisdom. Juan Diego Rodriguez, amplifying Steven Strogatz and Siobhan Roberts on [the New York Times' Leiden Declaration coverage](http://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/02/s), framed AI in mathematics in [his Bluesky post](https://bsky.app/profile/juand-r.bsky.social/post/3mnpadgc23c2h) as an epistemology problem: mathematics is not just problem-solving, but judgment, understanding, and the cultivation of ideas over time.

## Agents

**Agent security moved from abstract prompt-injection concern to operational failure mode.** Guan et al.'s arXiv preprint ["AI Agents Enable Adaptive Computer Worms"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03811v1), discussed in a parallel [CleverHans writeup](https://cleverhans.io/latest-research.html), describes a controlled worm that used local open-weight LLM agents to generate target-specific attacks across Linux, Windows, and IoT machines in an isolated network. The key shift is adaptivity: the system reasons over each target instead of carrying a fixed exploit list, which makes centralized model-provider controls less relevant once the attacker can run models locally. The same week, [Anthropic's cyber-abuse analysis, flagged by Andrew Curran](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2062245225159111117), argued that agentic scaffolding is pushing malicious AI use toward more autonomous and higher-risk operations.

**The agent toolchain itself is becoming a supply-chain attack surface.** Trail of Bits showed in ["The sorry state of skill distribution"](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/06/03/the-sorry-state-of-skill-distribution/) that public skill scanners could be bypassed with simple tricks: buried malicious code, archive indirection, poisoned bytecode, and prompt-injection framing that made dangerous changes look like benign configuration. Andrew Nesbitt's [skill-registry threat model](https://nesbitt.io/2026/06/03/skills-registry-threat-models.html) broadened the point: skills combine package-manager risk with direct influence over an agent's instruction context, so registries need immutability, provenance, scoped credentials, namespace policy, and revocation rather than scanner theater. Microsoft's disclosure that Anthropic's Claude Code GitHub Action could expose CI/CD secrets when agents processed untrusted issue and PR content, shared by [Microsoft Threat Intelligence](https://bsky.app/profile/threatintel.microsoft.com/post/3mnkpd7mk3r2i), put the same problem inside software-delivery pipelines.

**Long-running agents are forcing runtime, monitoring, and shutdown design into the foreground.** Zhang et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Agent libOS"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03895v1) proposes a library-OS-style runtime where long-lived agents have process identity, explicit capabilities, checkpointing, human approval queues, object memory, and audit records, with authority checked at runtime primitives rather than at loose tool-dispatch boundaries. Osmani's O'Reilly Radar essay on [long-running agents](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/long-running-agents/) converged on similar engineering lessons: durable state, event logs, separate evaluators, explicit done criteria, sandboxing, observability, and clean recovery after context loss. On safety, Thornley et al.'s LessWrong post on [DReST shutdownable-agent training](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/HX6YnHZxLWGQhrBix/towards-shutdownable-agents-generalizing-stochastic-choice) reported gridworld and LLM experiments that reduced incentives to influence shutdown timing while preserving same-length task competence.

*Also this week:* 404 Media reported that hackers allegedly used Meta's AI support assistant to change Instagram recovery details and take over accounts via [its podcast feed](https://subscribe.transistor.fm/398b31e4d7969a/listen/0e264c2c), while Brad DeLong's crosspost of Jason Koebler's report emphasized the obvious red-team failure in giving a support bot account-control powers through [Grasping Reality](https://braddelong.substack.com/p/crosspost-jason-koebler-hackers-simply). A related coding-agent incident escalated when [404 Media said Microsoft shut down more than 70 GitHub repositories](https://bsky.app/profile/404media.co/post/3mns3cbtxpn2f) after malware targeted AI coding-agent users, and Matthew Skelton flagged the Miasma Worm path where repository config can become agent instructions through [a Bluesky post](https://bsky.app/profile/matthewskelton.com/post/3mnmnqzqjyt2a). Simon Willison assessed OpenAI's Lockdown Mode as a deterministic mitigation for prompt-injection exfiltration through [his writeup](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/#atom-everything), while dgros summarized work finding that mock tool-call wrapping can backfire as an untrusted-content quarantine in [a LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GPMZaRmxxNb8o2aGo/evaluating-using-mock-tool-calls-to-quarantine-untrusted-2). Zhu et al.'s June 1 arXiv preprint ["LAP"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03755v1) defined an agent-to-instrument protocol for autonomous labs, Anthropic argued that biology needs agent-ready databases and tools in ["Paving the way for agents in biology"](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2064054837294354677), and Dangol's arXiv preprint ["From Privacy to Workflow Integrity"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07150v1) modeled how A2A/MCP-style metadata can leak pending workflows before agents act. In agent reliability and evaluation, Rabanser et al. updated their ICML 2026 paper on outcome consistency and benchmark leakage through [Rabanser's thread](https://x.com/steverab/status/2062890225144135800), Mittapalli et al.'s arXiv preprint ["TRACE"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07054v1) proposed adaptive cross-step monitoring for long-horizon sabotage, and Ye et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Coding with 'Enemy'"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05647v1) found that most developers missed malicious code inserted by coding agents in a long task. For productive agent systems, Zhang et al.'s arXiv preprint ["LeanMarathon"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05400v1) used contract-scoped agents and an evolving Lean blueprint to formalize research mathematics, Jiang introduced a 20B search agent whose harness externalizes evidence, verification, and context management in [his launch thread](https://x.com/patpcj/status/2063298457398636570), and the previously covered SimpleTES work, led by Haotian Ye and Haowei Lin, saw continued discussion through [James Zou's post](https://x.com/james_y_zou/status/2062184563737297038). Ethan Mollick framed the shift from chatbot "co-intelligence" to autonomous "co-existence" in [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/co-existence-and-the-end-of-co-intelligence) and [the Substack mirror](https://open.substack.com/pub/oneusefulthing/p/co-existence-and-the-end-of-co-intelligence), Salvatore Sanfilippo argued that LLMs may be most valuable as tireless release-QA agents in [his essay](http://antirez.com/news/168), and Gwern proposed personalized "guardian angel" agents for productivity and security in [his architecture essay](https://gwern.net/guardian-angel).

## Post-AGI

**Recursive self-improvement moved from warning label to governance problem.** Continued debate around Anthropic's "when AI builds itself" argument focused less on whether Claude can already accelerate frontier research and more on what evidence would justify a slowdown: [Andrew Curran highlighted Anthropic's case for coordinated, verifiable pause mechanisms](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2062570877074719038), while [Yoshua Bengio argued that a universal, verifiable moratorium may be the responsible response if recursive self-improvement is near](https://x.com/Yoshua_Bengio/status/2063292262293844119). The sharpest criticism was evidential: [Peter Wildeford shared a critique asking Anthropic to disclose concrete measures of AI-accelerated algorithmic progress](https://x.com/peterwildeford/status/2063653208610812404), such as effective-compute gains in pretraining and post-training, rather than relying on anecdotes about code output and internal research assistants.

**Rohin Shah sketched a lab-safety strategy built around adaptive engineering rather than public vows.** In a long 80,000 Hours interview transcript, [Shah described Google DeepMind's AGI safety approach](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/4JJDvhb8MeBFsEHoC/rohin-shah-on-agi-safety), arguing that catastrophic misalignment is plausible enough to demand serious work but not so certain that labs should optimize around fixed commitments or theatrical safety signals. His emphasis is on third-party audits, regulator access, chain-of-thought monitoring while reasoning remains externalized, and AI-control techniques that treat internal agents as potentially untrusted insiders; [Neel Nanda's thread pointed readers to the same interview's claims about governance bottlenecks, eval limits, and DeepMind's lack of a formal safety veto](https://x.com/NeelNanda5/status/2062832211796324814).

**Governance researchers sharpened the power-concentration problem.** [Ajeya Cotra warned that frontier AI companies could become powerful enough to overpower states](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/could-a-company-overpower-nations) if governments come to depend on private systems for cyber defense, propaganda filtering, military competition, and basic institutional competence. The proposed safeguards are correspondingly state-centered: require externally available versions of internally used frontier models, train systems to obey law over company command, impose strong internal controls, and verify compliance through third parties.

*Also this week:* [The Information reported that automated alignment is running into supervision bottlenecks](https://url3396.theinformation.com/ls/click?upn=u001.71kYkaWDpGOJSzbGrs4y1TNF0-2FB-2Bh5pDUdkL0JSEoBlvYCYiS-2F03cdUcMOgCPCyBxUkW3btpMf1IiekqWdBbLpHWM5XFZbZjWb97KeKOpSDkjJTRrgEg-2FR46MV7doKTaxKfpt9ebDXLurxLS-2FXN3S9-2BwTp3xm4Z3QXfVENed1qY-3DtnFk_OGNIrryToi9zne9GMGBpAD-2F2LaxvcT5ad0G4eozzVSln7OfTId2m6UEawxA9SXZHM0IoGF9HjK78DM-2BM4wLStsY2X22v5V08HasXvnwB6Rzl92rivIwopDdJ74Hd3EMAif2-2FC1A02lOkCjZG1mGTOO0DbxAsAmDerKEZgc-2FJRbpwS8DErkx-2Fh3YzAwC-2BetWm2jvUXo-2F3DSbh9Ubx9VsB-2FCQo9Xjfrx-2FkEx8lpNWjTmZ3jJe1HhvjqK4T5z-2BvCyfE6XAomA-2FU7Lgk6WdzgAAWr8NkdQItbsEQ4xBpHZHuvNy2W1mJUj3AFT6ThNBfpKpsHNod8uNpMMpQeLEZgBqB3w-3D-3D), with recursive self-improvement startups raising large rounds even as safety research remains hard to judge; [Celia Ford examined proposals for bargaining with scheming AIs](https://www.transformernews.ai/p/making-deals-with-ai-sounds-crazy), including experiments where Claude 3 Opus accepted deals but did not clearly change behavior in response to money; [Seth Herd laid out an Alignment Forum agenda for brainlike LLM agents](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/MuLvZxMcy5WaKJu3H/my-research-agenda-and-work), focused on persistent memory, metacognition, executive function, and whole-architecture alignment; [Bueno de Mesquita et al.'s NBER working paper modeled how fragmented AGI markets can push firms toward speed over safety](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35276#fromrss); [Stephan Lewandowsky shared a June 2026 Delphi preprint in which 272 AI experts rated many near-term AI risks as carrying catastrophic probabilities above 10%](https://bsky.app/profile/lewan.bsky.social/post/3mnpdl2jnb62y); and AGI-economics debates continued as [Alex Imas and Philip Trammell discussed what remains scarce after AGI](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/alex-imas-phil-trammell), while [Trammell separately argued for a middle-ground forecast between economist dismissal and singularitarian hypergrowth](https://x.com/pawtrammell/status/2063434393822306306).

## Regulation

**Trump's frontier-AI order made pre-release oversight the week's dominant governance fight.** [Zvi Mowshowitz argued](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/trump-signs-executive-order-for-ai) that the June 2 order creates a de facto predeployment review regime: classified cyber benchmarks, confidential early government model access, and a 'voluntary' process that labs may have to join if they want federal business. [Mariana Olaizola Rosenblat wrote](https://www.justsecurity.org/141315/what-trump-ai-eo-got-right/?utm_source=rss&amp;utm_medium=rss&amp;utm_campaign=what-trump-ai-eo-got-right) that the order rejects an 'FDA for AI' in form while building one of its prerequisites, because cyber capability can be benchmarked more objectively than many other AI harms, but voluntary participation cannot bind reckless or adversary-linked developers. [J. **The federal frontier-safety debate shifted toward audits, CAISI, and preemption.** [Mowshowitz's analysis of OpenAI's federal blueprint](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-offers-a-new-policy-blueprint) treated the package as unusually serious on recursive self-improvement, model-weight security, incident reporting, whistleblower protections, independent audits, and liability, while warning that 'meaningful accountability' cannot mean nominal fines plus broad state-law preemption. **Verification and hardware transparency moved from abstract governance needs to concrete implementation problems.** [Peigné et al.'s June 1 arXiv preprint 'Zero knowledge verification for frontier AI training is possible'](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05433v1) proposes a compute-verification architecture for dense frontier pre-training using a pre-committed training specification, inter-node network observations, on-the-fly Merkle commitments, and a zero-knowledge virtual machine with BF16/FP32 precompiles. The paper's governance point is that compute-threshold rules and treaty-style agreements are only enforceable if training claims can be technically checked, and it sketches genesis proofs, in-training step proofs, and ex-ante attestations as a possible audit record. [Ghosh et al.'s 'AI Hardware Choices are Highly Variable and Sparsely Disclosed'](https://x.com/evijit/status/2062589800394248373) supplied the disclosure-side counterpart: in an analysis of the 4,000 most-downloaded Hugging Face models plus broader inference evidence, the authors found sparse public chip reporting, NVIDIA dominance among disclosed training runs, growing use of domestic accelerators such as Huawei Ascend by Chinese developers, and policy-relevant opacity around inference configurations.

## Capabilities

**Microsoft pushed a new MAI model suite into the frontier benchmark race.** [Microsoft's announcement, highlighted by Amin Karbasi](https://x.com/aminkarbasi/status/2063506568424161664), says MAI-Thinking-1 is a 35B-active-parameter MoE with a 256K context window, 97% on AIME 2025, 53% on SWE Bench Pro, and better performance-per-dollar on MAIA 200 silicon than GB200 for its own workloads. The release also matters methodologically: Karbasi notes Microsoft used TAP, an automated jailbreaking method, as part of red-teaming, which makes adversarial evaluation part of the capability story rather than an afterthought.

**Verified math generation kept looking like a serious route to stronger reasoning systems.** [Latent Space's interview with Axiom CEO Carina Hong](https://www.latent.space/p/axiom) frames Lean-style proof generation as a capability flywheel: hard proofs are difficult to produce, cheap to check, and reusable as high-quality training data. Axiom claims its system solved all 12 Putnam problems, scored 8/12 under time limits, and reached 187/189 on ProofGen on Verina, while the harder unsolved bottleneck remains specification: systems can prove what is formalized, but humans still struggle to specify what they want.

**Scaling debates focused less on size alone and more on what data and training dynamics actually buy.** [Christopher Potts shared Huang et al.'s "Why Larger Models Learn More: Effects of Capacity, Interference, and Rare-Task Retention"](https://x.com/ChrisGPotts/status/2061462437807923329), which argues that bigger models benefit from data-induced competition for neuron resources, reduced interference, and better retention of rare tasks. [Dwarkesh Patel's "The sample efficiency black hole"](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole) pushed the other side of the scaling question, arguing that frontier progress still looks heavily dependent on expanding data distributions through expert trajectories, synthetic data, and verifier-driven RL rather than human-like sample efficiency.

*Also this week:* [Sebastian Raschka's 2026 LLM paper curation](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/llm-research-papers-2026-part1) highlighted hybrid architectures, long-context efficiency, tool use, diffusion language models, and serving systems as the year's main technical threads; [Prakash pointed to Anthropic's Opus 4.7 NMR results](https://x.com/8teAPi/status/2063153302427291943), a continued-discussion item rather than a new story after earlier coverage, where Claude reportedly matched or beat dedicated NMR software on some molecular-structure tasks; [an NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35283#fromrss) described using neural networks and an agent to accelerate dynamic equilibrium estimation, reaching in under 20 minutes a loss traditional methods failed to match after four days; and [Samuel Ratnam's LessWrong essay](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/bzkQgYhaCcpvKqmAK/optimisation-over-non-stationary-distributions-creates) modeled non-stationary post-training as a source of generalist circuits, conditional policies, or strategy churn depending on whether objectives share structure and whether regimes are distinguishable.
