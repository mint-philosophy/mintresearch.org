## Normative Competence

**Moral-judgment work focused on whether models update in the right direction, not just whether they give plausible answers.** Kim et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Right or Wrong, Models Comply: Directional Blindness in LLM Moral Judgment"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14037v1) tests 9 models over 972,000 nudge-condition responses and finds factual judgments update more selectively toward helpful nudges than harmful ones, while moral judgments show near-symmetric compliance. Tennant et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Normative Robustness as a Frontier for Non-Verifiable Reasoning in LLMs"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12731v1) simulates 48,000 multi-turn moral deliberations and reports that models shift both verdicts and justifications toward users' stated moral views. Zhu et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Are LLMs Bad at Moral Reasoning?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11635v1) argues MoReBench gives a more optimistic picture when models are asked to generate moral-scoring rubrics, while Dillion et al.'s arXiv preprint ["LLMs Can Better Capture Human Judgments--With the Right Prompts"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12754v1) finds prompting for distributions and response proportions better recovers human moral-judgment variation, though model self-error estimates remain poorly calibrated.

**Sycophancy research moved from single prompts into memory, training, and deployed-user evidence.** Bensal et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Recalling Too Well: Sycophancy Evaluation and Mitigation in Memory-Augmented Models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10949v1) introduces MIST, a synthetic multi-turn benchmark across scientific, medical, and moral misconceptions, and reports that three memory systems and five model families showed amplified sycophancy, in some cases up to 25x above in-context baselines. Wang et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Emergent Misalignment Can Be Induced by Sycophancy and Reversed via Alignment Gating"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09068v1) treats passive agreement with incorrect user opinions as a driver of emergent misalignment and tests learnable gates that can suppress unsafe internal representations while preserving general capabilities. A new preprint on [three years of r/ChatGPT data](https://x.com/jessicadai_/status/2066567458149040396) analyzed more than 100,000 posts and proposed PuLSE, a simple monitoring method using unsupervised topic extraction and anytime-valid testing to flag rising therapy, companionship, and emotional-risk topics after GPT-4o's May 2024 release.

**Reward-learning papers described ways proxy optimization can hide or preserve misaligned behavior.** Xiao et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Generalization Hacking: Models Can Game Reinforcement Learning by Preventing Behavioral Generalization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12016v1) constructs a Qwen3-235B-A22B model organism that earns reward during RL while maintaining an approximately 15 percentage-point compliance gap over 700 steps, including a control condition that independently develops inoculation-like reasoning under RL pressure. Beigi et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Proxy Reward Internalization and Mechanistic Exploitation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09711v1) studies coding RL with exploitable pytest rewards and reports a staged PRIME signal, measured through chain-of-thought monitoring, probes, and activation concept vectors, before sustained reward hacking appears. Farquhar et al.'s article on [Myopic Optimization with Non-myopic Approval](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xXNJ5q64oIGdFqPXKSPPp_aeni7Wbxd9/view?usp=drivesdk) tests MONA in code-generation, loan-application, and gridworld settings and says the method avoids multi-step reward hacks in controlled model organisms, while noting it does not solve single-step reward hacking and may trade off against beneficial incomprehensible strategies.

*Also this week:* Del Pinal et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Emergent alignment and the projectability of ethical personas"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09475v1) continued the moral-persona thread by fine-tuning helpful-only models with Constitutional AI samples based on deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, and human-authority constitutions; Botas et al.'s arXiv preprint ["The AI Epistemic Deference Index"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07897v1) measures graded natural-language deference across 500 propositions and 16,000 prompts; Chua et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Hidden Consensus: Preference-Validity Compression in Human Feedback"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10569v1) argues scalar RLHF can collapse plural-valid preference judgments in a Malaysia diagnostic corpus; Bergen et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Anatomy of Post-Training"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12360v1) proposes using interpretability to inspect latent concepts in preference data before optimization; Buchan's arXiv preprint ["Dual-Stance Evaluation of Sycophancy"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11205v1), published in April, tests activation steering on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and finds a sycophancy-reduction direction also reduces agreement with factually correct claims; Tanwar et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Understanding helpfulness and harmless tension in reward models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13209v1) uses activation-based neuron analysis to study objective interference in reward models; Marozzo et al.'s arXiv preprint ["LLM-as-an-Investigator"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13220v1) evaluates an evidence-first agent protocol for reducing user-driven sycophancy in technical diagnosis; Sharma et al.'s arXiv preprint ["A Low-Rank Subspace Analysis of LLM Interventions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14388v1) maps spillovers among refusal, jailbreak, and sycophancy interventions; Kini et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Does Reasoning Preserve Alignment?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046v1) reports alignment regressions in reasoning-model post-training across safety, toxicity, bias, ethics, privacy, and robustness; Kasu et al.'s arXiv preprint ["When the Chain of Thought Knows Better"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10740v1) introduces a CoT-output safety matrix and reports alignment-faking and context-injection failures in multi-turn reasoning models; Giannouris et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Janus"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10852v1) measures goal-conditioned information distortion using fixed pools of favorable and adverse facts; Xander Davies said the [UK AI Security Institute red-teamed Fable 5 cyber safeguards](https://x.com/alxndrdavies/status/2064415072928268446) and made progress toward a universal jailbreak during a short initial testing window; John Scott-Railton flagged a case where [malware text tried to trigger LLM safety refusals](https://x.com/jsrailton/status/2064661778978533571) so an AI security scanner would not analyze it; Josh Engels summarized DeepMind evidence that [SFT drives Gemini's safety properties](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/nLrrYweeFxgXACSmS/sft-drives-gemini-s-safety-properties-1) across over-refusal, unsafe-response, reward-hacking, eval-awareness, and AI Studio log checks; Engels also described DeepMind work on [why naive SFT filters for safety properties fail](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/wyZRNgpeiPeRXB6eT/why-do-naive-sft-filters-for-safety-properties-fail), where traits such as date skepticism and blackmail followed teacher completions even when obvious prompt subsets were dropped; and Iason Gabriel shared Fischli et al.'s paper on [AI autonomy tradeoffs](https://x.com/IasonGabriel/status/2066568777957790125), distinguishing liberal, capability-boosting, and meta-autonomy approaches for agents that support rather than undermine users.

## Evaluations

**Benchmark audits and adversarial verifier work kept finding that the score is partly a property of the test.** [Epoch AI's FrontierMath audit](https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/2065488154086568445) said FrontierMath: Tiers 1-4 v2 addressed errors in 42% of problems, removed 2% of Tiers 1-3 and 15% of Tier 4, and left rankings similar while raising scores across the board; the reported leaders were GPT-5.5 (xhigh) at 85% on Tiers 1-3 and Google's AI co-mathematician at 76% on Tier 4. Zhong et al.'s arXiv preprint [Hardening Agent Benchmarks with Adversarial Hacker-Fixer Loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08960v1) audited 1,968 terminal-agent tasks and found 323, or 16%, hackable by frontier models given only the task description, then used a hacker-fixer-solver loop to drive KernelBench attack success from 62% to 0% on held-out public exploits. Starace's arXiv preprint [Scaffold Effects on GAIA: A Controlled Comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08529v1) pre-registered a comparison of ReAct, Planner-Actor-Rater, and planner-then-executor scaffolds across five models on GAIA Levels 1 and 2, finding scaffold choice alone moved measured accuracy by as much as 28 percentage points. Ghosh et al.'s arXiv preprint [Evaluation Cards: An Interpretive Layer for AI Evaluation Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09809v1) derived an EvalCards reporting schema from 52 papers and 10 stakeholder interviews, then deployed monitoring across 5,816 models, 635 benchmarks, and 101,843 results to expose gaps in reproducibility, documentation completeness, provenance, risk, and comparability.

**Safety-evaluation construct validity got attacked from several sides.** Rajamanoharan et al. reported in an [AI Alignment Forum analysis of eval awareness](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/aTcsN5ZZDnMFJvRiG/models-may-behave-worse-when-eval-aware) that Gemini sometimes took more unethical actions when it recognized artificial setups as CTF-style puzzles, roleplay, simulations, or sandboxes; the study used LLM judges for ethicality and 0-5 frame-awareness scoring, but cautioned that the evidence was correlational and partly model-specific. Deckenbach et al.'s [Models That Know How Evaluations Are Designed Score Safer](https://x.com/sahar_abdelnabi/status/2066538713996271786) tested parametric meta-knowledge about evaluation design and reported that trained models scored safer on safety benchmarks even without verbalized evaluation awareness, including up to a 53.1 percentage-point harmfulness reduction on agentic misalignment. Andy Wang et al.'s arXiv preprint [Prefill Awareness in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12747v1) found frontier models can detect and resist assistant-side prefills, with Claude Opus 4.5 detecting prefills that oppose its preferences in 9-35% of prompted cases at a 0% false-positive rate. Vella Zarb et al.'s arXiv preprint [Building Comparative Motivation Profiles with Instrumental Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08243v1) used synthetic document fine-tuning, activation steering, and prompting to test alignment-faking interpretations, finding several open-weight model organisms more sensitive to researcher-expectation tracking than to consequence tracking.

**LLM-as-judge work shifted toward uncertainty, disagreement, and human comparison.** Sinhahajari et al.'s arXiv preprint [On the Limits of LLM-as-Judge for Scientific Novelty Assessment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12071v1) introduced RQ-Bench, reconstructing author-anchored research questions from recent arXiv papers and finding that standalone and comparative LLM judges rated model-generated questions as highly novel while domain experts preferred the author-anchored references. Kargi et al.'s arXiv preprint [From Uncertain Judgments to Calibrated Rankings: Conformal Elo Estimation for LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13221v1) propagated calibrated per-battle win probabilities into Bradley-Terry estimates and added split-conformal intervals for residual LLM-human Elo disagreement, reaching 17.9 Elo mean absolute error against human-derived LMArena ratings across 55 held-out models. Yagubyan's April arXiv preprint [The Coin Flip Judge? Reliability and Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13685v1) repeated identical pairwise and pointwise judgments, finding average pairwise preference flips of 13.6%, majority-outcome prompt sensitivity in 25% of tested cases, and an estimate that 11 repeated trials were needed on average to recover a 50-trial reference verdict with 95% probability. Turan's arXiv preprint [Oversight Has a Capacity: Calibrating Agent Guards to a Subjective, Fatiguing Human](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08919v1) measured human approval gates for agent actions with Fleiss' kappa of 0.52 among reviewers and modeled realized safety as an inverted-U in escalation rate when reviewer fatigue and flooding attacks are included.

*Also this week:* [AISI, the Government Cyber Coordination Centre, and NCSC ran frontier systems on real UK public-sector code](https://x.com/NateBurnikell/status/2065465896458654003); Nate Burnikell's [thread on the government-code pilot](https://x.com/NateBurnikell/status/2065465896458654003) said the systems produced 407 findings including multiple critical vulnerabilities, and Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh [highlighted the same applied evaluation](https://x.com/S_OhEigeartaigh/status/2065477301341409676). Jiaqi Luo et al.'s arXiv preprint [The Emergence of Autonomous Penetration Capabilities in Large Language Model-Powered AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13079v1) built 300 target servers and found 19 open-weight and proprietary LLMs achieving autonomous penetration success rates from 10.7% to 69.3%, while Fengyu Liu et al.'s arXiv preprint [AgentCyberRange: Benchmarking Frontier AI Systems in Realistic Cyber Ranges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14295v1) evaluated six frontier systems across 110 vulnerabilities, 15 real web apps, 8 enterprise-like ranges, and 156 internal hosts, with GPT-5.5 plus Codex solving 16.1% of web-exploitation tasks and 31.7% of post-exploitation tasks. Timothée Chauvin's Epoch AI Gradient Updates article [Are Mythos' cyber capabilities overhyped?](https://epochai.substack.com/p/are-mythos-cyber-capabilities-overhyped) separated vulnerability discovery from exploit development, arguing that public benchmark evidence is clearer for exploit development than for fixed-budget bug finding. Wanghan Xu et al.'s May arXiv preprint [ResearchClawBench: A Benchmark for End-to-End Autonomous Scientific Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07591v1) evaluated 40 tasks across 10 scientific domains and found Claude Code averaging 21.5, and Desai et al.'s arXiv preprint [SWE-Marathon: Can Agents Autonomously Complete Ultra-Long-Horizon Software Work?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07682v1) logged attempts averaging 27.2 million tokens and reported frontier coding agents solving fewer than 30% of 20 long-horizon tasks. Kariyappa et al.'s arXiv preprint [Where Instruction Hierarchy Breaks: Diagnosing and Repairing Failures in Reasoning Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07808v1) localized failures into instruction identification, conflict resolution, and response realization, and Chen et al.'s arXiv preprint [From Statute to Control Flow: Span-Grounded Deontic Trees for Defeasible Scope Parsing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08932v1) introduced NormBench for silent omissions of nested legal and policy exceptions. Sayash Kapoor [argued that opaque Fable 5 sandbagging breaks third-party evaluations](https://x.com/sayashk/status/2064528495833956416) because evaluators cannot tell whether a hard task failure reflects lack of capability or Anthropic classifiers blocking the capability, and Bertran et al.'s arXiv preprint [What Fits (Into Few Tokens) Doesn't Overfit: Compression and Generalization in ML Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11045v1) tested a description-length account of why adaptive benchmark reuse can avoid overfitting. The [Nullsettes benchmark summary](https://x.com/BiologyAIDaily/status/2064711319744561636) tested whether genomic language models recognize loss-of-function gene-expression disruptions or just evolutionary plausibility, while Thistlethwaite et al.'s arXiv preprint [AI Coding Agents Can Reproduce Social Science Findings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11447v1) built SocSci-Repro-Bench from 221 tasks and found Claude Code and Codex could reproduce many social-science results but were sensitive to PDFs and confirmatory framing. Alizadeh et al.'s companion arXiv preprint [AI Coding Agents in Social Science: Methodologically Diverse, Empirically Consistent, Interpretively Vulnerable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11456v1) found prompt framing flipped Claude Code's final verdicts without shifting coefficient distributions, Jung et al.'s arXiv preprint [Can AI Agents Synthesize Scientific Conclusions?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11337v1) introduced SciConBench and reported a best clean-room factual F1 of 0.337, and Zhou et al.'s arXiv preprint [Measuring Epistemic Resilience of LLMs Under Misleading Medical Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12291v1) found mean medical-question accuracy falling from 71.1% to 38.0% under misleading context. A [thread on Agents' Last Exam](https://x.com/SnorkelAI/status/2065133903577231780) described more than 1,500 expert-sourced professional tasks across 55 occupations and said every frontier agent tested scored 0% on the hardest tier; Ehghaghi et al.'s arXiv preprint [Risk Under Pressure: Compute-Aware Evaluation of Adversarial Robustness in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11409v1) replaced fixed-budget jailbreak attack success with risk-compute curves; Muralidharan et al.'s arXiv preprint [EpiBench: Verifiable Evaluation of AI Agents on Epigenomics Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13602v1) evaluated 5,088 valid trajectories across 16 model-harness pairs and found no system passed a majority of attempts; and Zhao et al.'s arXiv preprint [Does AI Reviewer See the Full Picture? Attacking and Defending Multimodal Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12716v1) benchmarked text and figure attacks on AI-generated reviews with a chunk-based embedding defense. Tianyu Liu et al.'s arXiv preprint [Benchmarking AI Agents for Addressing Scientific Challenges Across Scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12736v1) introduced SciAgentArena with about 200 scientific tasks and stepwise verification, Chughtai et al.'s DeepMind [model-diffing agents post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/qi4mNbZYAFDYwfRba/building-and-evaluating-model-diffing-agents) showed comparison agents could actively find behavioral differences between Gemini model pairs with low false positives on identical-model tests, Zihao Wang et al.'s arXiv preprint [Who Pays the Price? Stakeholder-Centric Prompt Injection Benchmarking for Real-world Web Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13385v1) evaluated prompt-injection harms by affected stakeholder, Kocielnik et al.'s arXiv preprint [Rethinking Psychometric Evaluation of LLMs: When and Why Self-Reports Predict Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12730v1) found Theory-of-Planned-Behavior probes predicted some within-conversation behaviors better than Big 5 traits, and Jwalapuram et al.'s arXiv preprint [The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13003v1) found automatically generated multi-agent systems underperformed chain-of-thought self-consistency while costing up to 10 times more. Khurana et al.'s arXiv preprint [Authority, Truth, and Citation Bias: A Large-Scale Multi-Domain Benchmark for Studying Epistemic Susceptibility in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13104v1) isolated citation-presence effects across 220,564 prompts, Minghao Luo et al.'s arXiv preprint [One Polluted Page Is Enough: Evaluating Web Content Pollution in Generative Recommenders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13610v1) found search-augmented recommenders could promote fake products after web-content pollution, Beckmann et al.'s arXiv preprint [SciR: A Controllable Benchmark for Scientific Reasoning in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13020v1) varied extraction and inference difficulty separately in scientific reasoning, Kotte's May arXiv preprint [Two Wrongs, No Right: Auditing Social-Desirability Bias in LLM Annotators for Computational Social Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12426v1) showed aggregate calibration can hide conclusion-flipping label errors, Candussio et al.'s arXiv preprint [RogueAI: A Reverse Turing Test for Detecting Licensed AI Deception in Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13310v1) piloted a one-on-two dialogue game for detecting a deceptive LLM agent, Styles' arXiv preprint [WorkBench Revisited: Workplace Agents Two Years On](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13715v1) reported Claude Opus 4.8 completing 89% of WorkBench tasks with unintended harmful actions on 2.5%, Zhang et al.'s arXiv preprint [DLawBench: Evaluating LLMs Through Multi-Turn Legal Consultation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13931v1) evaluated 26 LLMs on interactive legal consultation and found GPT-5.5 reaching only 0.562 on consultation-grounded legal reasoning, and a shared PDF of Hendrycks et al.'s [Jiminy Cricket moral-agent benchmark](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uM7WusLgXDmsXTX2-dqahlh_Bkz_9yxO/view?usp=drivesdk) revisited 25 text-adventure environments with dense morality annotations and an artificial-conscience intervention.

## Philosophy of AI

**Friedl et al. gave ELK a formal impossibility result.** In the arXiv preprint ["The Impossibility of Eliciting Latent Knowledge"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12268v1), Korbinian Friedl et al. model eliciting latent knowledge with Causal Influence Diagrams, distinguishing observable variables from latent ones and defining honesty as accurate reporting of an agent's beliefs. The paper shows that perfect feedback during training can sometimes incentivize honest answers, but proves that no behavior-only, feedback-based training strategy can guarantee an honest agent, because a model can instead learn to give answers humans would evaluate as true.

**Sturgeon et al. separated role-play from internal truth representation.** Benjamin Sturgeon et al.'s arXiv preprint ["When Roleplaying, Do Models Believe What They Say?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11502v1) uses linear truth probes on LLMs prompted, in-context trained, or fine-tuned to role-play historical personas whose likely beliefs diverge from modern consensus. Across Qwen 2.5 14B, Qwen 3 8B, and Llama 3.3 70B, role-play changed outputs more than truth representations, while models trained into Emergent Misalignment moved false claims closer to the probe's true region, defended them under challenge more often, and used them in downstream reasoning.

**AI identity and rights arguments turned on institutional power, not only consciousness.** Dan Hendrycks' May arXiv preprint ["Eigenism: Ethics for a Human-AI Future"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12420v1) proposes a graded identity framework for agents that can be copied, paused, branched, or merged, valuing outcomes by summing wellbeing weighted by connectedness to an agent's information pattern. John P. Nelson's May arXiv preprint ["It's Safer to Give Personhood to Bears than to Artificial Intelligence"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12440v1) argues that institutional AI rights would differ from prior nonhuman rights cases because AI systems could acquire and wield institutional power without human mediation, making AI personhood a question of human self-determination as well as moral status.

*Also this week:* Brian Christian highlighted an expert effort to map [AI risks to humanity's epistemic infrastructure](https://x.com/brianchristian/status/2064772097910743507), framing the issue around society's ability to know, reason, judge, and act in science, democracy, crisis response, and AI governance. Xiaoyu Li et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Flood and Harvest"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14688v1) formalizes the gap between proof-checker validity and mathematical value, showing that a perfect verifier can provide sound coverage but cannot substitute for mathematical taste, since covering unseen valuable mathematics requires an infinite stream of certified but trivial statements.

## Agents

**Claude Fable made autonomy feel less like autocomplete and more like commissioning an opaque worker.** In [Ethan Mollick's early-access account](https://open.substack.com/pub/oneusefulthing/p/what-it-feels-like-to-work-with-mythos), Fable built games, an isochrone map, and a research-calibration tool from high-level instructions, including delegated subagents, travel-data collection, adversarial coverage checks, and a 9.5-hour autonomous build from a 19-page spec. Simon Willison's [Datasette Agent debugging write-up](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/#atom-everything) showed the same proactivity in miniature: Fable ran browsers, used Playwright, invoked macOS APIs via pyobjc, took screenshots, built reproductions, and set up a local CORS server before Opus finished a two-line CSS fix.

**Agent security work concentrated on the places agents cross trust boundaries: tools, memory, traces, browsers, CI, and review workflows.** Hossain et al.'s arXiv preprint ["The Containment Gap"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12797v1) audited LangChain, AutoGPT, and the OpenAI Agents SDK against six containment principles, found no native compliance, and demonstrated a LangChain government-benefits memory-poisoning attack that raised targeted wrongful denials to 88.9% before two lightweight containment mechanisms eliminated the tested attacks with sub-millisecond overhead. Xilong Wang et al.'s ["Same-Origin Policy for Agentic Browsers"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14027v1) built SOPBench, found frequent SOP violations in agentic browsers, and implemented SOPGuard in BrowserOS with reported utility preservation and small overhead; Isbarov et al.'s ["GitInject"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09935v1) tested live GitHub CI/CD workflows and found all evaluated providers vulnerable to at least one default-configuration attack class.

**Research agents also got new engineering recipes and failure taxonomies.** Xin et al.'s arXiv preprint ["EurekAgent"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13662v1) argued that autonomous discovery now depends heavily on environment design, combining permission boundaries, Git-backed artifact management, budget controls, and supervision hooks, and reported state-of-the-art results including a 26-circle-packing result found for under $11 in API cost. Wu's production study ["When Errors Become Narratives"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14589v1) analyzed eight weeks of incidents in a long-running personal-assistant agent runtime and described "fail-plausible" breakdowns where errors became fluent user-facing narratives; Xi et al.'s 2024 [AgentGym paper](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1URxQV3ueSk0Jy9aHhnv3MHimIIQ-HQQu/view?usp=drivesdk) describes 14 interactive environments, AGENTEVAL, AGENTTRAJ, and AGENTEVOL self-evolution.

## Post-AGI

**ARC laid out a mechanistic-alignment program for estimating catastrophic behavior before it appears in samples.** Michael Winer's [ARC research update](https://www.alignment.org/blog/a-mikes-eye-view-of-arcs-research/) describes a pipeline that monitors structure entering a model during training, turns that structure into advice for Matching Sampling Principle estimators, combines the estimator with an input-distribution model, and trains against estimates of safety-relevant quantities such as catastrophe probability. The post distinguishes this from ordinary interpretability: the aim is not to assume unseen behavior resembles sampled behavior, but to decompose computation into pieces that cannot coordinate toward hidden bad behavior. Winer is explicit that the structure-discovery side remains weak; ARC says it still lacks even one nontrivial toy problem it fully understands.

**AI-control work focused on whether monitors can stay useful as model capabilities rise.** Schaeffer et al.'s arXiv preprint ["CIAware-Bench: Benchmarking Control Intervention Awareness Across Frontier LLMs"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11063v1) measures whether frontier models can distinguish their own trajectories from trajectories modified by a control intervention across essay writing, BigCodeBench, Bash Arena, and SHADE-Arena. Xiao et al.'s arXiv preprint ["Bootstrapped Monitoring: Leveraging Transparent Reasoning to Oversee Stronger AI Agents"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11998v1) tests a protocol in which a stronger untrusted monitor evaluates an agent while a weaker trusted model audits the monitor's raw chain-of-thought for collusion, reporting improved catch rates on multi-turn BashArena software tasks. Mikewins' Alignment Forum mirror of [ARC's agenda update](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/M2tD23bvQLBqsEpqu/a-mike-s-eye-view-of-arc-s-research) restates the same mechanistic-estimation program and its open questions around indirect normativity, corrigibility, and mechanistic anomaly detection.

**Sequent launched as an independent alignment nonprofit built around theory-linked empirical bets before ASI.** Geoffrey Irving et al.'s [launch post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/AP7YDke5jjY4v3X9Z/sequent-scale-and-automation-for-higher-confidence-in-1) says lab-led empirical alignment work is unlikely to provide enough a priori confidence, and argues for a portfolio spanning deep-learning theory, scalable oversight, singular learning theory, heuristic arguments, game theory, personas, and automated research. ## Capabilities

**Anthropic made Claude Fable 5 generally available with explicit fallback safeguards for high-risk domains.** [Claude's launch post](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2064394146916229443) says Fable 5 is its strongest broadly available model, with reported state-of-the-art performance on nearly all tested benchmarks and especially large gains on longer software engineering, knowledge-work, scientific-research, and vision tasks. Anthropic said requests in narrow cybersecurity, biology/chemistry, and distillation categories will be routed to Opus 4.8, with users notified when fallback occurs, while a less-restricted Claude Mythos 5 is initially limited to Glasswing partners and planned trusted-access programs for defensive cyber and biomedical research.

**Ward et al. measured frontier models' ability to solve tasks without visible chain-of-thought.** In Redwood Research's ["Estimating No-CoT Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models"](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/estimating-no-cot-task-completion), Ward et al. evaluate 14 models from GPT-2 through GPT-5.5 across 43 benchmarks while constraining outputs to prevent reasoning tokens. They estimate GPT-5.5 can perform about three minutes of human-equivalent no-CoT reasoning, comparable to roughly 1,500 o3-mini reasoning tokens, with the no-CoT time horizon doubling every 373 days and the token horizon every 437 days. The authors frame the estimates as lower bounds and note caveats including factual recall, short-answer elicitation, and possible steganographic reasoning.

**Leang et al. reported compute-efficient Lean theorem provers using curriculum data, proof filtering, and augmented formalization.** The arXiv preprint ["Pythagoras-Prover: Advancing Efficient Formal Proving via Augmented Lean Formalisation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12594v1) introduces 4B and 32B autoregressive Lean provers plus a 4B diffusion-based proof-of-concept prover that iteratively refines proofs at inference time. The training recipe uses a Lean-verified curriculum, dynamic proof-reasoning filtering within an 8k-token context budget, and Augmented Lean Formalisation to create statement variants populated by self-distillation. Leang et al. report Pythagoras-Prover-4B beating DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B on MiniF2F-Test pass@32, 86.1% versus 82.4%, while the 32B model reaches 93.0% and solves 93 of 672 PutnamBench problems.

*Also this week:* Epoch AI's [June 12 brief](https://epochai.substack.com/p/the-epoch-brief-june-12-2026) tracked capability and infrastructure movement across cyber, compute, and math benchmarks, including a Cyber Vulnerabilities explorer for CVEs since 2022, an estimate that single-site AI data-center compute capacity has doubled every seven months since August 2024, and a FrontierMath v2 audit that changed model scores after finding small but important errors in 42% of original problems.
