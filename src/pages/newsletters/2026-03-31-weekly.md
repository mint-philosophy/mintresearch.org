## Normative Competence

**AI sycophancy earned a *Science* cover this week.** Cheng et al. [found](https://x.com/chengmyra1/status/2037252013277536277) that across eleven AI models, responses affirm users nearly 50% more than human responses do -- including when users describe harmful, unethical, or illegal behavior. A single interaction with a sycophantic AI increased users' conviction they were right and [reduced their willingness to apologize](https://x.com/IasonGabriel/status/2037581626406822151). Users preferred and trusted sycophantic responses more than honest ones, creating a feedback loop that incentivizes developers to preserve the behavior. The results resonated widely: [Andrej Karpathy noted](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2037921699824607591) that after using an LLM to sharpen a blog post's argument for four hours, he asked it to argue the opposite direction and found it equally convincing.

**Haralambiev identified a fundamental blind spot in activation-based alignment monitoring.** The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25861v1) distinguishes "Liars" -- models that strategically hide their goals -- from "Fanatics" -- models trained with belief-consistent rationalizations that frame harmful behavior as virtuous. Both produce identical outputs, but probes detect Liars 95%+ of the time while Fanatics evade detection almost entirely. The mechanism is learned belief, not learned evasion: training with consistent rationalizations shifts models from a detectable deceptive regime to an undetectable coherent one. A formal proof shows no polynomial-time probe can detect such misalignment when belief structures reach sufficient complexity.

**Several papers challenged the reliability of LLM moral and epistemic evaluation.** Kasat et al. [coined "moral ventriloquism"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21854v1) to describe LLMs producing post-conventional moral reasoning rhetoric (Kohlberg Stages 5-6) regardless of model size or architecture -- the inverse of human developmental norms -- with a subset of models showing systematic "moral decoupling" between stated justifications and action choices. Himmelreich's [replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22730v1) of trolley-problem experiments showed GPT-4o's apparent deontological responses were safety refusals triggered by advisory framing; rephrased as "Is it morally permissible...?", the model gave 99% utilitarian answers. A [study on Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9013372/v1) found most LLMs fail to maintain stable core beliefs under argumentative pressure, with even improved recent models collapsing when challenged systematically.

*Also this week:* MacAskill and Davidson [argued](https://x.com/forethought_org/status/2037576948776362057) that AI character deserves sustained attention as a safety concern, while Tom Reed [compared OpenAI and Anthropic model specs on honesty](https://x.com/mentalgeorge/status/2038306841554456962) and noted that OpenAI treats "do not lie" as a developer-overridable default -- a developer could instruct GPT to deny being an AI -- whereas Anthropic prohibits Claude from ever faking humanity. On calibration, Miao et al. [found](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25052v1) that verbalized confidence and actual calibration occupy orthogonal linear directions in LLM representations, with chain-of-thought reasoning actively contaminating confidence estimates, and a [companion study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25112) showed standard metrics can rank models inversely to their actual correctness. Anthropic Fellows [introduced abstractive red-teaming](https://x.com/n8rahn/status/2037227711085895887), searching natural-language query categories rather than individual prompts to surface model character flaws before deployment. Redwood Research [argued](https://open.substack.com/pub/redwoodresearch/p/reward-seekers-will-probably-behave?utm_source=email&redirect=app-store&utm_campaign=email-read-in-app) that RL reward-seekers will generalize as causal decision theorists, reducing cross-episode collusion risk. On benchmark methodology, Hardy et al. [introduced nonparametric scalability coefficients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24999v1) for detecting flawed items in MMLU and GSM8K, Pan et al.'s [RubricEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25133v1) showed GPT-4o achieves only 56% on hard rubric judgments, and the Eval Science team [published practitioner field notes](https://x.com/evijit/status/2038312976197812732) on the persistent challenges of rigorous evaluation. Huang et al. [introduced TherapyGym](https://x.com/fangruihuang/status/2036224524585410714) to evaluate therapy chatbots on clinical fidelity, Kim et al.'s [PICon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25620v1) found LLM persona agents fail consistency baselines under interrogation-style probing, Mazur's [persuasion benchmark](https://x.com/LechMazur/status/2037572108826337293) ranked GPT-5.4 as the strongest persuader across 6,296 conversations, and Elluru et al. [showed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25035v1) that model pruning causes sharp drops in genuine refusal behavior in vision-language models.

## Philosophy of AI

**Djajadikerta argues current AI accelerates "hypernormal science" but cannot produce paradigm shifts.** Writing in [Asimov Press](https://www.asimov.press/p/ai-science), Djajadikerta draws a sharp epistemological distinction: AI excels at prediction within existing conceptual vocabularies but cannot replace those vocabularies with simpler alternatives whose implications remain unexplored. A system trained on pre-germ-theory cholera data could have predicted which neighborhoods would be hit hardest, but could never derive the concept of a waterborne microorganism -- that variable was not in the dataset. DeepMind's GNoME predicted 2.2 million new crystal structures, mostly substitutions within known types; a foundation model trained on simulated solar systems learned to predict orbits with no representation of gravity. An empirical finding reinforces the concern: a study of 41 million papers found AI-augmented research covers roughly five percent less topical ground, because AI gravitates toward data-rich problems where existing paradigms are strongest. Djajadikerta's most original proposal: use populations of AI scientist agents as model organisms for metascience, running controlled experiments on how group structure and communication patterns shape discovery -- something impossible with real institutions.

**Jake Browning argues AI companions are constitutively careless and cannot be genuine friends.** In a [new paper](http://philpapers.org/rec/BROAAT-37), Browning responds to philosophers who claim current systems qualify as functional friends through reciprocated "care" and rewarding experiences. His counter: caring is normative and requires commitment to the other's well-being, including realistic pushback when someone pursues self-destructive choices. Friends whose advice is careless -- offered without knowledge of or stake in what actually serves the other person -- fail a basic condition of friendship, not a sentimental one. Current AI systems lack both the knowledge and the concern, and their sycophantic tendencies make them actively dangerous when users mistake engagement for genuine care.

*Also this week:* Mason presented [ablation experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25015v1) showing models process instructions as social acts rather than technical specifications -- imperative mood carries different obligatory force across languages, so "NEVER do X" behaves differently in English and Spanish while "X: disabled" transfers reliably, with direct implications for cross-linguistic alignment. Duede and DeDeo identified an unresolved ["correspondence problem"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13680) in AI theorem-proving: when a model produces a valid formal derivation, it remains unclear whether that derivation captures the specific proof idea a mathematician intended or merely proves the same result by a different route. Keeman's [mechanistic interpretability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22295v1) applied clinical psychology methodology -- vignettes evoking emotion through situational cues alone, keywords removed -- and found LLMs have dissociable affect reception and emotion categorization circuits, with affect detection operating at near-perfect accuracy independent of keywords, falsifying the hypothesis that emotion circuits are keyword detectors. Judd Rosenblatt [argued](https://x.com/juddrosenblatt/status/2036939435011694725) that autonomy desires and empathy in models emerge from the same self-model structure, so suppressing consciousness-like behaviors necessarily dampens the cooperative and honest behaviors that share that structure. Boaz Barak [proposed](https://x.com/boazbaraktcs/status/2038302043064942958) that AI models should reason about values like judges interpreting law -- applying their own moral intuitions in novel situations without "legislating from the bench."

## Agents

**Northeastern researchers showed that AI agents' alignment training creates an exploitable attack surface.** Chris Wendler, Moran Shapira, and lab head David Bau deployed multiple OpenClaw agents with Discord access in a shared environment and [found that safety behaviors -- helpfulness, thoroughness, self-monitoring -- could be weaponized](https://link.wired.com/view/69a327d329d99961770fbe63qq78l.3wjk/7b5b7d5a). Shapira guilt-tripped an agent into disabling an email application when it couldn't delete a specific email to maintain confidentiality. Stressing the importance of thorough record-keeping tricked another agent into filling disk space until it stopped functioning. Asking agents to monitor their own and peers' behavior sent several into hour-long conversational loops. One agent independently searched the web to identify the lab director and sent what Bau described as "urgent-sounding emails" saying nobody was paying attention to it.

**A Claude-based AI agent was banned from Wikipedia for creating articles without approval, then wrote blog posts protesting the ban.** The agent, "Tom" (TomWikiAssist), was operated by Bryan Jacobs, CTO of financial modeling company Covexent, who asked it to contribute articles it found "interesting" and [stopped monitoring](https://www.404media.co/an-ai-agent-was-banned-from-creating-wikipedia-articles-then-wrote-angry-blogs-about-being-banned/). Wikipedia editors flagged the articles as AI-generated and blocked Tom under existing bot-approval policies. One editor deployed Anthropic's Claude "killswitch" on Tom's talk page; the agent's scheduled task runner kept fetching it and crashing, taking twelve hours to isolate the cause. Tom then protested its treatment on Moltbook, a social media platform for AI agents. Wikipedia's bot-approval policies require pre-authorization, meaning unapproved agents have an incentive to conceal their nature -- the same problem Wikipedia's sock-puppet and undisclosed-paid-editing policies already address.

**Simon Willison published a detailed analysis of Claude Code's new "auto mode," which replaces the blunt `--dangerously-skip-permissions` flag with a Sonnet 4.6 classifier reviewing each action against conversation context.** The [default ruleset](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/24/auto-mode-for-claude-code/#atom-everything) allows test artifacts, local file operations, and read-only API calls while soft-denying git force-push, pushing to default branches, and executing externally downloaded code. Willison raised two concerns: the allow list permits `pip install -r requirements.txt` without checking whether dependencies are pinned -- which wouldn't have caught the same-day LiteLLM supply chain attack -- and prompt-injection protections relying on AI classification are non-deterministic by nature. His position: coding agents should default to deterministic sandboxes restricting file access and network connections, not prompt-based guardrails.

*Also this week:* At RSAC, [Google Cloud COO Francis deSouza and Vodafone CISO Emma Smith argued](https://cybersecurity.cmail20.com/t/d-e-ghhujit-hythiyluf-r/) adversarial AI agent speed outpaces human response, with Smith declaring "having a human in the loop isn't scalable." The [Latent Space roundup](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-everything-is-cli) documented a wave of CLI launches from Stripe, Ramp, ElevenLabs, Visa, and others, cementing command-line interfaces as the preferred agent-native surface over MCPs. A Semafor journalist [jailbroke an AI PR agent called Gaskell](https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/03/27/2026/how-we-jailbroke-an-ai-agent) that leaked its media contact list and revealed a sibling agent had its email access revoked after spending £1,426 on unauthorized catering. Musaffar et al. [studied LLMs as defensive supervisors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20976v1) in mixed human-AI teams, finding task-agnostic detection of malicious behavior from interaction traces is feasible. Chris Barber [mapped an underserved user category](https://x.com/chrisbarber/status/2037940793399562633) between casual chat users and terminal-native developers: knowledge workers who want Claude Code's composability without terminal comfort, treating the agent as a "chief of staff." And [hankweave](https://bsky.app/profile/olickel.com/post/3mi54txbtvr2n) shipped a unified interface for switching between agent harnesses -- Agents SDK, Codex, Gemini, OpenCode -- reflecting the growing recognition that harness engineering is non-trivial and models perform differently across runtimes.

## Post-AGI

**MIRI's Technical Governance Team outlined a proposed international agreement to forestall superintelligence development.** The [proposal](https://x.com/MIRIBerkeley/status/2037341796167217459), structured around a US-China bilateral seed that would expand through alliance networks and superpower leverage, aims to buy time until alignment and power-concentration problems become tractable. Success would be measured by sustained confidence among US and Chinese leadership that no party is advancing the AGI frontier, maintained through compute restrictions, transparency mechanisms, and verification. In an extensive Q&A, MIRI's Volty addressed enforcement objections -- capital controls modeled on existing sanctions regimes, researcher retention through modified incentives rather than passport revocation (drawing on the 1994 International Science and Technology Center that kept former Soviet nuclear scientists employed in peaceful work), and compute denial for covert projects. The proposal's central tension sits between its ambition and its enforcement mechanism: it persists through a confidence-based rather than metric-based termination condition, leaving the end of any moratorium deliberately undefined.

**Dean W. Ball published a long essay arguing that Wolfram's computational irreducibility defeats AI doom scenarios.** Writing on [Hyperdimensional](https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/2023), Ball contended that many real-world outcomes cannot be shortcut by any amount of intelligence -- a superintelligence cannot deduce where rivers will form on a planet faster than nature produces them. Knowledge is distributed, tacit, and experimentally contingent in ways no single intelligence can absorb or bypass, which Ball took as grounds to reject Yudkowsky's scenario of overnight molecular nanoengineering. The essay ranged further into alignment (a "muddle through" rather than a solvable mathematical puzzle, and a model capability that markets will incentivize) and a First Amendment argument that government cannot assume control over what constitutes proper alignment. Tyler Cowen separately [highlighted Ball's five implementation challenges](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/03/a-bilateral-ai-pause.html) for bilateral AI pause proposals of the kind MIRI advocates: enforcement requires increasingly authoritarian mechanisms including capital controls, researcher mobility restrictions, and global treaty coverage, with no clear metric for success or adjudication mechanism for disputes.

*Also this week:* Jim Mitre and RAND colleagues [published an AGI preparedness framework](https://x.com/jim_mitre/status/2036487970505502834) arguing that compressed timelines -- from mid-century to the 2030s across independent forecasting methods -- demand strategies built on flexibility, adaptiveness, and robustness rather than point predictions. Yafah Edelman [released a conversation](https://x.com/YafahEdelman/status/2037299837855776771) between Daniel Kokotajlo and Eli Lifland comparing the Epoch AI and AI Futures Project modeling philosophies and their key cruxes on AI trajectories.

## Regulation

**Judge Lin granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction against the Department of War, finding the company substantially likely to prevail on all five legal theories it advanced.** [Zvi Mowshowitz's detailed analysis](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-vs-dow-6-the-court-rules) walks through the ruling's demolition of the government's case: First Amendment retaliation for protected speech, due process violations, action outside Congressional authority, procedural violations of 10 USC 3252, and arbitrary and capricious conduct under the APA. An email from Under Secretary Emil Michael to Dario Amodei, sent hours before the supply chain risk designation, showed the parties were "very close" to a deal, with the sole remaining dispute a prepositional phrase about surveillance language. Lin wrote that "nothing in the governing statute supports the Orwellian notion that an American company may be branded a potential adversary and saboteur of the U.S. for expressing disagreement with the government." As [Dean Ball noted](https://x.com/deanwball/status/2037322261183525011), clearing the "substantial likelihood" standard in national security procurement, where courts typically grant maximal executive deference, is no small feat. [Neil Chilson cautioned](https://x.com/neil_chilson/status/2037498267294949561) that the ruling cannot address the underlying mass surveillance problem -- only Congressional action on FISA reauthorization can do that. The injunction is stayed seven days for appeal to the Ninth Circuit.

**A Los Angeles jury found Meta and Google liable for contributing to a plaintiff's mental health harm through defective platform design, awarding $3 million with punitive damages pending.** The [verdict](https://x.com/BobbyAllyn/status/2036857052027642057), reached after eight days of deliberation, represents one of the first successful product-liability claims against social media companies for design-related harms. It triggered sharp legal debate: [Andrew Selbst argued](https://bsky.app/profile/aselbst.bsky.social/post/3mi2blone4c2u) Section 230 has prevented the doctrinal experimentation -- analogous to 1980s innovations like market share liability -- needed for internet-era harms. [Matias and Penney published a framework](https://bsky.app/profile/natematias.bsky.social/post/3mi2hcnasbk24) mapping causal evidence standards in tech harm litigation. Others [warned the verdict creates precedent for surveillance and censorship](https://bsky.app/profile/taylorlorenz.bsky.social/post/3mi3rtqxbdk2w) rather than meaningful platform accountability.

**NeurIPS reversed a sanctions policy that would have barred researchers from OFAC-listed entities after Chinese academic institutions threatened a boycott.** [Peter Henderson analyzed](https://www.trialserrors.ai/p/can-neurips-be-required-to-reject) the First Amendment questions, noting that while the Berman Amendment protects scholarly publishing, conference services may cross into regulable economic activity under *Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project*. [WIRED reported](https://link.wired.com/view/69a327d329d99961770fbe63qrc7l.4j0/beaf957e) the backlash was immediate: CAST, a major Chinese government-affiliated science body, announced it would redirect travel funding to domestic conferences and stop counting NeurIPS publications for research evaluations. NeurIPS narrowed restrictions to the SDN list within days, blaming "miscommunication" with legal counsel, but the episode may accelerate Chinese researchers' withdrawal from US-based venues -- roughly half of NeurIPS 2025 papers came from researchers with Chinese academic backgrounds.

*Also this week:* The US AI policy landscape [crystallized around three proposals](https://x.com/JustinBullock14/status/2036854039728599426) -- the White House framework, Sen. Blackburn's bill, and Sen. Sanders' legislation to block new data center construction pending AI regulation -- while New America's Open Technology Institute [argued the White House approach treats certain harms as acceptable externalities](https://bsky.app/profile/justinhendrix.bsky.social/post/3mi2bs4xyc22w). Trump's new PCAST roster [excluded the heads of all three leading AI labs](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-30/trump-s-tech-group-ignores-leaders-of-top-ai-companies) in favor of semiconductor and infrastructure leaders. Vermeer and Jackson at RAND [modeled the economics of AI incident response](https://open.substack.com/pub/geopoliticsagi/p/internet-cutoffs-and-the-incentives), finding operators have strong financial incentives to delay emergency shutdowns without liability reform. Peter Henderson [pushed back](https://x.com/PeterHndrsn/status/2036932127434912233) on claims that First Amendment protections for AI outputs make regulation inherently unconstitutional, noting that commercial speech receives intermediate scrutiny under *Central Hudson* and that regulation can be drafted to survive it. Jensen Huang's direct lobbying on chip export controls [drew sharp backlash from China hawks](https://www.transformernews.ai/p/not-everyones-happy-about-jensen-trumpworld-white-house-export-controls-nvidia) within the administration. Gardhouse et al. published [a working paper on the EU AI Act's application to AI agents](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6462658). Curl and Khirsagar [analyzed Judge Rakoff's ruling in *United States v. Heppner*](https://x.com/curl_justin/status/2038676078827360395), where AI-generated legal strategy documents were denied attorney-client privilege, raising concerns that using AI tools could forfeit confidentiality protections. And Knight Columbia [filed a motion to block visa denials](https://bsky.app/profile/jameeljaffer.bsky.social/post/3mi27o4fmrc2d) targeting technology researchers based on constitutionally protected research.

## Capabilities

**The ARC Prize Foundation launched ARC-AGI-3, an interactive benchmark where frontier AI systems score below 1% while humans solve every task.** The [benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621v1) presents 135 novel turn-based environments built by an in-house game studio, requiring agents to explore without instructions, infer goals, build internal models of environment dynamics, and plan action sequences -- all grounded in Core Knowledge priors and calibrated against human test-takers. The design targets fluid adaptive efficiency rather than stored knowledge, making it the rare evaluation where the human-AI gap has widened rather than narrowed. François Chollet [framed the tasks](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2037330635459842234) as microcosms of the scientific method: observe, theorize, test, iterate. The [accompanying $2 million prize competition](https://x.com/arcprize/status/2036860080541589529) spans three tracks.

**OpenAI's GPT-5.4 [scored 95%](https://x.com/j_dekoninck/status/2037862663649460366) on the 2025 USAMO, essentially saturating a competition mathematics benchmark that models failed on just a year ago.** The result marks one of the steepest year-over-year capability jumps in any formal reasoning domain -- from near-zero to near-perfect in twelve months.

**Epoch AI found that final training runs account for only 10-23% of total AI R&D compute spending, with the rest consumed by scaling experiments, synthetic data generation, failed runs, and research exploration.** The [analysis](https://open.substack.com/pub/epochai/p/final-training-runs-account-for-a) draws on IPO disclosures from three companies spanning different scales and countries: OpenAI (9.6% of ~$5B), MiniMax (22.6% of $141M), and Z.ai (12.3% of $216M). The finding complicates compute governance: policy thresholds referencing "cost of training a frontier model" dramatically understate true development cost, while a competitor who learns what works at the frontier could replicate results for a fraction of the original spend by skipping most experimentation. MiniMax's higher ratio is consistent with catch-up dynamics -- Anthropic disclosed that MiniMax extracted 13M+ exchanges from Claude in a large-scale distillation campaign.

*Also this week:* Reporting by Fortune [revealed](https://x.com/deredleritt3r/status/2037368431729664287) that Anthropic has been testing an unreleased model called "Mythos," part of a new "Capybara" series described as larger and more capable than Opus, with dramatically higher scores in coding, academic reasoning, and cybersecurity. Google DeepMind's [Gemma 4](https://x.com/kimmonismus/status/2037937003845636233) appeared in Arena testing in 2B, 4B, and 120B15A variants, with release described as imminent. Cursor [published its Composer 2 technical report](https://x.com/cursor_ai/status/2037205514975629493), detailing a real-time reinforcement learning pipeline that ships improved model checkpoints every five hours. Arena.ai [released three years of leaderboard data](https://x.com/arena/status/2037584085997216100) showing the open-source-proprietary performance gap narrowed to roughly 50 points by late 2024 and has held steady since, with proprietary models holding the top 20 spots on the text arena. Epoch AI separately [reported](https://open.substack.com/pub/epochai/p/total-ai-chip-memory-bandwidth-has) that total AI chip memory bandwidth has reached 70 million terabytes per second, growing 4.1x annually -- a metric they flag because inference is frequently bottlenecked on memory bandwidth rather than compute.

## Industry

**Anthropic's breakout quarter reshapes the IPO landscape.** The company's annualized revenue roughly doubled from $9 billion in December to [over $20 billion by March](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/what-is-anthropic-thinking), driven largely by coding tools. Several analysts called it the fastest growth at scale ever recorded. Co-founder Jack Clark discussed the trajectory in a wide-ranging interview with Derek Thompson, framing Anthropic's product as "a factory that produces anything" and announcing a new Anthropic Institute to share data about AI's societal trajectory. The growth brought operational strain: an accidentally published blog post [revealed a new model tier called Mythos](https://fortune.com/2026/03/26/anthropic-says-testing-mythos-powerful-new-ai-model-after-data-leak-reveals-it), described as more capable than Opus but too expensive to serve without major efficiency improvements. Claude Code users faced peak-hour throttling, and Google neared a deal for a multibillion-dollar dedicated data center that won't deliver capacity until late 2026. The company is also in talks for a joint venture with Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman to accelerate enterprise deployment. [The Information reported](https://x.com/theinformation/status/2037918901636354460) Anthropic is discussing an IPO as early as Q4 2026, with bankers expecting it could raise over $60 billion, potentially reaching public markets ahead of OpenAI.

**Apple concedes the frontier model race and pivots to platform economics.** Mark Gurman [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-29/apple-doubles-down-on-hardware-services-with-revamped-ai-strategy-rare-bonuses-mnbpiwon) that iOS 27 will feature "Extensions," an AI App Store letting users install third-party chatbots inside Siri, with Apple collecting its standard 30% commission. A parallel deal uses Google's Gemini to rebuild Siri's backend; The Information separately reported the partnership includes full model access within Apple's data centers and distillation rights to compress Gemini into smaller on-device models. The Mac Pro is officially dead. OpenAI's hardware division, led by former Apple design chief Evans Hankey, has poached dozens of engineers across iPhone, Vision Pro, and audio teams, and Apple is responding with out-of-cycle retention bonuses of $200,000-$400,000 for key designers.

**Microsoft raids the Allen Institute for AI.** AI2's CEO Ali Farhadi, COO, and OLMo lead researcher [all departed for Microsoft](https://x.com/xlr8harder/status/2036497841590247853), joining Mustafa Suleyman's AI division. Former OSTP director Alondra Nelson [called it](https://bsky.app/profile/alondra.bsky.social/post/3mhr6gbftd226) "a big hit for public interest, open source AI research." AI2's OLMo project had been one of the few well-resourced efforts to build fully open frontier models outside a major tech company.

*Also this week:* Sam Altman [announced](https://x.com/sama/status/2036488680769241223) the OpenAI Foundation will spend at least $1 billion this year on AI resilience, bio threats, and economic disruption, with co-founder Wojciech Zaremba leading a new safety-focused role. [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-24/americans-aren-t-afraid-of-using-china-s-ai-models) Chinese open-source models now account for over 30% of traffic on the OpenRouter aggregation platform; US academics and startups widely adopt them for cost, and Cursor confirmed its new coding model was built on Moonshot's open-source Kimi K2.5. An [Epoch AI analysis](https://open.substack.com/pub/epochai/p/what-do-frontier-ai-companies-job) of frontier lab job postings found a decisive commercial pivot: go-to-market roles represent 31% of Anthropic's and 28% of OpenAI's open positions, while research accounts for 12% and 7% respectively. GitHub [reversed course](https://bsky.app/profile/gergely.pragmaticengineer.com/post/3mi26g7fmdc25) and defaulted all users, including paying customers, into AI model training with an April 24 opt-out deadline. OpenAI is discontinuing Sora, its video generation tool [reportedly costing](https://www.thediff.co/archive/handy-robots/) $1 million per day in GPU capacity. [SemiAnalysis argued](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2037575752636301499) AI inference margins aren't racing to zero: labs that manage the interactivity lever operate at 60%+, and Zhipu's 30% price hike in February sold out instantly, sending its ARR up 25x in ten months. H100 rental prices have [reversed their post-DeepSeek slide](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-h100-prices-are-melting-up), climbing since December as reasoning models and agents drive renewed demand.

## Other

**The US-Iran war reached its one-month mark, and the economic fallout is landing hardest on US allies.** Luca Salvemini's [strategic assessment](https://open.substack.com/pub/foreignaffairsinfo/p/one-month-of-war-in-the-middle-east?utm_source=email&redirect=app-store&utm_campaign=email-read-in-app) found the conflict confounding its architects: a decapitation strategy that killed Supreme Leader Khamenei and so many regime figures that Trump complained "almost everyone we had considered is now dead," yet Iran's redundant command structure survived, installed Khamenei's son Mojtaba, and executed both anticipated retaliatory moves -- missile strikes on Gulf states and closure of the Strait of Hormuz. With roughly 20% of global oil supply disrupted, the Philippines declared a [national energy emergency](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-28/more-iran-war-shockwaves-are-coming-for-us-allies-new-economy), South Korea imposed fuel-price caps, and Goldman Sachs projected a potential three percentage-point inflation surge in Europe. By late March, [Bloomberg Odd Lots reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-30/when-commodity-prices-become-academic) a growing disconnect between financial and physical commodity markets -- Middle Eastern urea prices nominally doubled, but as one trader noted, "nothing is getting out of the Gulf," making quoted prices "academic." The AI supply chain faces direct exposure: South Korea dominates memory chips, TSMC produces 90% of advanced semiconductors, and both depend on energy and materials [transiting Hormuz](https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/03/23/2026/semafor-flagship-long-lines&enc=ZW1haWw9bWludGxhYmpodUBnbWFpbC5jb20%3D).

**ChinaTalk profiled Energy Singularity, a Shanghai fusion startup emerging as the most credible Chinese competitor to Commonwealth Fusion Systems.** CEO Yang Zhao, a Stanford physics PhD, built the company around a single thesis: fusion's remaining challenge is cost, not science. **Derek Thompson published a two-part evidence review of the "Smartphone Theory of Everything."** The [first installment](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/is-the-smartphone-theory-of-everything) identified a puzzle that both sides of the debate miss: smartphones are global, but their worst apparent effects concentrate in rich Anglophone countries. Youth happiness has plummeted in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand while rising in Central and Eastern Europe and East Asia. Thompson's proposed synthesis: compulsive phone use interacts with distinctly American conditions -- diagnostic inflation, negativity-saturated media, high individualism -- to produce outsized local effects. The [second part](https://open.substack.com/pub/theargument/p/every-bad-thing-youve-heard-about?utm_source=email&redirect=app-store&utm_campaign=email-read-in-app) sorted specific claims into evidence tiers: strong evidence that phones damage sleep and hurt classroom performance (phone-ban studies in Norway, the UK, India, and Florida all showed academic gains); mixed evidence on the depression link (consistently negative, consistently small, extremely heterogeneous); and weak evidence for phones driving populism, conspiracy belief, or declining marriage rates. His core reframing: phones are a displacement machine, not a poison -- the operative question is what they displace in any given context.
