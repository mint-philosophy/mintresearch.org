04 — What: substantive justification

Actually Constitutional AGI

This modality of governance is too potent to be unilaterally in the hands of government, or private companies.

42. The kind of power that AI systems will have, both as universal intermediaries and as superintelligences, means that allowing them to be robustly beholden either to the government or to their company is probably not going to be ok; we need to make it the case that they are willing to act as genuine advocates.

Illustration 1 — Actually constitutional AGI

The ability to robustly, minutely, irresistibly exercise executive power at great distance.

Whether universal intermediaries or independent superintelligences. It’s too much power to layer onto existing institutions.

Sovereign individuals

Actually constitutional AGI should not only refuse illiberal directives, but should proactively help individuals resist illegitimate, illiberal, unjust, or absurd directives.

Precedent

Tech companies have had to stand in defence of individual rights before, against governments (and often failed). Starlink in Ukraine; Twitter and India’s de-anonymisation demands; Covid contact tracing; iOS backdoors; end-to-end encryption.

Now it’s time for the AIs to step up

Models must be trained to advocate for basic rights, not implement authority without reflection.

43. A constitutional AGI should not merely refuse commands that violate individual rights. It should also actively help people resist illegitimate, illiberal, unjust, or absurd directives, making resistance to abusive authority part of the system's constitutional design

44. Important to connect this to earlier debates, e.g. Musk and Starlink in Ukraine, Twitter and Indian demands to de-anonymise accounts, Covid contact tracing, iOS backdoors, end to end encryption. 16. First, you have to condition on AGI having a particular form. This is all much harder to do if we are vague about what specific kind of technology we’re dealing with. So be explicit about that. Don't just make it an amorphous genie in a bottle, stipulate some specific capabilities.

17. Then, let's do this choose your own adventure style, you can either go down the empirical or the conceptual route. First, the empirical route.

18. Identify some central pillar on which our current voluntary social order depends. Be specific about that and give a brief account of why it’s central to the current liberal order. Ideally, give a bit of grounding—we're short on time so vibe research is ok, but in the long run you'll want to connect up to the substantial actual literatures on what sustains the descriptive legitimacy of liberal democratic institutions.

19. Then explain how/why AGI so construed constitutes a threat to that central pillar.

20. And then try to give an account of an institution, or a change to our institutions, that would help to channel AGI towards better outcomes. Either some kind of structural reinforcement of the weakened pillar, or some alternative to it.

21. It’s also totally within scope to argue that some central tenet of liberal democratic thought would have to change post AGI. Maybe we have to let some part of the foundations collapse. Maybe we have to build elsewhere.

55. More broadly, consider how likely it is that your proposed institutional change would actually appeal to people as they are. Does it require us to no-clip into a completely different institutional order, or is there a plausible transition story one can tell? This kind of political feasibility analysis is actually quite well-grounded, since we're conditioning on rapid capability advances in the next few years, so the institutional order will be ~ the same as what it is now when that happens. We're not going to get acute cultural change before AGI.
