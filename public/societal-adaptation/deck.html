01 — Context

Institutional decline meets transformative AI

250-year-old liberal democratic institutions are sliding into a basin of mutually reinforcing crises. Into this comes math that wakes sand up.

The variance of possible outcomes is very high. By the end of the 2030s we could no longer be able to use networked computers; AI-enabled authoritarianism might dominate the world. Or AI may be our best hope for escaping the civilisational basin.

Key framing

AI’s role in governance and policy is not just, or even mostly, about governing AI itself.

If AGI is going to affect everything, then most “AGI Policy” will be about preparing every other societal domain for AGI, not regulating AGI itself.

Three Dimensions of Preparedness

1

Vulnerability Scanning + Mitigation

How will AGI (esp agents) disrupt institutional equilibria?

2

Obstacle Mapping + Elimination

If AI, like other normal technologies, will be hidebound by societal cruft, can we do something about that?

3

Capacity Building

Don’t just talk, build: RL environments, participatory processes, reduced transaction costs.

02 — Vulnerability Scanning

Our institutions are an ecosystem co-evolved with human agents. Introducing billions of AI agents is like introducing cane toads to Australia’s island ecology: they will find unoccupied niches and break equilibria that depend on specifically human properties.

Identify, Model, Mitigate

1. Identify the equilibrium that AGI is likely to disrupt

2. Model AGI’s impacts absent intervention

3. Harden the institution to preserve a stable and desirable equilibrium

03 — Map · Obstacle mapping

Sclerosis as a to-do list

“AI as Normal Technology” was right about one thing: many barriers lie in the way of deploying powerful AI.

Institutional sclerosis has an asymmetric effect: it won’t slow the most serious risks, but it will block many of the benefits.

But it’s not a law of nature.

Which of these obstacles provide important friction? Which should be refactored?

Drug discovery stuck in 10-year / $2.5B approval pipelines

Online tax filing killed by Intuit/TurboTax lobbying

City agencies still running on handwritten notes and paper records

04 — Build · Building state capacity

Using AI to revolutionise state capacity

If AI were used to massively improve bureaucracies and core service delivery, many democratic concerns about AI would dissipate. Municipal government may be the most promising level.

Key design question: agents are great at bullshit work. Should we layer agents over dysfunction, or zero-based budgeting for procedure first?

RL environments for government

Any government knowledge-work bottleneck is low-hanging fruit. But beware the McKinsey problem on steroids: public institutions must control the RL environments.

Participatory decision-making

Current mechanisms are reactionary and superficial. AI agents could enable genuine deliberation: open-source urban planning, showing systemic consequences of choices.

Reducing costs of negotiation

AI agents can dramatically lower transaction costs across labour relations, procurement, regulatory compliance, intergovernmental coordination. When negotiation is cheaper, more problems become solvable through agreement.

Krier 2025; Shahidi et al. 2025.
