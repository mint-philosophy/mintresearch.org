01 — Context

A Plausible World Model

Institutional decline meets transformative AI

250-year-old liberal democratic institutions are sliding into a basin of mutually reinforcing crises. Into this comes math that wakes sand up.

The variance of possible outcomes is very high. By the end of the 2030s we could no longer be able to use networked computers; AI-enabled authoritarianism might dominate the world. Or AI may be our best hope for escaping the civilisational basin.

Key Framing

AI's role in governance and policy is not just, or even mostly, about governing AI itself.

Instead it is about navigating this transition to powerful AI. This will affect everything. The people we train now will helm it.

02 — Capabilities

What AGI Actually Looks Like

We've already achieved something that would have been widely regarded as AGI three years ago if it arrived all at once. Whatever you think of the labels, the capabilities speak for themselves.

Locked In

Agentic AI — drop-in remote workers across all knowledge work. The "Claude Code moment" is the confirmation signal.

Essentially a solved problemNeeds only good RL environments and reward signals. There's still a threshold in competence/insight/judgment, but (a) that threshold is higher than most entry-level employees, and (b) every reason to think it will be crossed.

On the Horizon

AI for Science — superhuman expertise and originality. The kind that enables accelerating recursive self-improvement.

Early signsAI for math is the canary in the coal mine. Modest RSI is already happening — Anthropic's engineers barely write code themselves — but as yet it is a "lossy" form of self-improvement.

Can't Rule Out

Independent ASI — autonomous superintelligence. Plausibly beyond current architectures, but maybe don't bet against it.

Surprisingly few Rubicons remainBetween AI-for-science and ASI there may be surprisingly few remaining Rubicons. Embodied AI (robotics) is a separate path, also worth watching. Worth factoring into models but should not be the focus.

Where we are now: GPT-4 was the first inflection point. o1 the next. Opus 4.5 the third. The junior analyst threshold has been crossed. The next Rubicon is creativity and originality.

04 — Scan

Vulnerability Scanning

Our institutions are an ecosystem co-evolved with human agents. Introducing billions of AI agents is like introducing cane toads to Australia's island ecology — they will find unoccupied niches and break equilibria that depend on specifically human properties.

Democracy

Erosion of costly signals sustaining trust; agency laundering through intermediaries; dissolving the co-location constraint that forces inclusive politics

Markets

Strategic replicators that self-clone; agent capital concentration creating novel inequality dynamics; the intelligence curse

Bureaucracy

Parliamentary vs. presidential systems have very different vulnerability profiles for AI-enabled state automation

Law

What happens when everyone has legal representation for everything? Solve the backlog or radically exacerbate it?

Security

AI surveillance and lethal force settled by corporate contract; geopolitical implications of AI agents deploying across borders

Social

Parasocial relationships at scale; epistemic isolation; the end of boredom as a creative force

05 — Map

Obstacle Mapping

The barriers to AI deployment are not a wall, they are a jungle. Some obstacles protect people; some protect incumbents. Obstacle mapping means examining each barrier and asking: is this load-bearing safety, or is it calcified incompetence, rent-seeking, or reflexive caution?

Load-bearing safety

Requirements that genuinely protect people. Removing them creates real harm. Identify these and protect them fiercely — they're the ones most likely to be labelled "red tape" by people who don't bear the risks.

Calcified incompetence

Procedures that once served a purpose but now persist through institutional inertia. The obstacle mapper treats these as a to-do list: understand their original purpose, design replacements that preserve the function, then remove the obstacle.

The key skill is telling these apart. Most policy discourse treats all barriers as one or the other. The AGI-ready student sees the taxonomy.

06 — Build

Building State Capacity

Using AI to revolutionise state capacity

If AI were used to massively improve bureaucracies and core service delivery, many democratic concerns about AI would dissipate. Municipal government may be the most promising level.

Key design question: layer agents over dysfunction, or zero-based budgeting for procedure first?

RL Environments for Government

Any government knowledge-work bottleneck is low-hanging fruit. But beware the McKinsey problem on steroidsBuilding RL environments through competing private companies recreates the consulting trap: governments outsourced, saved on public service costs, but spent more on consultants with conflicting interests. Public institutions need to control the RL environments while using private models. — public institutions must control the RL environments.

Participatory Decision-Making

Current mechanisms are reactionary and superficial. AI agents could enable genuine deliberation: open-source urban planningCitizens produce specification-backed alternatives rather than vague objections — analogous to forking a software repo, but for policy. Agents survey preferences efficiently and show participants the systemic consequences of their choices., showing systemic consequences of choices.

Reducing Costs of Negotiation

AI agents can dramatically lower transaction costs across labour relations, procurement, regulatory compliance, intergovernmental coordination. When negotiation is cheaper, more problems become solvable through cooperation.

07 — Use + Verify

Agent Fluency & Verification

Agent fluency is a core professional competency. But the productivity multiplier is precisely what makes human expertise indispensable.

Research on Palantir's "Maven" claims 20 people can do the work of 2,000 in selecting military targets. It would be extraordinary if 20 people could verify that work. There is no git revert for people's lives.

This requires real graduate-level management skill. Some people find it demotivating to verify an AI agent's work; need to unlock the sense of potential that comes with being able to 10x your productivity.

Verification Gap

Checking frontier AI math proofs: 20 minutes → days in six months. Output difficulty grows faster than confirmation difficulty. Kirchner et al. 2024

Cognitive Debt

Moving fast without a mental map accumulates zombie systems. Open question whether time is saved if everything requires verification. Storey 2026

Bainbridge Irony

Intuition comes from being nose-to-nose with the problem. As AI takes over more of the work, the remaining human verification becomes harder — exactly when it matters most.

08 — Curriculum

What This Looks Like in Practice

A curriculum built around these four competencies doesn't replace traditional policy education — it extends it into the territory that will define the next decade.

Core Modules

Domain-specific vulnerability analysis. Obstacle taxonomy workshops. State capacity design labs. Agent-assisted policy drafting with verification protocols.

Practicum

Real-world projects with municipal governments. Students design AI-enhanced processes, map obstacles, and build verification frameworks — not simulations.

The goal is graduates who can walk into any policy institution and immediately contribute to the AI transition — not as technologists, but as people who understand what's coming and can shape how it lands.
