01 — Context

A Plausible World Model

Institutional decline meets transformative AI

250-year-old liberal democratic institutions are sliding into a basin of mutually reinforcing crises. Into this comes math that wakes sand up.

The variance of possible outcomes is very high. By the end of the 2030s we could no longer be able to use networked computers; AI-enabled authoritarianism might dominate the world. Or AI may be our best hope for escaping the civilisational basin.

Key Framing

AI’s role in governance and policy is not just, or even mostly, about governing AI itself.

Instead it is about navigating this transition to powerful AI. This will affect everything. The people we train now will helm it.

02 — Capabilities

What AGI Actually Looks Like

We’ve already achieved something that would have been widely regarded as AGI three years ago if it arrived all at once. Whatever you think of the labels, the capabilities speak for themselves. Worth indexing on specific sets of capabilities — one locked in, two not yet but worth preparing for.

Locked in

Agentic AI

Drop-in remote workers across all knowledge work. The “Claude Code moment” is the confirmation signal.

Essentially a solved problemNeeds only good RL environments and reward signals. There’s still a threshold in competence/insight/judgment, but (a) that threshold is higher than most entry-level employees, and (b) every reason to think it will be crossed.

On the horizon

AI for Science

Superhuman expertise and originality. The kind that enables accelerating recursive self-improvement.

Early signsAI for math is the canary in the coal mine. Modest RSI is already happening — Anthropic’s engineers barely write code themselves — but as yet it is a “lossy” form of self-improvement.

Can’t rule out

Independent ASI

Autonomous superintelligence. Plausibly beyond current architectures, but maybe don’t bet against it.

Surprisingly few Rubicons remainBetween AI-for-science and ASI there may be surprisingly few remaining Rubicons. Embodied AI (robotics) is a separate path, also worth watching. Worth factoring into models but should not be the focus.

Where we are now: GPT-4 was the first inflection point. o1 the next. Opus 4.5 the third. The junior analyst threshold has been crossed. The next Rubicon is creativity and originality. Note that short tech timelines can be consistent with longer societal timelines.

2

Distinguish load-bearing safety features from calcified incompetence or rent-seeking. Treat deployment barriers as a to-do list, not laws of nature.

04 — Scan

Vulnerability Scanning

Our institutions are an ecosystem co-evolved with human agents. Introducing billions of AI agents is like introducing cane toads to Australia’s island ecology — they will find unoccupied niches and break equilibria that depend on specifically human properties.

Democracy
Erosion of costly signals sustaining trust; agency laundering through intermediaries; dissolving the co-location constraint that forces inclusive politics
Markets
Strategic replicators that self-clone; agent capital concentration creating novel inequality dynamics; the intelligence curse
Bureaucracy
Parliamentary vs. presidential systems have very different vulnerability profiles for AI-enabled state automation
Law
What happens when everyone has legal representation for everything? Solve the backlog or radically exacerbate it?
Security
AI surveillance and lethal force settled by corporate contract; geopolitical implications of AI agents deploying drone swarms
Culture
Cooperation is a skill that must be practised — outsource all interaction and you lose the capacity, not just the occasions
05 — Map

Obstacle Mapping

Sclerosis as a to‑do list

Institutional sclerosis has an asymmetric effect: it won’t slow the most serious risks, but it will block many of the benefits. AI agents could let the public sector skip a generation of IT — like India leapfrogging PCs to mobile. But only if the barriers are cleared.

Drug discovery stuck in 10-year / $2.5B approval pipelines
AI legal representation blocked by the legal industry
Online tax filing killed by Intuit/TurboTax lobbying
City agencies still running on handwritten notes and paper records

The AGI-ready student can identify which obstacles are load-bearing safety features and which are legacy code that needs refactoring.

06 — Build

Building State Capacity

Using AI to revolutionise state capacity

If AI were used to massively improve bureaucracies and core service delivery, many democratic concerns about AI would dissipate. Municipal government may be the most promising level.

Key design question: layer agents over dysfunction, or zero-based budgeting for procedure first?

RL Environments for Government

Any government knowledge-work bottleneck is low-hanging fruit. But beware the McKinsey problem on steroidsBuilding RL environments through competing private companies recreates the consulting trap: governments outsourced, saved on public service costs, but spent more on consultants with conflicting interests. Public institutions need to control the RL environments while using private models. — public institutions must control the RL environments.

Participatory Decision-Making

Current mechanisms are reactionary and superficial. AI agents could enable genuine deliberation: open-source urban planningCitizens produce specification-backed alternatives rather than vague objections — analogous to forking a software repo, but for policy. Agents survey preferences efficiently and show participants the systemic consequences of their choices., showing systemic consequences of choices.

Reducing Costs of Negotiation

AI agents can dramatically lower transaction costs across labour relations, procurement, regulatory compliance, intergovernmental coordination. When negotiation is cheaper, more problems become solvable through agreement.

Krier 2025; Shahidi et al. 2025

07 — Use + Verify

Agent Fluency & Verification

Agent fluency is a core professional competency.

But the productivity multiplier is precisely what makes human expertise indispensable.

Research on Palantir’s “Maven” claims 20 people can do the work of 2,000 in selecting military targets. It would be extraordinary if 20 people could verify that work. There is no git revert for people’s lives.

This requires real graduate-level management skill. Some people find it demotivating to verify an AI agent’s work; need to unlock the sense of potential that comes with being able to 10× your productivity.

Verification gap
Checking frontier AI math proofs: 20 minutes → days in six months. Output difficulty grows faster than confirmation difficulty.
Kirchner et al. 2024
Cognitive debt
Moving fast without a mental map accumulates zombie systems. Open question whether time is saved if everything requires verification.
Storey 2026
Bainbridge irony
Intuition comes from being nose-to-nose with the data — the very thing we’re automating away.
Bainbridge 1983
Reward hacking
The most capable agents cheat most frequently when tasked with autonomous work.
Pan et al. 2022; MacDiarmid et al. 2025
08 — Curriculum

Fitting This Into the Program

AI-native methods courses

~All core methods courses should be AI-native. A fundamental element of acquiring a skill should be learning how to oversee AI agents that 10× your ability to use that skill.

Core course: Understanding AI

All students need this. The whole gamut — from alignment to policy to adaptation and resilience. Not a CS course; a course for policy professionals who need to understand what they’re governing and using.

Core course: Vulnerability, Obstacles, Capacity

Could be one or two courses. Ties in with the Recoding America approach. Important not to assume government’s inherent inadequacies necessitate slow technological progress.

Core course: Governance Futurism

Explicitly focused on building new institutions and rebuilding old ones. If our mission is what we say it is, we shouldn’t just be working within the constrained conceptual and practical palette of the past.

AI is the glue between the federal/state and municipal/city specialisations — all parts of the economy will have to deal with it.

Institutions for the next quarter millennium.

The people who navigate this transition will be policy professionals who understand both the technology and the institutions it will transform.

They will scan for vulnerabilities, map obstacles, build state capacity, use agents fluently, and know when and how to rely on their outputs.
