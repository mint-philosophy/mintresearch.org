NSA network outage disrupts staff during scheduled maintenance

AP reported that critical operations continued despite the internal disruption. Clayton attributed the failure to patching; Warner warned that AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is straining systems.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 17 November 2026 469 words · ~2 min

The National Security Agency’s classified internal communications network went offline on Tuesday, November 17, during scheduled maintenance, Eric Tucker and Konstantin Toropin reported for the Associated Press. A defense official, speaking anonymously because they were not authorized to discuss the incident by name, identified the affected network as NSANet, which agency employees use to communicate classified information. The NSA acknowledged disruption to some internal systems but said its “mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational.” It said redundant infrastructure sustained operations during the disruption, which it described as temporary degradation during routine work. AP credited CNN with first reporting the outage; its account did not establish the technical cause.

Reporting from Fort Meade described the effects on staff. In an account updated November 18, Martin Matishak of The Record reported delayed opening and staggered arrivals intended to avoid tens of thousands of employees signing in together. One source said the network provided intermittent service, while another said it had been unstable for weeks. Backup centers elsewhere in the country were expected to take on intelligence-collection work. In a Wednesday, November 18 update, two sources told CBS that NSANet was running again, but staggered arrivals continued because officials feared simultaneous logins would overload it. CBS reported no announced end to that schedule.

Jay Clayton attributed the broader run of technology disruptions to overlapping upgrades in a CNBC interview quoted by AP. He said organizations were patching older systems with known vulnerabilities and encountering problems as they took systems down and restarted them. Later Tuesday, in an interview with Fox Business, he specifically attributed the NSA outage to patching following the identification of a PHP vulnerability. He said some systems failed to restart as expected or communicate properly with other systems. The NSA’s own statement described scheduled maintenance without confirming his explanation.

Senator Mark Warner’s November 17 statement addressed outages across banks, technology companies and government systems. Warner, the Senate Intelligence Committee’s vice chairman, said AI was enabling vulnerability discovery across large numbers of systems at unprecedented speed. In his account, that created pressure to apply fixes immediately, often simultaneously, straining infrastructure unable to accommodate such rapid changes. He said the available evidence pointed toward legitimate security work and that he would continue investigating. His statement did not identify the NSA’s particular fault or establish a common technical cause across the incidents.

Warner also used the disruptions to argue for stronger AI and cybersecurity rules. He criticized the administration’s cuts to CISA and called for reliable information about the incidents. His office’s statement described legislation he introduced with Senators Brian Schatz and Andy Kim that would impose pre-deployment testing, stronger cybersecurity requirements and mandatory reporting of serious safety and security incidents for the most advanced AI systems. Warner and his colleagues had sought unanimous consent for the bill that week.
