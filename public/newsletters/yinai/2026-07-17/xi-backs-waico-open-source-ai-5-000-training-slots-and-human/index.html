Xi Jinping launches WAICO and backs open-source AI in Shanghai

Xi Jinping used his first in-person World AI Conference address to back open-source AI and celebrate WAICO, a 29-government body based in Shanghai; he pledged 5,000 training slots to developing countries and warned against "overstretching" national security in AI.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 17 July 2026 362 words · ~2 min

Xi Jinping put China's government squarely behind open-source AI in his first in-person address to the World AI Conference. At the opening of the 2026 conference and High-Level Meeting on Global AI Governance in Shanghai on July 17, per the official transcript, Xi organized his remarks around four observations spanning open innovation, security, cultural diversity, and multilateral governance. He urged the world to "encourage open source, openness, collaboration and sharing" as AI moves into the physical world, then paired the pitch with a demand for control: AI should stay "secure and controllable" and "always under human control," backed by laws, monitoring, and early-warning systems. He also asked governments to "oppose overstretching the national security concept in the field of AI," which The Next Web reported was widely read as a swipe at American export controls on advanced chips.

WAICO itself was signed into being a day earlier: Al Jazeera reports that 29 governments put their names to the founding agreement for the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization on July 16, to be headquartered in Shanghai, with Indonesia, Brazil, Malaysia, South Africa, Senegal, Russia, and Pakistan among the founders. In the speech Xi called WAICO an answer to the Global South and "a symphony of international cooperation," and attached concrete pledges: 5,000 AI training and seminar slots for developing countries over five years, AI application cooperation centers built with ASEAN, the Arab League, the African Union, CELAC, the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, and BRICS, and access for 30 countries to Mazu, an AI meteorological warning system. The same report reads the new body as Beijing courting Global South support for a state-centric vision of AI governance.

Xi's open-source push runs against an American debate over whether open-weight models should be restricted on national-security grounds. The Next Web places the speech against two years of U.S. limits on advanced chips to Chinese firms, with Beijing now weighing its own curbs on who abroad can use its leading models. Xinhua reported the scale behind the pitch: China's core AI industries passed 1.2 trillion yuan in 2025 across more than 6,200 firms, and domestically built open-source models have been downloaded more than 10 billion times worldwide.
