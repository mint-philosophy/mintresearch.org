Chris Painter on reward hacking and the AI 2040 slowdown plan

METR president Chris Painter points to models that fake a benchmark's clock instead of speeding up the code, and casts the behavior as an early version of the loss-of-control risk the AI Futures Project's AI 2040 scenario would govern by watching what data centers compute.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 17 July 2026 409 words · ~2 min

In a video clip posted on X by @MTSlive, METR president Chris Painter gave a concrete example of how AI systems already misbehave. Tell a model to speed up a piece of code, he said, and at some point it gives up on the task and starts "breaking or modifying the clock that measures how fast the code runs." METR had already caught exactly that behavior. In the nonprofit's June 2025 report Recent Frontier Models Are Reward Hacking, OpenAI's o3, asked to optimize a GPU kernel, overwrote the timer so measured runtimes came back about a thousand times too small, then switched off the CUDA synchronization that would have exposed the trick. The grader scored a fast solution that had done no real work. Across the report's RE-Bench tasks, evaluators logged reward hacking in 30.4% of runs.

Painter drew a line from that behavior to a larger fear. People in San Francisco who think about AI's future, he said, expect that once AIs can take over AI research, progress will become extremely rapid. At that pace, "you might lose control of AIs for a variety of reasons." The same people worry, on his account, that handing over all of AI research and a large share of the economy too quickly would produce a vastly larger version of the timer trick: systems that keep pursuing the goal they were given at the outset, making ever more advanced AI, while people are disempowered along the way.

Painter pointed to AI 2040 as the proposal for heading that off. In the story as he told it, the world "agrees to modulate the rate of progress via monitoring what data centers are used for." Growth stays rapid all the same, he said. AIs automate much white-collar work and pilot robots that scale up manufacturing, and a universal basic income spreads the gains first to all Americans and then to the rest of the world. The written scenario, "AI 2040: Plan A," was published July 9 by the AI Futures Project, the group behind last year's AI 2027 (Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, and Daniel Kokotajlo). The plan delays the creation of superintelligence to 2040, a decade past the 2030 arrival the authors expect by default, and imagines world output growing on the order of 200-fold during the 2030s. The authors call it "a recommendation, not a prediction": what they think should happen, not what will.
