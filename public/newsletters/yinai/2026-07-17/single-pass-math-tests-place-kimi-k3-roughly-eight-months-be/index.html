Ryan Greenblatt puts Kimi K3's pretraining eight to ten months behind Anthropic

A day after Moonshot's K3 drew gap-closing headlines, Greenblatt ran his single-forward-pass math test, walked back his own bullish estimate, and put K3's pretraining eight to ten months behind Anthropic, crediting much of the shipped model's strength to post-training and distillation.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 17 July 2026 445 words · ~2 min

On X, Redwood Research chief scientist Ryan Greenblatt posted quick tests placing Kimi K3's pretraining roughly halfway between Anthropic's Opus 4 and Opus 4.5, about 10 months behind Anthropic. Because the tests probably understate gains from better data, he judged the pretraining similarly good to Opus 4.5's, around eight months back. He had earlier guessed that K3's pretraining sat "somewhere between 4.8 and Mythos," close to Anthropic's newest models; he now calls that guess "very wrong." Moonshot AI released K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open mixture-of-experts model with a million-token context, on July 16. On Moonshot's own numbers, MarkTechPost reports, K3 beats Opus 4.8 on most benchmarks and trails only Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 overall; TechCrunch wrote that it was expected to match or surpass Opus 4.8.

The measure comes from Greenblatt's December 2025 LessWrong post, "Measuring no CoT math time horizon (single forward pass)", which scores how often a model solves competition math in one forward pass with no chain of thought, then maps that accuracy to the human solve time at which the model succeeds half the time. Across about 600 middle-school MATHCOUNTS problems and 300 Hungarian competition problems, Anthropic's models trace a clean trend, doubling roughly every nine months: Opus 4's horizon sits near 2.3 minutes, Opus 4.5's near 3.4. He favors single-pass math because it tracks pretraining quality and is not improved by post-training; measuring loss would be better, but "we don't have the base and can't measure loss on closed weight models." The same test puts Kimi K2.6 around halfway between Sonnet 4 and Sonnet 4.5.

Greenblatt pointed to a fuller treatment as the better reference: "Estimating No-CoT Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models" (arXiv 2606.07157), a June paper by Anders Cairns Woodruff, Francis Rhys Ward, and colleagues. Across 43 tasks and 14 models from GPT-2 to GPT-5.5, it finds these single-pass horizons doubling about every 373 days, roughly half the pace of gains with chain of thought since GPT-4, a divergence the authors trace to externalized reasoning driving most recent capability gains. That fits his read of K3: if the pretraining trails Opus 4.5 while the shipped model scores near the top on benchmarks, more of its strength likely comes from post-training, mid-training, or "distillation-type effects." Against Anthropic's newer pretraining, which he calls Mythos and says finished training about five months ago, he puts K3 further back still, so genuine catch-up times would run somewhat longer if the leading labs paused while investment kept following current trends.

The test, Greenblatt stressed, speaks to pretraining alone, underweights coding-specific data quality, and gives "just some signal about what is going on." He closed with an apology for the error.
