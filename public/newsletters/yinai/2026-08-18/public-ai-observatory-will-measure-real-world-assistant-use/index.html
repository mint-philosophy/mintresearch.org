Assistant use varies across public data sources

A team from 14 institutions applies 145 features to 23,158 conversations; its first release shows why no single public corpus represents real-world use.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 18 August 2026 224 words · ~2 min

In the preprint The AI Observatory: A Public Measure of Real-World AI Use, Shayne Longpre, Anka Reuel, Dayeon Ki, and collaborators from 14 institutions present a public platform for comparing real assistant conversations. The first release applies one taxonomy of 145 features to 23,158 conversations and 85,633 turns from seven sources: WildChat, ShareGPT, AI Archive, Grok shared chats, LMSYS Chat 1M, Chatbot Arena, and the National Internet Observatory. The annotations describe user function, topic, sensitive use, interaction style, multi-turn dynamics, media, language, and conversation structure. The released dataset contains the annotations; raw conversations remain with their original sources.

Comparing the seven sources, the authors find that source choice changes the apparent mix of tasks, conversation structures, and sensitive uses. Applying the March 2025 Anthropic Economic Index occupational filter to six accessible sources would exclude 47.9 percent of conversations as nonoccupational. Much of that material concerned personal, social, cultural, and safety-related use. WildChat conversations grew longer over time, and OpenAI model variants drew different patterns of use. The system uses GPT-4.1 to label conversations; parent-category F1 against an author-created human consensus set ranged from 0.756 to 0.942. Because the source populations do not represent all assistant use, the authors identify proprietary deployments, on-device assistants, and much of the Global South as major gaps. The project site hosts the taxonomy, reconstruction tools, and analysis dashboard.
