Britain assesses the cost of losing frontier models

The Cabinet Office asked BIST to examine growth and cybersecurity effects after US export controls briefly forced Anthropic to suspend its newest models.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 18 August 2026 179 words · ~2 min

In an August 18 Financial Times report, Lucy Fisher writes that the Cabinet Office has asked the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade to assess the effect on UK growth if British companies lose access to the latest frontier models. Fisher said the assessment also covers cybersecurity, including vulnerabilities that could follow a cutoff. Dame Chi Onwurah, chair of the Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee, welcomed the assessment and called for a plan to reduce “unacceptable levels of dependency.”

Anthropic said a June 12 US export-control directive barred every foreign national from Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Unable to verify nationality in real time, the company suspended both models for all customers. Anthropic restored Fable 5 globally on July 1 after the controls were lifted; Mythos 5 returned only to approved US organizations. Fisher linked Britain’s assessment to that interruption. A July government reorganization had already moved AI strategy, public-sector AI adoption, and the AI Security Institute to the Cabinet Office, while BIST gained a mandate to connect science and technology with business growth.
