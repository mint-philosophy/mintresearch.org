Nvidia names Colette Kress interim CEO after Jensen Huang resigns amid investigations

Nvidia confirms Kress's interim appointment after the board accepted Huang's resignation on November 11. WIRED examines the board's motives; The Information reports investments in DeepSeek intended to secure a Blackwell supply relationship.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 12 November 2026 924 words · ~5 min

Nvidia has replaced Jensen Huang with Colette Kress as interim chief executive following the board inquiry announced on November 8. Caroline Haskins and Lauren Goode report the departure in WIRED; Nvidia's regulatory filing dates the board's acceptance of Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director to November 11, effective immediately. The November 12 announcement says Kress will retain her CFO duties during the search for a permanent successor, which will consider internal and external candidates. The board also appointed Harvey C. Jones, a director since 1993 and former Synopsys chief executive, as its first chair. Neither document states why Huang left.

WIRED attributes its account of the board's reasoning to one person familiar with the matter. That person says Huang's alleged relationship with a subordinate and his business dealings with Megaspeed probably weighed more heavily than the espionage allegations involving Alice Huang. WIRED describes Megaspeed as a Singapore AI company where Alice Huang previously held a senior position. The article connects the company's dealings to Huang's longstanding opposition to restrictions on selling American chips to China: he has argued that the restrictions surrender customers and encourage China to develop competing technology.

The subordinate allegation appeared in The New York Times on November 11. Ryan Mac, Tripp Mickle and Georgia Gee cite five people who worked at Nvidia in reporting that Huang had an extramarital relationship with Kimberly Powell, an executive who reported to him. They also identify Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz as the law firm leading the board's investigation. In an earlier WIRED report, Lauren Goode traced Huang's use of crisis communications firm Heller to at least October 2025. By that month's end, Nvidia communications staff had been told to include the firm in personal and professional press inquiries about him. That month, the Times had reported a Commerce Department investigation into whether Megaspeed helped Chinese companies evade export controls.

Huang's departure leaves Nvidia replacing a founder who ran the company for 33 years. Fortune reports that he had about 60 direct reports, that Nvidia has no chief operating officer, and that the company had not set out a clear succession plan. Kress has been CFO since September 2013. Inside Nvidia, Paresh Dave and Zoë Schiffer report for WIRED that a suggestion-box post asking the board to consider employee sentiment attracted more than 1,600 comments, many urging Huang's return. The post preceded his resignation; many employees had expected the investigation to finish before a leadership change.

In the accompanying Information report, Jing Yang and Qianer Liu describe another business relationship involving companies tied to Alice Huang. Three people with direct knowledge told them that Century Litong and its Shanghai-listed parent, Jiangsu Lettall Electronics, invested in DeepSeek's second funding round. One of those people said the investments were intended to establish a continuing supply of Nvidia Blackwell chips to DeepSeek, despite restrictions on selling those chips to China. According to the sources, DeepSeek employees introduced the companies to other investors as a substantial supplier of black-market Nvidia chips and argued that this warranted an allocation in the round.

The companies, collectively known in the industry as Litong Inc, invested through vehicles created by other investors, so no Litong entity appears directly on DeepSeek's shareholder list. The Information cites stock exchange filings and Chinese corporate records showing that Lettall put 95 million yuan ($14 million) into a vehicle run by Monolith Management. Founded by former Sequoia Capital China partner Cao Xi, Monolith is DeepSeek's third-largest investor. A source said Litong invested through several vehicles; the reporters could not establish its total contribution.

The Information carefully describes Alice Huang's connection to these businesses. She never held a stake or executive post at Century Litong or Lettall; the reporters traced her links through past ownerships and corporate transactions involving her sister, Huang Yu, and husband, Kang Wei. Century Litong became an Nvidia preferred cloud partner four months after its founding in 2023. The article recaps the outlet's earlier finding that it imported 240,000 Nvidia chips worth at least $7.5 billion into China since 2023. Two additional sources say Century Litong imported Blackwell chips. Their account does not establish that those chips reached DeepSeek, and the reported investment purpose remains a plan for supply.

DeepSeek's round ran from July to October, raising 105.5 billion yuan ($15.8 billion) from nearly 30 investors at a valuation of about $92 billion, The Information reports. Tencent and CATL were its largest backers. The company has been adapting to Huawei chips while continuing to seek Blackwell hardware for research and as a fallback. The report therefore describes a possible commercial route to computing resources alongside DeepSeek's fundraising, with documentary evidence for one investment and anonymous sourcing for the proposed chip supply.

Separate inquiries into Megaspeed continue. Paul Mozur reports in the Times that Singapore held Alice Huang's passport from June 2025 until February 2026 over potential false declarations relating to the company. Singapore said it released her with a warning because of insufficient evidence and barred her from returning. It also said US agencies had provided limited assistance after it sought Supermicro's cooperation; Supermicro denies selling products to Megaspeed. On the espionage allegation, CNN reports disagreement among US intelligence officials and quotes Todd Blanche saying the Justice Department had reached no conclusion about Alice Huang's status. China has denied that she was a spy.

Nvidia says its third-quarter results will be released on November 17 as scheduled. Jones says the board expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption while Kress leads the company.
