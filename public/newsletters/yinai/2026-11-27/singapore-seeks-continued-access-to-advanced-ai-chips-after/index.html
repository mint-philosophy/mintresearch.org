Singapore seeks continued access to US AI chips

Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong described what Singapore could offer Washington on enforcement. Separately, Bloomberg identified a Shanghai data center’s restricted Nvidia equipment from a lender’s records.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 27 November 2026 598 words · ~3 min

Claudia Chong reported in The Information that Singapore is discussing with Washington how businesses there can keep buying advanced AI chips under US export controls. Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong described the talks at the Asia Future Summit on November 27. Asked whether Nvidia had stopped shipping chips to Southeast Asia, Gan said Singapore had yet to confirm a halt and was asking businesses how the rules affected them. He pointed out that some of the largest data center operators in Singapore are American companies serving customers across the region.

In the government transcript, Gan described a limit to what Singapore could offer Washington. Singapore can investigate activities on its own territory and share evidence under its own laws. When transactions take place abroad, it may have to ask its trading partners for the evidence it needs to act. Officials were reviewing how they monitor trade, he said. He gave no details of an agreement on continued chip access.

The US Commerce Department rule, in effect since November 19, generally requires a license to ship certain advanced chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia or Brazil, or to transfer them within those countries. US-headquartered recipients and subsidiaries of US-headquartered parents qualify for an exception when they meet reporting requirements. A separate license requirement can apply to overseas data centers that give customers based in China and certain other jurisdictions remote access to large amounts of computing power. Such customers can rent time on servers without taking delivery of a chip.

As Thursday’s issue reported, people involved in the financing told Bloomberg that some banks had delayed work on a proposed $6.6 billion loan to Bridge Data Centres. ByteDance is among the tenants of its Malaysian facilities. Bridge declined to comment.

In Shanghai, a separate Bloomberg investigation identified restricted hardware from records that Shanghai Innovation Bank filed with Chinese regulators. Shanghai Shuoyao had offered equipment at the Yangtze River Delta Project as collateral for a 96 million yuan loan. Andy Lin, Mackenzie Hawkins and Kari Soo Lindberg matched a serial number from the filing to a listing in a public warranty database. It identified a Gigabyte Technology rack containing 72 Nvidia Blackwell Ultra chips. Gigabyte declined to identify the buyer or say when it sold the rack.

Another borrower, Shanghai Kezhan, had listed a GB200 server at the same facility when it obtained a 90 million yuan loan in late 2025. US rules required a license to ship either model to China. Bloomberg could identify the two borrowers and the equipment they pledged. The route the chips took to Shanghai remains unknown. As we previously reported, Nvidia introduced Alibaba to Alice Huang, who founded Singapore-based Megaspeed. Shanghai Shuoyao has acknowledged providing services to Megaspeed, and Megaspeed used a rendering of the Shanghai site in investor materials. Bloomberg said it had no evidence that Megaspeed itself diverted equipment there. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

US lawmakers had sought Nvidia records and interviews before Bloomberg published the Shanghai findings. In their November 24 Tech Policy Press tracker, Justin Hendrix and Dean Jackson documented the range of requests. Representative Suhas Subramanyam wanted records that might show whether anyone received kickbacks. Senator Andy Kim sought a classified briefing and sworn testimony from Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. After Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang, incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries called for an investigation into Nvidia and Commerce and wanted Congress to legislate on exports in the defense authorization bill. Senator John Hickenlooper asked Senate leadership candidates to consider a select committee on AI.
