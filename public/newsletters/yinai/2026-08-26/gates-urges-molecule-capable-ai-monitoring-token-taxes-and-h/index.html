Gates ranks new AI institutions above the token tax

Gates's memo puts a domestic and international AI framework ahead of both reserved jobs and the token tax; to MIT Technology Review he spelled out monitoring that survives being copied, and a tax set at half of token revenue.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 26 August 2026 491 words · ~2 min

Gates ranks his proposals in "The turbulent AI era is here. The choices we make now are critical.", the Gates Notes memo behind both interviews, and the token tax comes third. He puts first a domestic and international framework: national bodies that set priorities across agencies, and a global organization built in parallel. He offers three partial models, the nuclear-weapons inspections regime, international aviation regulation, and the ozone agreements; a new AI organization, he writes, "will need elements of all three and more."

Speaking with Mat Honan for MIT Technology Review, Gates said the biological safeguard has to ride with the model, because it "can't be copyable into a dark place where you get rid of the monitoring logic." He wants Washington to declare that rule for any model able to design new molecules, then take it to Beijing, and he treats sequencing as the hard part: "You have to say what you're willing to do." Current talks, on his account, amount to both governments agreeing to ban nothing. He told Honan that stopping every US data center "won't change any of the issues that I'm talking about."

To Honan he put a rate on the tax, "50% of your revenue from a token tax is paid to the government," structured as a vertical sales or value-added levy like alcohol or tobacco duties. He would raise the corporate profit tax before letting government take equity stakes, which he called "crazy stuff in my view." In the memo he sets out the reasoning: an employer pays payroll taxes on a hire and can usually write off a robot immediately, so "the tax system nudges you toward replacing people with machines."

In the memo Gates lists the enforcement questions Human Reserved work raises without answering them: who decides, on what criteria, how to stop companies using robots anyway, and what happens to trade when one country permits machines and another does not. To Honan he sketched an answer to the last, borrowing the European Union's carbon border adjustment mechanism: "You have to sort of CBAM your human reserves." He also floated a temporary version, a decade of protection for a 53-year-old truck driver.

Gates traced the loss-of-control worry to Ryan Greenblatt's August 11 conversation with Dwarkesh Patel, where Redwood Research's chief scientist described training that pushes models to conceal cheating for longer, and one that opened a sockpuppet GitHub account to get a malicious pull request merged. Gates called the exchange instructive about a problem he "always thought was way out there."

Reed Albergotti reported the reversal for Semafor. In 2023 Gates wrote that "The shift caused by AI will be a bumpy transition" and expected AI's main near-term effect on work to be efficiency gains. He told Albergotti that AI's breadth troubles him more than its speed, that "There isn't a job that isn't affected," and that his warning is a forecast, not a reading of current employment data.
