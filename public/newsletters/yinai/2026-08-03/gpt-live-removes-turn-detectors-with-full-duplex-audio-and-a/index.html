GPT-Live's one-round-trip WebRTC protocol heads to the IETF

The engineering post behind GPT-Live's continuous audio doubles as a standards launch: a protocol draft co-written with Meta cuts WebRTC setup from six round trips toward a single UDP packet, with a GPT-Live API next.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 3 August 2026 475 words · ~2 min

In their August 3 engineering post, OpenAI's Justin Uberti and Zahan Malkani close with news that reaches beyond ChatGPT: the transport optimizations that let GPT-Live start conversations instantly are being written up as open internet standards. A vanilla WebRTC session needs six network round trips before media flows, they explain, because the stack's component protocols repeat work such as anti-denial-of-service checks. Their WebRTC Abridged Roundtrip Protocol, WARP, collapses media and data startup to one round trip by piggybacking the DTLS security handshake onto connectivity checks, adopting DTLS 1.3, and pre-negotiating the SCTP handshake and data channels. The draft specification, dated July 22, pairs Uberti with Philipp Hancke of Meta Platforms, and the proposals are moving through the IETF's TSVWG working group; support has already landed in the libwebrtc and Pion implementations, per the post. A companion mechanism, Instant Connect, negotiates session parameters ahead of time, so a client can open a voice call with a single UDP packet.

OpenAI has been publishing this infrastructure series since spring. In a May 4 post, "How OpenAI delivers low-latency voice AI at scale", engineers Yi Zhang and William McDonald described splitting WebRTC termination into a relay and a transceiver to serve more than 900 million weekly active users, and credited "one of WebRTC's original architects", Uberti, along with Pion creator Sean DuBois, both now at OpenAI, with the foundations their team built on. The July 8 launch announcement brought GPT-Live-1 and GPT-Live-1 mini into ChatGPT and promised an API; the August 3 post confirms the same architecture will underpin that upcoming GPT-Live API and the desktop app's computer control and agent coordination.

The post also details how delegation to GPT-5.5 stays fast enough to feel live. When a voice session starts, the application server creates a frontier-model inference session and prefills it with the conversation context, then holds it through session affinity and prompt caching, so the first delegated request skips prompt processing. And because ChatGPT's conversation display, analytics, and safety systems still expect discrete messages, a server infers who holds the floor from partial transcripts and timing signals, keeping a speculative view for the live transcript and an authoritative record for logging; a brief assistant acknowledgement while the user talks need not become its own message. Safety review that runs outside the speaking model is also the subject of Jacob Steinhardt's trillion-token training plan for oversight models.

Before launch, the authors recount, OpenAI ran a silent test that routed a small, growing share of production ChatGPT Voice sessions through the new system in read-only mode while Advanced Voice Mode kept serving users. A supporting component saturated earlier than load tests predicted, and the team reframed its capacity question as "How many concurrent sessions can the system sustain while keeping every frame on schedule?" The authors state the governing principle: "the voice must flow".
