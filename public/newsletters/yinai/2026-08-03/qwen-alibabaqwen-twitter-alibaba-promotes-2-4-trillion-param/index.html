Alibaba launches Qwen3.8-Max and pledges its first Max-class open weights

The 2.4-trillion-parameter flagship arrives with multi-day autonomous coding showpieces and $2-per-million input pricing; weights land on Hugging Face within a week alongside an open Qwen3.8-27B.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 3 August 2026 449 words · ~2 min

On X, Alibaba's Qwen team announced Qwen3.8-Max on August 3, calling it "our most capable model to date" and pricing the API at $2.00 per million input tokens and $6.00 per million output. The Qwen Team's blog post, "Qwen3.8-Max: A New Bar for Coding and Cowork," specifies 2.4 trillion total parameters with 95 billion active per query and a one-million-token context window, and says the launch marks "the first time we will open-source the weights of a Qwen-Max-class model," with weights due on Hugging Face and ModelScope within a week. A smaller Qwen3.8-27B goes open alongside it.

The blog leads with multi-day autonomous runs. Alibaba reports that the model built a coding agent, oh-my-cli, across roughly 16 days of unattended operation, logging 265 commits, 127 pull requests, and 151 issues by July 30 in a public GitHub repository that now shows 536 commits; that it reproduced a data-selection research paper from nothing but the paper and a set of GPUs in about five days, then evolved a method beating the paper's approach by 2.7 points on AIME24; and that it drove a cryptographic accelerator design from 8,298 gates down to 678 over roughly 500 autonomous turns. The published benchmark table concedes several coding rows to Claude Fable 5, which posts 80.0 on SWE-bench Pro against Qwen3.8-Max's 67.7, while claiming leads on PaperBench at 93.0 and across much of the multimodal suite. Many of those scores came from running Qwen inside the Claude Code harness, and QwenCloud exposes an Anthropic-compatible API so developers can do the same.

The release extends the trillion-parameter open-weight contest Moonshot AI joined with Kimi K3, whose 2.8-trillion-parameter weights shipped on July 27, The Decoder reports. Alibaba previewed Qwen3.8-Max on July 19 at the World AI Conference in Shanghai, two days after Moonshot's announcement; MarkTechPost relayed Hacker News threads that read the timing as a direct response and questioned how anyone serves a 2.4-trillion-parameter model when a single H200 GPU carries 141GB of memory. The Decoder adds that Alibaba's numbers await independent verification, and that independent testing of Kimi K3 fell well short of top Western models on cybersecurity and mathematics.

Reaction on the announcement thread gathered around the small model as much as the flagship. Unsloth AI replied "We can't wait to make quants so everyone can run it locally!", the thread's most-liked response, while other replies asked why the comparison charts omitted Kimi K3. The South China Morning Post reports that the same day brought QwenWork, Alibaba's workplace agent platform, into public beta against Tencent's WorkBuddy, Moonshot's Kimi Work, Claude Cowork, and ChatGPT Work, folding the model launch into a broader contest over agent products on both sides of the Pacific.
