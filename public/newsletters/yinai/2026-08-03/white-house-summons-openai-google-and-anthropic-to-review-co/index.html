White House convenes OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic staffers to review the pre-release model framework

The oversight framework staffers will review at the White House grew out of a June executive order, a 19-day suspension of Anthropic's Fable and Mythos models, and a gated GPT-5.6 release; the 85-person cyber office now holding the file faces doubts about its authority.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 3 August 2026 499 words · ~2 min

According to The Information's Leo Schwartz and Stephanie Palazzolo, the Office of the National Cyber Director will host Tuesday's session, the companies will send staff-level representatives, and the invitation described a discussion of next steps. A White House official said Monday that the framework, a voluntary procedure for labs to submit models to the government before release, was finished by its August 1 deadline, without saying whether it has taken effect. The administration shared an earlier draft with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google in late July, and companies were still pressing for specific language on open-source models, the class of release Alibaba is advertising for its 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen3.8-Max. Sam Altman discussed the framework in Washington last week with National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross and Office of Science and Technology Policy Director Michael Kratsios.

The deadline traces to Executive Order 14409, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," signed June 2, which left the covered-model definition to a classified benchmarking process. By Norton Rose Fulbright's account, Treasury, the Department of War through the NSA, and Homeland Security through CISA had 60 days to design a framework under which participating developers give the government access to covered frontier models, those with advanced cyber capabilities, for up to 30 days before release. The order disclaims any mandatory licensing or pre-clearance mechanism and never says what a company gains by volunteering.

The Center for AI Safety's newsletter reconstructed the two interventions that preceded the framework. Anthropic released Fable 5 on June 9 as the public version of its withheld Mythos tier; on June 12, after a jailbreak reportedly let Fable search for cyber vulnerabilities, the government ordered both models restricted for non-citizens, and Anthropic, unable to filter access by citizenship, suspended the models entirely. Al Jazeera reported that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's June 30 letter lifting the restrictions required Anthropic to detect security risks proactively, work with the government on standards for future models, and report malicious activity. OpenAI, at the government's request, previewed GPT-5.6 on June 26 with a small group of trusted partners, an arrangement it described as short-term. When TechCrunch asked in July how the government decided GPT-5.6 was safe to release, Altman named Lutnick, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and Cairncross as his interlocutors; Mina Narayanan of Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology and Dean W. Ball both said outsiders cannot see the testing or the requirements.

Lawfare examined the office now holding the file. Congress created it in 2021 to coordinate cyber policy; it runs on 85 full-time staff, its authority depends on presidential delegation, and Bessent reportedly pressed for aggressive action on AI because Cairncross moved too slowly. Tuesday's meeting also lands while the Senate's frontier-model bill sits stalled over how much review power the federal government should hold, a question the voluntary framework now answers in practice before Congress does. The framework reviews models before release; the synthetic newscasts YouTube removed and the AI-generated melodramas X's revenue-sharing program pays creators for show what circulates afterward.
