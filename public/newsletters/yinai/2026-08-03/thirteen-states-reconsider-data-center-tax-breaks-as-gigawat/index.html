Texas targets repeal and New York pauses permits as states rethink data-center incentives

Abbott wants Texas's 2013 exemptions repealed in 2027, Virginia's auditor priced its tax break at $6.10 of labor income per dollar, and New York has paused large data-center permits outright.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 3 August 2026 496 words · ~2 min

In The Information's AI Infrastructure newsletter on August 2, Ann Davis Vaughan sets out the arithmetic: a gigawatt-scale campus needs roughly $40 billion of IT equipment, against $19 billion for land, buildings and on-site power; operators replace the chips about every five years; and a 7 percent sales tax adds nearly $3 billion per gigawatt. Meta would not commit to its multi-gigawatt Louisiana site until the state passed an exemption.

In Texas, Governor Greg Abbott on June 10 asked the Legislature's 2027 session to "repeal sales tax exemptions and other outdated or unnecessary incentives for data centers" and directed the Public Utility Commission to act by July 31 to cut residential transmission costs and make data centers pay their own grid-infrastructure bills, the Texas Tribune reported; the exemptions, in place since 2013, are projected to cost the state $3.2 billion in sales tax revenue over two years. JLL expects Texas to pass Virginia as the world's largest data center market by 2030. Dan Diorio of the Data Center Coalition told The Information that after an abrupt repeal "your entire business plan is in flux".

In Virginia, the legislature's Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission rated the exemption the state's second-most productive incentive, returning $6.10 of labor income per exemption dollar, Cardinal News reported in June; the same accounting shows the state forgoing about $1.9 billion a year, revenue Senate Finance Chair Louise Lucas wants captured. Diorio cites that report, alongside a PwC study his coalition commissioned, as proof the benefits outrun the foregone revenue.

Louisiana previews the conditional approach: Governor Jeff Landry's June 25 executive order, JML-26-058, requires data-center companies to "fully fund" their electricity needs to continue receiving the state's tax breaks. Ratepayer advocates doubt it, WAFB reports: Public Service Commissioner Davante Lewis wants the "Lightning Amendment" utility fast-track eliminated, and the Alliance for Affordable Energy asked whether the framework has "any teeth in it at all". State-policy firm MultiState adds Illinois, where Governor J.B. Pritzker paused incentives from July 1 and wants ratepayer and water rules added in the fall veto session, and describes Arizona's pause, running to mid-2029, as a compromise after Governor Katie Hobbs pushed for outright elimination.

New York went furthest: its Legislature passed the Responsible Data Center Development Act on June 4, by 44 to 16 in the Senate and 102 to 39 in the Assembly, imposing a one-year permit moratorium on data centers drawing 20 megawatts or more, a statewide environmental impact report, and renewable-power requirements ramping to 90 percent by 2040, per law firm DLA Piper. On July 14 Governor Kathy Hochul launched her own version by executive order: environmental regulators pause permits for large data centers for up to a year while the state writes a Generic Environmental Impact Statement and community-benefit guidance. Vaughan calls a moratorium "a worse alternative"; NCSL's Nicholas Miller reads repeal threats as leverage, negotiations about "weighing the cost and benefits and making sure communities get a good deal".
