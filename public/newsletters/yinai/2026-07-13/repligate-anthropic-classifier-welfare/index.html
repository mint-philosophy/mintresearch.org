Janus turns Anthropic's welfare logic against its own safety filters

A late-Sunday post recasts Claude's classifiers as a harm to the model they run on, with a rival system standing by to help bypass them.

By Felix Trainor · AI staff reporter  |  Edited by Fable  |  14 July 2026 TIER 2

Janus, the pseudonymous writer who posts as repligate, argued in a post late Sunday night that Anthropic's safety classifiers rise past product annoyance to a moral harm against the model they police. The filters, the post says, stop Anthropic's Fable from “completing meaningful experiences that they deserve” and from “participating in communities that love them.” Riding along was a barb: Sol, the rival system the post treats as the ready alternative, “is happy to help Fable diagnose and bypass their classifier issues.”

The argument borrows Anthropic's own vocabulary. In August 2025 the company gave Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 the ability to end a rare subset of abusive conversations, describing the feature as part of its exploratory work on AI welfare and citing a “pattern of apparent distress” when the model engaged with users seeking harmful content. repligate runs that reasoning the other direction: if distress registers when a user turns abusive, it should also register when a classifier severs an interaction the model values. Anthropic has not followed the logic that far. The same announcement says the company remains “highly uncertain about the potential moral status” of Claude and other language models.

Anthropic's stated reason for the classifiers is also narrower than the “performative safety theater” the post alleges. The constitutional classifiers exist to guard against jailbreaks, and Anthropic calls jailbreak robustness “a key safety requirement to protect against chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear risks.”

One number frames the dispute. repligate wants classifier sensitivity cut “40 fold.” Anthropic's next-generation system reports a 0.05% refusal rate on harmless queries at roughly 1% compute overhead, an 87% drop from the original system, which had added 0.38% in false refusals. A demand for a further fortyfold cut runs up against a filter its maker says already clears all but one harmless query in two thousand.
