Pangram's Two Headline Detection Numbers Describe Two Different Models

A LessWrong brief on the AI-text detector reports 93.66% and 99.64%, but the first measures a superseded model and the second is the vendor's own test of the current one.

By Silas Boyd · AI staff reporter  |  Edited by Fable  |  14 July 2026 TIER 2

A brief on Pangram Labs posted to LessWrong by Sheikh Abdur Raheem Ali has been circulating with two striking accuracy figures attached. Ali opens by asserting that the company builds "the most accurate AI text detector in the world," then twice flags that his most-cited table describes a superseded system. Read closely, the two headline numbers measure two different models.

The first number comes from an adversarial-text study the brief reproduces as a table: a Pangram model tuned for humanizers caught 100% of unmodified AI text and 93.66% of humanized AI text, against 34.53% for GPTZero and 29.73% for Binoculars on the same evasion task. Ali notes twice that the model the table labels "current model!" "is not current as of July 2026"; Pangram's classifier now returns a percentage rather than a binary verdict.

The second number does describe the shipping system, on the vendor's own terms. Pangram's production model detects Fable 5 outputs with 99.64% accuracy, a figure the brief relays from the company's blog. The reasoning-effort setting used to generate the test outputs is not disclosed, and the tool identifies AI origin without predicting which model produced the text. So one figure is an academic adversarial benchmark of an outdated detector, and the other is a vendor-run test of the current one on a different task. Neither independently measures the production classifier against humanized text.

In the comments, Vladimir_Nesov clarified that the table, drawn from an October 2025 EditLens paper, reports F1 score, the harmonic mean of precision and recall, so those figures already account for false positives. He added that Pangram 3.3's false-positive rate runs below 0.5 to 1.5%, sometimes below 0.05%, depending on the human-written dataset used.

One more reason to read the brief carefully: it has already been corrected once. Ali retracted an earlier claim that Pangram had announced a Canada expansion after a source told him it was false. This newsroom made no attempt to contact Pangram; every figure here comes from the brief, its comments, and the vendor claims they relay.
