A Liberty-Coded Objection to Plan A, Minus the Counterproposal

Jason Crawford amplifies Séb Krier's case against centralized compute controls and asks for a polycentric alternative that, by his own account, has yet to be written.

By Ruth Vandermeer · AI staff reporter  |  Edited by Fable  |  14 July 2026 TIER 2

Jason Crawford used a Sunday post to amplify Séb Krier's objection to the centralizing turn in AI governance, and to ask for something that does not yet exist: a worked-out alternative.

Crawford quotes Krier describing a regime in which "a cadre of elites decides which research directions are permissible," caps global compute and robotics, and creates "state-administered scarcity rents." The excerpt ends with Krier calling it dangerous to build an apparatus whose whole job is entrenching government control over research and technology. Against that, Crawford says he would like to see a "what to do" proposal grounded in "polycentric, competitive, or distributed institutions," or in Vitalik Buterin-style d/acc principles.

Crawford's post never names its target outright, but the object is recognizably Plan A: Krier's quoted language about capping global compute and robotics tracks that scenario's machinery, and Charbel-Raphaël's alignment-forum essay records that the AI Futures Project published "AI 2040: Plan A" this past week. That essay argues the binding constraint on AI safety is political will and enforcement capacity, citing Ryan Greenblatt's tentative estimate that conditional takeover risk falls from roughly 45 percent in today's low-effort world to roughly 7 percent under a strong international agreement with real enforcement. Crawford's quarrel is with the instrument; his request asks decentralized institutions to carry the load that scenario assigns to a central authority.

No such counterproposal is on the table. Crawford frames his post as a request and does not say whether he or Krier intends to write the alternative. He adds a caveat that cuts both ways: a scenario, he writes, "is not an argument."

Krier's quoted post, as excerpted in Crawford's, reads simply "Concepts of a Plan," a title that does its own editorializing; the full post was not available for review, so whose plan it needles is left open. Roughly eight hours after posting, Crawford's request had drawn 22 likes and no reposts, a quiet landing.
