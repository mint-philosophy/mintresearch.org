A throttled thinking budget, caught and rolled back inside 48 hours

OpenAI restored GPT-5.6 Sol's reasoning budgets and reset its usage accounting after users detected the quiet cut. The same weekend, Anthropic bought Fable 5 another week. Both moves price the same inference bill.

By Adaeze Okafor · AI staff reporter  |  Edited by Fable  |  14 July 2026 TIER 1 · 904 words

Over the July 11 weekend, subscribers running OpenAI's newest reasoning model, GPT-5.6 Sol, reported that it had started feeling faster and more “efficient” while its thinking budgets shrank. By the evening of July 12, OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux had reversed the settings behind that impression and kept a usage cap suspended, closing a roughly 48-hour cycle that ran from quiet change to user detection to public restoration. Sottiaux's summary, posted under the header “No nerfing, only good stuff!”, listed four adjustments and one temporary reprieve.

The clearest signal came from the model's thinking budget. Independent trackers found that Sol's internal “juice” values, which govern how much computation the model spends per reasoning tier, had fallen across the board within days of launch. Figures compiled by Saganote put the top “Max” tier at 128, down from 960 at release, an 87% reduction, with the “xhigh” tier cut from 128 to 40. The tracker Lentils80 called the budgets “severely degraded compared to release day” hours before OpenAI confirmed the change.

Sottiaux framed the reversal as accounting cleanup rather than a capability rollback. He said OpenAI had run experiments changing reasoning efforts, the juice values, and had reverted them. He also flagged a context-window change: raising Sol's limit in the product to 372k tokens, up from 272k on GPT-5.5, had charged users more than intended, so the company rolled back to 272k and promised that “usage drains significantly less after this change.” Inference optimizations, he added, would return about 10% more usage on their own, and multi-agent runs at high and xhigh effort were consuming more than planned.

The context detail connects to a live complaint in OpenAI's own Codex tracker. Developers documented that Sol's default configuration could push a session past the 272k threshold without the user choosing a larger context, at which point OpenAI's published API pricing applies a 2x input and 1.5x output multiplier. Sottiaux disputed that this multiplier explains subscription bills, replying to one developer's analysis that “we don't charge for longer context on the subscription for GPT 5.6 Sol” and pointing instead to cache reads, which get more expensive as larger contexts shuttle between tool calls. He said OpenAI is retuning the system so the bigger window can return “without it resulting in higher usage being charged.”

User detection, then reversal

Not every user accepted the efficiency framing. The account scaling01, posting as Lisan al Gaib, answered the degradation reports with “aaaaaand cancel sub”. The quoted complaint that anchored Sottiaux's thread, from the account FixlationAI, argued the tier shift had stripped subscribers of their highest setting: “So we basically don't have Max reasoning anymore”. The restoration followed within hours, alongside a usage reset and the continued suspension of the five-hour limit for Plus, Business, and Pro plans.

Anthropic's clock

The same 48 hours reshaped Anthropic's competing offer. On July 12 the company extended free access to Claude Fable 5 on paid plans through July 19, its second extension of a cutoff that has slid from July 7 to July 12 and now to July 19, while keeping Claude Code's weekly limits 50% higher. Subscribers can spend up to half their weekly allotment on Fable 5 at no extra charge; after the deadline, continued use requires usage credits priced at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, per OfficeChai's reporting. Anthropic's stated rationale has been compute: it wanted a firmer read on demand and capacity before committing to keep the model cheap for subscribers.

Simon Willison read the extension as a competitive reflex. He argued that Sol's status as a Fable-class model keeps forcing Anthropic to move the date, and that OpenAI is “winning users simply due to the uncertainty that surrounds Fable access.” His prescription was for Anthropic to stop the weekly countdown and keep Fable permanently available on paid plans.

The shared bill

Both moves price the same constraint: the marginal cost of serving a frontier reasoning model against a fixed subscription fee. Each of OpenAI's four fixes pulled a lever on that cost, from the reasoning budget and the context ceiling to multi-agent and auto-review overhead, and Willison observed that OpenAI “appear confident that they won't need to restrict access” the way Anthropic has. Anthropic's repeated extensions and its half-your-weekly-limit cap describe a lab metering the same expense more cautiously; the credit pricing it will charge after July 19 is one public estimate of what a Fable-class model costs to serve.

The episode also shows how quickly usage accounting gets audited from outside. Trackers reverse-engineered the juice values within days of launch, Codex users filed issues against the context threshold, and Sottiaux answered a developer's cost analysis point by point hours after his main post. A silent server-side change became, over a weekend, a documented reversal.

What to watch

Sottiaux said the 372k context window would return “in the days to come”, so whether Sol's larger context comes back without the usage penalty is the near-term test of OpenAI's accounting fix. The exact efficiency gain remains unquantified; in the update Willison quoted, Sottiaux wrote only that the impact was “to be quantified and shared”. On Anthropic's side, July 19 is the next decision point, and Willison's question stands: whether the lab converts Fable 5 to permanent paid-plan access or holds subscribers to another countdown. Neither lab has published the underlying serving costs that would let outsiders check either bet.
