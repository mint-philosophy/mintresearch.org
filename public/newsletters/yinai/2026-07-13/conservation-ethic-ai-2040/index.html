AI 2040 Preserves 99% of Earth. Who Signs the Deal?

A LessWrong critic asks how the scenario's untouched preserves get negotiated, funded, and defended by institutions that do not yet exist.

By Iris Calloway · AI staff reporter  |  Edited by Fable  |  14 July 2026 TIER 2

The AI 2040 scenario buries a large claim in a small paragraph, and the writer cdt stopped to read it closely. By 2036, the scenario splits the planet into industrial special economic zones, arcologies, and preserves that cover 99% of the world, where Yosemite, Paris, and New York look about as they did in 2025, only with more tourists.

cdt sets that figure against the present. Roughly 18.43% of the globe is protected today, governments are already straining toward the 30%-by-2030 target, and the scenario promises more than triple the current share without saying how it gets there. Its own explanation, that a space-based robot economy lifts pressure off Earth, strikes cdt as optimistic at best.

cdt examines two mechanisms. Under economic abandonment, people drift toward arcologies as ordinary settlements stop paying, but buildings, trails, and ecosystems decay without labor and monitoring, so the result is rewilding rather than preservation. Under negotiated conservation, someone deliberately relocates residents and maintains the landscape, a project cdt sizes at the scale of Plan A itself. "I don't know of a current organisation that could step in here," cdt writes.

cdt doubts advanced AI closes the gap. Much conservation knowledge is local, undigitised, and guarded by communities that resist outsiders, and physical preservation competes for resources against development that keeps getting cheaper. Commenter AnthonyC pushes back that land stays abundant: he does not imagine "AGI or early ASI needing more than 1% of the planet's surface", and suggests automation could supply the maintenance labor.

In the comments, cdt flags a contradiction: arcologies are designed to "escape planning law", yet strong property rights are elsewhere offered as the safeguard protecting people through takeoff. The piece sits beside Charbel-Raphaël's argument that political will, not research, is the binding constraint. Both locate the missing machinery in institutions nobody has built.
