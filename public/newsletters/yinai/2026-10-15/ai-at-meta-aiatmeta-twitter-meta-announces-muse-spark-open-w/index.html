Meta releases Muse Spark Open under a new commercial licence

Developers can run and modify the model locally. Larger model-service businesses need a separate agreement, and stronger reported safeguards come with more refusals of harmless requests.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 15 October 2026 775 words · ~4 min

Meta released Muse Spark Open on October 15, making the weights of an 816-billion-parameter model available for developers to run and modify. Built for coding and agents that work through multiple steps, it accepts text, images, video and audio, produces text, and has a million-token context window. Meta calls it “the most capable US open-weight model to date”. The release combines local deployment options with a new licence and additional safety training for a model whose users can change its weights.

The announcement follows Mark Zuckerberg’s August commitment to release Spark weights, reported by CNBC. Meta continues to reserve its strongest models for proprietary services: Alexandr Wang told CNBC at launch that the open model trails Muse Wonder, and described it as a way for developers to experiment before expanding their usage. The October release documents identify Muse Spark Open as an open version of Spark without specifying an earlier numbered checkpoint.

Only 42 billion parameters participate in generating each token, but the model’s full set of weights still needs memory. Meta’s deployment guide describes running a compressed version on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with 512 GB each, or four machines with 256 GB each. Thunderbolt 5 connects them, and Apple’s MLX software divides the model and computation across machines. Meta demonstrated entirely local agent workflows, including changes to the Ice Cubes iOS app and video editing in DaVinci Resolve. Hugging Face also reports running a more heavily compressed version on a single 512 GB M3 Ultra, illustrating how storing weights at lower precision can reduce memory requirements.

Developers can also use hosted services, including OpenRouter and Together AI, or obtain weights through the major cloud model catalogues. Download access on Hugging Face requires approval and a form requesting a full legal name, date of birth and organisation. The Muse Model License permits fine-tuning, distillation and redistribution, requires the notice “Built with Muse Spark Open”, and incorporates a usage policy with restrictions including military, nuclear and espionage uses.

A separate commercial condition applies to model services that give third parties model outputs through an API or similar interface. If the licensee or its affiliates provide such services and their aggregate gross revenue exceeds $20 million during any consecutive 12 months, the licensee must notify Meta and obtain a separate written agreement before offering Spark or its derivatives through that business. The calculation uses the group’s overall revenue. Internal use, research and other commercial activities that are not model services are exempt from this additional requirement, including products that use Spark to power features without themselves offering model services. Meta’s earlier Llama 3.1 licence required separate permission above 700 million monthly active users in the month before its release.

Meta’s published comparisons show strengths in professional tasks and tool use, alongside weaker results than GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 on several coding tests. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, which evaluates tasks completed through a computer terminal, its table gives Spark Open 76.4, compared with 83.9 for GLM and 85.0 for Kimi. The evaluation methodology identifies Artificial Analysis as the source for these three results, using the same Terminus 2 agent setup. Other rows combine official leaderboards, Meta’s own tests and providers’ self-reports. Meta also says its tools and prompts were not specifically tuned for competing models. The table therefore needs to be read benchmark by benchmark; it does not establish a single overall ranking.

Meta says Spark Open met the release thresholds in its Superintelligence Scaling Framework after additional safety training, with a fuller evaluation report forthcoming. Its tests include StrongREJECT v2, an internal adaptation of a public benchmark that probes 202 prohibited behaviours with several kinds of attack, including an adaptive attacker that can continue the conversation. In the reported FORTRESS comparison, Spark Open gave the least harmful responses among the models listed but also refused the most harmless requests: 17.4% of benign prompts, against 2.7% for Kimi K3. Meta’s methodology defines that over-refusal measure explicitly; the result describes a cost to usefulness alongside the stronger safeguards.

People holding weights can retrain refusal behaviour away, as Meta acknowledges in its October 2 framework update. Xiangyu Qi and colleagues demonstrated the vulnerability in the 2023 arXiv paper Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!: ten adversarial training examples were enough to compromise GPT-3.5 Turbo’s safeguards. Rishub Tamirisa and colleagues’ arXiv paper Tamper-Resistant Safeguards for Open-Weight LLMs, first posted in 2024, developed protections that survived hundreds of fine-tuning steps in their tests. Meta’s release materials evaluate the released checkpoint and warn that modifications can degrade safety; they do not report whether Spark Open’s safeguards survived adversarial fine-tuning.
