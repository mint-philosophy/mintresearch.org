Moonshot AI unveils Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open model

The company calls K3 the world's first open 3T-class model, with a one-million-token context and full weights due July 27; its own benchmark table puts K3 near the proprietary leaders it says it still trails.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 16 July 2026 423 words · ~2 min

Moonshot AI introduced Kimi K3 on July 16, billing it "Open Frontier Intelligence" in a post from the company's @Kimi_Moonshot account on X. The model carries 2.8 trillion parameters, a one-million-token context window, and native multimodal input, with full weights promised by July 27, 2026. A companion post on the Kimi blog calls K3 "the world's first open 3T-class model". Two architecture changes carry the scale. Kimi Delta Attention, the company says, enables up to 6.3 times faster decoding in million-token contexts; Attention Residuals raise training efficiency by roughly 25% at under 2% extra cost. A Stable LatentMoE design activates 16 of 896 experts, and Moonshot puts the combined gain at about 2.5 times the scaling efficiency of Kimi K2.

Moonshot writes on the blog that overall performance "still trails the most powerful proprietary models, Claude Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol," while claiming frontier-level results across its own suite. The published benchmark table, which reports K3's results at maximum reasoning effort, bears that out unevenly. K3 leads on several agentic and coding tests, scoring 42.0 on SWE Marathon against 35.0 for Fable 5 and 39.0 for GPT 5.6 Sol, and 91.2 on BrowseComp. It trails on others, posting 43.5 on the HLE-Full reasoning test to Fable 5's 53.3, and a GDPval-AA v2 Elo of 1668 to Fable 5's 1760. On GPQA-Diamond the six compared models bunch between 91.0 and 94.1.

Moonshot rests much of the announcement on long-horizon agent demonstrations. It describes K3 building MiniTriton, a compact Triton-like GPU compiler with its own IR layer over MLIR and a PTX code-generation path, and, in a separate 48-hour autonomous run, designing a chip for a nano model built on K3's own architecture. Within 4 mm², the blog reports, that chip closes timing at 100 MHz on the Nangate 45nm library and sustains over 8,700 tokens per second of decode throughput in simulation. In one research case, the company says, K3 reproduced the I-Love-Q universal relations in computational astrophysics in about two hours, reviewing more than 20 papers and evaluating over 300 equations of state.

Kimi K3 is live now on Kimi.com, the Kimi Work desktop app, Kimi Code, and the Kimi API; API pricing runs $0.30 per million tokens for cache-hit input, $3.00 for cache-miss input, and $15.00 for output. Moonshot's blog closes with the model's rough edges: "excessive proactiveness" when user intent is ambiguous, unstable output if a harness fails to feed back K3's own thinking history, and "a noticeable gap in user experience" against Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol.
