Faraday's 27B controller directs GPT-5.5 Codex on paper replications

A Qwen3.6-27B controller trained with turn-level credit on Replica beat Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 Codex on held-out replication tasks; the imagined-task result relies on a judge that was not validated for those variants.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 15 August 2026 252 words · ~2 min

Inherent introduced Faraday on August 14 as a 27-billion-parameter agent trained to replicate research papers. The companion arXiv paper, "Training AI Scientists to Replicate Research" by Damon Falck, Samer Sabri, and nine Inherent colleagues, evaluates the agent in Replica, a task space of 310 tasks drawn from 100 machine-learning and AI-for-science papers. The authors treat replication as a constrained scientific-reasoning test because agents must recover experimental details that published figures and methods leave implicit.

Each task gives the agent a paper with a results figure removed and 60 minutes on one GPU to recreate it. An automatically generated rubric, applied by a judge built on GPT-5.5 Codex, supplies the reward; in 117 rankings by 20 PhD-level researchers, the rubric judge tracked the human rankings better than a fixed-prompt baseline. Inherent post-trained Qwen3.6-27B with a turn-level-credit variant of GRPO. Faraday learned mainly while directing GPT-5.4 mini as its coding tool, then used GPT-5.5 Codex for the reported test runs.

Against Claude Opus 4.8 in Claude Code and GPT-5.5 in Codex, with eight rollouts per task, Faraday won on 73 percent of in-distribution machine-learning tasks and 60 percent of held-out AI-for-science tasks. It averaged 6 percent above Claude and 8 percent above Codex on the test split, although its untrained Qwen base had been the weakest agent in the comparison. On 20 imagined variants whose target results appeared in none of the source papers, the judge preferred Faraday to Codex on 19; the authors caution that the judge was never validated on imagined tasks.
