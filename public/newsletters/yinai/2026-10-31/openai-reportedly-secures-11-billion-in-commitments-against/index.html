OpenAI seeks more investors after $11 billion in commitments

The Journal reports an unfinished raise below its original $30 billion target. CNBC confirms a revised goal of up to $20 billion, while reported valuation bases differ.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 31 October 2026 553 words · ~3 min

OpenAI has secured roughly $11 billion in commitments for a funding round initially intended to raise $30 billion, Berber Jin reported in the Wall Street Journal on October 30, citing people familiar with the matter. His account puts the company's valuation at $1.25 trillion, below the $1.4 trillion it had hoped to achieve. Some large investors showed limited interest amid a technology selloff, he reports. OpenAI is continuing to seek commitments, so the figures describe an unfinished financing; they do not establish how much cash has reached the company.

Jin reports that OpenAI told shareholders it would approach family offices and wealthy individuals over the coming weeks. One source said the company now hoped to raise up to $20 billion in total. Ashley Capoot and Lora Kolodny independently confirmed that revised goal for CNBC on October 31. Their source said the round remained in progress and had not closed. OpenAI did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment. The Journal credits the Financial Times with first reporting the reduction in the target.

CNBC's earlier reporting helps separate the changes in the amount sought and the valuation. On October 22, Kate Rooney and Ashley Capoot reported that OpenAI was seeking up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation excluding the new capital. That was a pre-money figure. CNBC's October 31 article attributes a post-money valuation of $1.25 trillion to the FT; post-money includes the investment. Jin's article does not specify the basis. These accounts support a reduced fundraising target, but do not establish an exact, consistently measured decline in valuation or final share terms.

OpenAI's own March 31 financing announcement provides the completed-round comparison: $122 billion in committed capital at an $852 billion post-money valuation. The current reported valuation remains above that earlier figure, despite falling short of the company's later ambition. March also brought more than $3 billion from individual investors through bank channels, which OpenAI described as its first such participation. The planned outreach to wealthy investors therefore extends a channel the company has already used. It does not, by itself, establish that institutional investors have exhausted their capacity to invest.

Jin places the financing alongside OpenAI's preparations for a possible public listing as soon as early 2027 and its effort to accelerate revenue growth. He reports that second-quarter sales rose about 18% to $6.7 billion, while Anthropic's more than doubled to $11.6 billion. Anthropic's public IPO filing provides related context for that competition. Jin also notes third-party evidence that OpenAI had recently regained some market share after reducing prices on its latest models. The sales comparison concerns the second quarter; the reported share gains are more recent.

The Journal's August 18 financial report, by Jin and Corrie Driebusch, explains the expectations behind the revenue comparison. OpenAI had signed computing agreements premised on much higher future sales, and some shareholders were disappointed by its pace of growth. The company told investors growth had accelerated following July model launches. The article also reports that OpenAI's operating loss widened from $9.3 billion in the first quarter to $12.3 billion in the second, including stock-based compensation.

Jin attributes the weaker fundraising response partly to investors becoming more selective after disappointing earnings at Google, Amazon and Intel. The Journal discloses that its owner, News Corp, has a content-licensing partnership with OpenAI.
