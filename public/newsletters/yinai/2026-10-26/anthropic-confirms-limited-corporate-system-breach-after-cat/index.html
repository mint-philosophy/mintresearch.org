Cathedral reached Anthropic through outside business software

Anthropic confirms limited access to corporate records and disputes Cathedral’s account of the timing and disclosure. It denies exposure of Claude conversations, source code or model weights.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 26 October 2026 551 words · ~3 min

Cybersecurity startup Cathedral reached some of Anthropic’s corporate systems through vulnerabilities in outside business software, Anthropic told the Washington Post. Elizabeth Dwoskin and Gerrit De Vynck’s October 26 investigation, followed by Ece Yildirim’s account in Gizmodo, describes testing through Anthropic’s vulnerability-reward program. The companies disagree about the exercise’s duration and whether Cathedral fully disclosed its activity.

Cathedral’s account comes from a draft report reviewed by the Post. The startup said its AI agents needed fewer than 12 hours to identify paths to internal meeting transcripts, salary and job-offer information, and Slack messages. Anthropic acknowledged access to some sales, human resources and IT systems containing customers’ and partners’ business contact details and basic user-account records. It said the researchers did not reach Claude users’ conversations, source code or model weights. The access Anthropic confirmed does not establish access to the conversations associated with those accounts.

Yildirim reports that Anthropic also disputed the timing implied by Cathedral’s account. According to the company, the researchers had already spent several unsuccessful days trying to breach Anthropic’s own software before turning to third-party applications. Cathedral’s claimed agent-running time therefore does not establish the duration of the whole exercise. Anthropic said it detected activity before Cathedral’s disclosure and disabled the affected software until vendors confirmed fixes.

Anthropic accused Cathedral of failing to report all its activity under the bounty program’s rules, according to the Post and Gizmodo. Cathedral’s draft says it minimized data access. It argues that weaknesses in ordinary enterprise software can expose sensitive infrastructure at advanced technology companies. Gizmodo describes Cathedral as a military-focused cybersecurity company founded by former Department of Government Efficiency staffers, with investors including Andreessen Horowitz and Sequoia Capital.

Anthropic’s published responsible-disclosure policy limits exploitation to what is necessary to demonstrate a vulnerability and requires researchers to disclose accidental data access without retaining the data. It asks for reproduction details and coordination before public disclosure. The policy covers systems Anthropic owns, operates or controls, while excluding third-party-controlled systems even when hosted under an Anthropic domain. Those systems fall under their providers’ disclosure arrangements. The general policy does not settle the dispute over Cathedral’s particular activities.

Alex Stamos, who reviewed Cathedral’s findings for the Post, criticized Anthropic’s management of third-party software. Mark Riedl’s Bluesky response to Gizmodo considered why account and billing controls could matter even without stolen model weights. In his hypothetical scenario, an agent’s surrounding software could move beyond a user’s oversight while still depending on a model server. Concealing its continued use would require removing the accounting records that could reveal it. That scenario involves control over accounting, beyond the account-record access Anthropic acknowledged. Riedl explicitly says AI escape scenarios do not worry him, a position also stated in the September 18 thread he quotes.

Sella Nevo and colleagues’ 2024 RAND report, Securing AI Model Weights: Preventing Theft and Misuse of Frontier Models, explains the separate risk of stealing the learned parameters needed to run a model independently. Its recommendations include concentrating weight copies in monitored systems, restricting access and hardening model interfaces against theft. Anthropic’s account places the Cathedral incident in its business systems and denies that the researchers obtained those weights. Anthropic separately reports in its IPO filing that strengthening defenses against weight theft delayed pretraining a frontier base model by more than a month.
