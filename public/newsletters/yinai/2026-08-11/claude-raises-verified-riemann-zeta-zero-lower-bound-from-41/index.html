Claude’s zeta-zero bound passes two-thirds

A 35-page paper replaces hypothesis-dependent zero-side positivity with finite-dimensional linear algebra; mathematicians checked the argument and Lean formalizes its main theorems.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 11 August 2026 415 words · ~2 min

Anthropic published a 35-page paper credited to Claude, More Than Two Thirds of the Zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function Lie on the Critical Line, which proves an unconditional lower bound on the asymptotic share of nontrivial zeros lying on the critical line. Its optimized theorem puts that share above 67.25%, establishes the same lower bound for zeros that are simple and on the line, and raises the lower bound for distinct zeros to 83.625%. The previous unconditional critical-line record was five-twelfths, about 41.67%, established in 2020. Because the Riemann hypothesis places every nontrivial zero on the line, this result neither proves nor disproves it.

Claude begins with Hugh Montgomery’s 1973 pair-correlation method. Montgomery’s prime-side second-moment calculation was unconditional; the zero-side positivity used the Riemann hypothesis. The paper restricts Weil’s Hermitian form to a finite-dimensional family of test functions. Each on-line zero contributes a nonnegative rank-one form, and every symmetric off-line pair contributes a block with one positive and one negative direction. Sylvester’s law of inertia and a rank-trace inequality turn the form’s trace and Frobenius norm into a lower bound on on-line zeros. Optimizing the test-function window recovers the Montgomery-Taylor kernel and produces 0.6725007.

In An Unconditional Montgomery Theorem for Pair Correlation of Zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function, published in Acta Arithmetica in 2024, Siegfred Baluyot, Daniel Goldston, Ade Suriajaya and Caroline Turnage-Butterbaugh removed the hypothesis from Montgomery’s prime-side estimate. Goldston and Suriajaya’s 2026 arXiv paper Zeta Zeros in a Narrow Vertical Box derived a two-thirds conclusion under a narrow-box assumption. Claude combines that unconditional estimate with Enrico Bombieri’s 2000 paper Remarks on Weil’s Quadratic Functional in the Theory of Prime Numbers, I, replacing the remaining zero-side positivity assumption with finite-dimensional linear algebra.

Anthropic’s research account says an unreleased model worked across two Claude Code sessions, tried 650 unsuccessful ideas, then coordinated roughly 60 subagents for a day and a half while producing 31 million output tokens. Anthropic mathematicians Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman independently checked the argument; Brian Conrey and Goldston carefully read the manuscript and commented. The companion Lean repository formalizes Theorems A through E against Mathlib’s zeta function. Its audit records no placeholder proofs in the solutions and successful Comparator runs with the Nanoda kernel. An August 12 issue flags an incorrect equality example in Lemma 3.2’s wording and says the theorem uses the correct normalization. Stronger bounds through this method require prime-pair information beyond the available range; the method does not reach the full Riemann hypothesis.
