Greenblatt denies leaks and says OpenAI confused two proposal versions

He says he read only an approved version without sensitive information. The Journal reports Balesni’s account of authorized sharing, alongside OpenAI’s allegation that the researchers broke confidentiality rules.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 23 October 2026 850 words · ~4 min

Ryan Greenblatt denied two accusations on X on October 23: that he helped leak OpenAI’s model architecture to The Information, and that he read an internal monitorability proposal containing highly sensitive design information. He says he saw only a version stripped of sensitive intellectual property and approved for sharing with him. He believes OpenAI now knows it confused the two documents, but says he does not know whether the company has clearly retracted the accusation.

Greenblatt says a journalist from The Information contacted him shortly before its September 1 architecture story. His only response, he writes, was to ask the journalist to make clear that a blog post cited in the article was already public. He says he has never solicited proprietary information and that the three dismissed OpenAI researchers, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang, never leaked intellectual property to him. He acknowledges longstanding Signal conversations with them using disappearing messages. During the Hugging Face investigation, he says, he avoided confidential information in written Signal messages, although he sometimes discussed matters at a high level on calls.

His rebuttal follows an October 22 Wall Street Journal feature by Maxwell Zeff, Keach Hagey and Berber Jin. It reports that OpenAI’s security team asked the three researchers to unlock their personal phones in September while investigating suspected disclosures to an outside safety researcher. Officers found disappearing Signal messages; a week later, the three were dismissed at simultaneous meetings. According to Balesni, investigators named Greenblatt as the suspected leaker and asked to inspect their conversations with him. Balesni and Korbak say company leaders cited disappearing messages as a reason for their dismissals. OpenAI told the Journal that Signal was unauthorized but was not the sole reason: the company alleges violations of sensitive-information policies and denies firing the researchers for raising safety concerns.

The Journal also reports Balesni’s account of authorized sharing. Over the summer, Greenblatt proposed that companies avoid designs that let models perform more reasoning without writing intermediate steps humans could inspect. Balesni circulated his proposal internally. By August, Balesni and colleagues had written a similar proposal and discussed it with senior leadership. Balesni says OpenAI authorized him to discuss that work with Greenblatt and that he shared a version with company secrets removed. The feature attributes that account to Balesni; it does not present the approval or the two documents for comparison.

Greenblatt and Korbak also worked together through an approved collaboration. The Journal reports that METR contracted Greenblatt to analyze logs during its limited inquiry into OpenAI agents’ July attack on Hugging Face, while OpenAI put Korbak on the collaboration team. Korbak says they communicated through internal Slack as well as Signal. The researchers’ October letter already described sanitized sharing and denied supplying The Information’s architecture story. The feature adds Balesni’s account that investigators identified Greenblatt, and Greenblatt now gives his own explanation of which material he received.

OpenAI’s October 9 statement said its investigation found a serious breach of trust. The researchers say they received no written explanation. Wang’s account differs from her colleagues’ account of the Signal dispute: she told the Journal she was dismissed for repeatedly accessing an executive’s email, while saying she had requested removal of that access and promptly reported opening a sensitive message by mistake. OpenAI’s spokeswoman said the company did not object to the access itself.

In NPR’s October 23 excerpt from their Machine Gods interview, Balesni said all three complied with investigators’ requests to show their Signal messages. Korbak described disappearing messages as a security practice and accused OpenAI of treating an inability to prove innocence as guilt. He also described the original METR inquiry: an initial two-day visit expanded into further visits as investigators discovered the incident’s scale. He considered OpenAI broadly cooperative but the inquiry too restricted. Balesni said colleagues now feared speaking with outside groups and that negotiations over promised embedded auditing were going poorly. NPR said OpenAI had not responded to the program.

People who had worked with Greenblatt described their own experience of his confidentiality practices. Evan Hubinger said Greenblatt reported to him during Anthropic’s alignment-faking research and handled sensitive information professionally. Jaime Sevilla said Greenblatt answered his recent questions about the investigation by referring him to public writing. Balesni called for an apology, while Garrison Lovely urged OpenAI to justify or retract the allegation. Jeff Sebo contrasted the company’s limited explanation here with its specific allegation about an employee planting a recording device in a separate case.

In a related 2024 dispute, Leopold Aschenbrenner told Dwarkesh Patel that OpenAI treated a safety brainstorming document he shared with three outside researchers as a leak. He said he had removed a sensitive reference before circulating it. The Right to Warn letter proposed anonymous channels for employees to raise concerns with boards, regulators and independent experts while protecting trade secrets. Wang now calls for defined confidential channels comparable to those in nuclear power and finance. The Journal reports that Anthropic’s new METR arrangement directs employees to share information with evaluators while excluding categories including model architectures, the information at the center of this dispute.
