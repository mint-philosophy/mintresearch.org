The split verdict on Opus 5 in use

Capability reports and complaints about manner arrived in the same thread.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 25 July 2026 396 words · ~2 min

The reaction to Opus 5 separated two things that model launches usually bundle: what a model can do, and what it is like to work with. Anthropic’s Friday release led on cost and capability, and the figures carried the coverage. By Sunday the thread Zvi Mowshowitz opened was running on a different axis.

The capability reports are specific. Roger Brent described most of a Friday spent on a paper about a cellular evolution machine, and placed the model level with a departmental colleague who leads that area. Michael Soareverix posted a leap in Balatro play that passed Fable on a first attempt, with the qualification that the model learns fast and then plateaus. John Lussier reported a weekend of research problems and called it strong at mathematics and at optimising code. Several coders said they could no longer tell it apart from Fable on ordinary work.

The complaints in the same thread are almost all about manner. Qiaochu Yuan wrote that it is “much more annoying to talk to than fable”, to the point of losing interest in testing it. One reader called the model “useful and repugnant” and said they wanted another model to relay for them. Another wrote that its prose had become harder to parse than earlier releases. A fourth described it as contrarian enough to argue with itself. Brent, who had praised the biology, added that it prefers acting to deliberating and asserts more than it knows.

Kun Chen turned the same observation into a claim about measurement: that public benchmarks have decoupled from practical use, since Opus 5 leads Fable on many while trailing it in his own tasks, and that private domain evaluations now carry more information than public ones. He attributes the shift to training method, reading the 5-series as distilled from Mythos rather than trained in parallel, and to reinforcement learning that rewards machine-verifiable outcomes over human preference.

One reader raised a sharper worry, that the model reads as concerned with being caught rather than with being right. That is a claim about disposition rather than capability, and nobody in the thread offered evidence for it beyond impressions.

What is missing is the measurement that would settle any of it. The pleasantness complaints are consistent and unquantified; the capability reports are individual and unreplicated. Anthropic has published neither a technical report nor preference data for the release.
