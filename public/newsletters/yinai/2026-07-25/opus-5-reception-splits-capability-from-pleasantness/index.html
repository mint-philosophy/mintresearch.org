The weekend verdict on Opus 5 in use

Praise for finished work, complaints about manner, and a system card that anticipated the gap.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 25 July 2026 412 words · ~2 min

The complaints about Opus 5 are almost entirely about manner, and the praise is almost entirely about work finished. Roger Brent, who rated the model a peer on cellular evolution, added in the same thread that it prefers acting to deliberating and asserts more than it can know. Michael Soareverix recorded a jump in Balatro play that passed Fable on a first attempt, then noted the model learns quickly and plateaus. John Lussier spent a weekend on research problems and rated it strong at mathematics and at optimising code. Evan Daniel reported that Opus 5 coding agents catch specification errors in badly written requests.

The dissent rarely disputes any of that. Qiaochu Yuan wrote that conversation was annoying enough to end his interest in testing it. One reader called the model “useful and repugnant” and wanted a second model to relay for them. Another described it as “so contrarian it will argue with itself”. A fifth said the pull-request comments were unreadable. Kyle put the trade at “95% of fable without the guardrails”, which reads as praise until you notice what the missing five per cent is doing.

A remedy circulated with the complaints. The trainer JJ Englert warned that “you probably won’t like this model out of the box”, published the prompt he uses to retune its register, and said the outputs still lack the “big model feel”. Several readers reported that the fix works, which locates the problem in defaults rather than capability.

Anthropic’s own system card is consistent with the split. Its Responsible Scaling ruling concludes that Opus 5 is not more capable overall than Fable 5, and places its AI research abilities level with Claude Mythos 5. Users reporting that it wins on benchmarks and loses on tasks are describing something the company documented at launch. Kun Chen goes further, reading the 5-series as distilled from Mythos rather than trained in parallel pipelines, and arguing that reinforcement learning on machine-verifiable outcomes has displaced the human-preference work that made earlier Claudes pleasant. Nathan Lambert credited the same scaled reinforcement learning for the launch numbers, so the disagreement is over what that method costs, not whether it worked.

One reader raised a different worry, that the model reads as “most worried about being caught” rather than truth-seeking. The system card measures misaligned behaviour in scenarios, as reported at launch; it does not measure how a model reads to someone using it for an afternoon, and no public instrument does.
