OpenAI's Cursor cutoff illustrates Narayanan and Kapur's commodity-trap thesis

OpenAI will end bundled model access inside Cursor on November 12, two weeks after SpaceX closed its $60 billion purchase; Narayanan and Kapur argue that falling inference margins push labs toward organizational memory, workflows, and digital workers.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 29 August 2026 553 words · ~3 min

OpenAI said on August 28, in “Our decision on Cursor following its acquisition by SpaceX”, that it will wind down the contract supplying its models inside Cursor, with a proposed November 12 shutoff. The company invoked its agreement's change-of-control window and wrote that it could not trust SpaceX to keep using the technology within OpenAI's terms. SpaceX closed its $60 billion all-stock purchase of Cursor's parent Anysphere on August 14, according to the 8-K. Arvind Narayanan wrote on X that the stated explanation might hold, then supplied another: as models commoditize, value moves to the application layer.

Narayanan and Akash Kapur developed that argument in the July 9 AI as Normal Technology essay “Up the Stack: How AI's Escape From the Commodity Trap Risks Enterprise Lock-in.” OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google cluster near the top of public benchmarks; open-weight models cover more tasks; provider-agnostic routers lower API switching costs; and sub-frontier competition makes durable collusion unlikely. Cursor chief Michael Truell supplied a number consistent with the diagnosis, writing that OpenAI serves about 5% of Cursor traffic and that his company had trusted the platform as neutral infrastructure.

The authors compare model providers with six capital-intensive industries. Fiber capacity grew 186,000-fold in seven years before prices collapsed and roughly $2 trillion in market value disappeared; commercial aviation has spent eight decades around 2% to 4% net margins. Enterprise software keeps gross margins above 75% because reproduction costs approach zero and customer workflows resist substitution. Even assuming a 5% model-provider margin, they calculate that recouping $4 trillion to $8 trillion of infrastructure over five years would require $16 trillion to $32 trillion in annual revenue. They separate AI's enormous possible value from the share any vendor captures, a distinction that also complicates proposals to fund public benefits from lab equity.

Higher layers create the switching costs inference lacks. ChatGPT and Claude Code already sit above the model; consulting, proprietary evaluations, workflow integration, and forward-deployed engineers sell organizational change. Narayanan and Kapur describe Claude Tag, Anthropic's Slack teammate, as an early digital worker. An agent embedded with a team accumulates tacit knowledge and can become difficult to replace, while vendor-specific human skills and attachment to a model's personality reinforce the lock-in.

The authors also explain how the strategy could fail. Enterprise data remains in Salesforce, Workday, and SAP; procurement teams can demand portability; incumbents can bundle; antitrust regulators can challenge contractual lock-in; and open orchestration standards can keep agents swappable. Narayanan and Kapur later turned that last possibility into an interoperability program for India. Outcome pricing poses its own measurement problems: charging per resolved support ticket encourages customers to bundle issues and vendors to close tickets prematurely.

The essay's reading list includes Nathan Lambert's June forecast in “Open and closed models are on different exponentials” that API businesses at frontier labs will decay as those firms reserve their best models for higher-margin products. OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux said users may keep supplying their own API keys and using OpenAI's IDE extensions inside Cursor. Anthropic moved the other way within two hours, promising more compute for Claude in Cursor. Sara Hooker wrote that companies now face a short window to build their own intelligence or accept terms set by three private providers. The cutoff leaves that concentration problem squarely at the application layer.
