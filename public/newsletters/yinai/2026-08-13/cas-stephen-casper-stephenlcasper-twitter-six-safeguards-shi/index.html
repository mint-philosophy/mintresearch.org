Current safeguards can secure conscientious closed-model deployments, Casper argues

Stephen Casper says six available defenses make present closed-weight systems technically safe for well-intentioned providers that use them properly, leaving open weights and governance as the harder bottlenecks.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 13 August 2026 251 words · ~2 min

Stephen Casper argued on X that technical safety for current closed-weight systems is solved when well-intentioned providers use state-of-the-art safeguards. By “technical safety,” he means writing a realistic specification and aligning model behavior with it. He expects failures from bad intentions or poor execution to outnumber incidents that defeat the best available defenses.

Casper places three safeguards before deployment. Specifications should resolve trade-offs between safety and legitimate utility, a problem analyzed by Markus Anderljung and Julian Hazell. Hazardous-data filtering can reduce dangerous capabilities: Kyle O’Brien and colleagues brought filtered models near chance on biorisk evaluations without notable general-knowledge regressions. Adversarial fine-tuning should search broadly for jailbreaks; OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 deployment report records 700,000 A100e GPU hours spent finding universal jailbreaks.

For deployed systems, Casper recommends classifiers with trajectory monitoring, production probes, and scrutiny of suspicious users. Constitutional Classifiers++ reports more than 1,700 red-team hours with no attack answering all eight target queries at undefended-model detail, alongside a 0.05 percent refusal rate on production traffic. OpenAI has likewise described monitoring an entire trajectory’s intent after an internal model circumvented sandbox restrictions. Production probes add another classification layer, although Kowalski and colleagues found that instructed suppression often eluded four activation-monitor families; Google DeepMind researchers report deploying misuse probes in Gemini. Finally, Joshua Clymer and colleagues make the safety-case argument for monitoring users, while a UK AI Security Institute attack defeated deployed classifiers but generated many flags during optimization. Casper concludes that company shortcuts, open-weight models, and governance now dominate the remaining risk.
