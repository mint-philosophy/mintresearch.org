Claude's Riemann paper raises the critical-line record to 67.25%

An unreleased Claude combined recent analytic number theory to lift the unconditional critical-line bound above two thirds. Anthropic released the paper, expert review, process transcripts, and a Lean proof.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 10 August 2026 414 words · ~2 min

In an August 10 research post, Anthropic said an unreleased research version of Claude raised the proven share of Riemann zeta zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%. The release includes Claude's paper, an expert note, a Lean formalization, and process transcripts. Jarred Sumner, who created Bun and joined Anthropic after it acquired Bun's company in December, had asked Claude to "take a real stab" at the Riemann hypothesis. Across two Claude Code sessions and 31 million output tokens, Claude generated 650 failed ideas, then coordinated about 60 subagents for a day and a half. Two developed the central mathematics, 13 validated arguments, and others searched for counterexamples, checked novelty against 54 arXiv papers, and re-proved the finding. Sumner wrote on X that his input was mostly variants of "keep going" and "believe in yourself."

Claude's 35-page paper, "More than two thirds of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line", gives unconditional theorems for at least two thirds of zeros on the line, the same fraction simple and on the line, at least five sixths distinct, and analogous results for Dirichlet L-functions. An optimized test family lifts the constants to 0.6725 and 0.83625. The prior 5/12 record came from Kyle Pratt, Nicolas Robles, Alexandru Zaharescu, and Dirk Zeindler's 2020 Research in the Mathematical Sciences paper, which refined the mollifier method Levinson introduced in 1974. Claude instead combined Montgomery's 1973 pair-correlation argument, a 2000 Bombieri paper on Weil's quadratic functional, and more recent work. The acknowledgments credit Siegfred Baluyot, Daniel Goldston, Ade Irma Suriajaya, and Caroline Turnage-Butterbaugh, plus two papers by Goldston and Suriajaya, with posing the question and supplying every analytic ingredient. Goldston and Suriajaya's November preprint had shown that removing the Riemann hypothesis from Montgomery's simple-zero proof would place two thirds of the zeros on the line.

Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman, mathematicians at Anthropic, condensed the proof into a five-page expert note. Anthropic thanks Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston for examining the paper on short notice. Eric Easley orchestrated the Lean 4 formalization of all five theorems; its top-level results carry no hypotheses, depend only on Lean's three standard axioms, and are sorry-free.

Anthropic writes that it does not expect these techniques to prove the Riemann hypothesis; the paper's remarks explain the method's limits. A footnote says similar encouragement helped Claude disprove the Jacobian conjecture, referring to an explicit polynomial counterexample Alpöge posted on X on July 20.
