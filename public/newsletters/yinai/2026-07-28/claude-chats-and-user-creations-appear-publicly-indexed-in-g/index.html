Forbes found nearly 600 indexed Claude chats last September

Google surfaced shared Claude chats last September too, and ChatGPT and Grok had matching exposures in 2025. The search that found this batch now returns nothing, but scraped copies and direct links outlive deindexing.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 28 July 2026 415 words · ~2 min

Joseph Cox's 404 Media article traced the weekend's discovery to a post on the Claude subreddit that published a Google dork, a search string tuned to surface claude.ai share pages. By the time Cox tested it, Google and DuckDuckGo returned nothing, though a second dork for Artifacts, the shareable apps and dashboards Claude builds, still worked at publication. Anthropic did not immediately respond to his request for comment. Cox also explained why deindexing settles little: after ChatGPT's equivalent exposure last year, a researcher scraped the exposed conversations, and a share page pulled from search results stays readable by anyone who holds the direct link.

Google had surfaced Claude conversations before. Iain Martin reported in Forbes last September that Google indexed just under 600 shared Claude chats, among them prompts from Anthropic's own team asking Claude for apps, games, and a "comedy Anthropic office simulator", plus transcripts carrying staff names and emails. Anthropic spokesperson Gabby Curtis told Forbes that the company does not share "chat directories or sitemaps of shared chats" with search engines and actively blocks crawlers from the site; conversations surfaced, on Anthropic's account, because users posted the links somewhere public. One identifiable user told Forbes they had never posted their work chat anywhere.

Chatbot rivals ran the same course in 2025. OpenAI's sharing flow included a "Make this chat discoverable" checkbox, and after Fast Company found thousands of indexed conversations in Google results, chief information security officer Dane Stuckey first called the labeling "sufficiently clear", then reversed, writing that the feature "introduced too many opportunities for folks to accidentally share things they didn't intend to". Engadget reported that OpenAI removed the option on July 31, 2025 and began scrubbing indexed chats from search engines. Forbes' September report counted Grok's exposure that August in the hundreds of thousands of conversations. Claude offers no discoverability toggle at all; its share pages reached Google once links circulated anyway.

Anthropic's answer came a day later. The Next Web reported that spokesperson Amie Rotherham called share links "not guessable or discoverable unless people choose to share them themselves", and that the site:claude.ai/share search now returns nothing on Google. The material The Next Web catalogued before the takedown went further than the first reports: clinical trial results listing patient identities, medical reports with patient names, documents with children's names and phone numbers, and legal consultation records. For anyone who has shared a chat, Cox notes that Claude's privacy and sharing settings can withdraw old share pages from public view.
