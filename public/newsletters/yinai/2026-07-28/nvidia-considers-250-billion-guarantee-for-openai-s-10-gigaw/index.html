A former uranium plant anchors Nvidia's $250 billion backstop

The campus Nvidia would guarantee sits on the Energy Department's former uranium-enrichment site in Piketon, with Japan pledging $33 billion for its gas plant; within a day of the talks surfacing, Nvidia's credit default swaps hit a record and Apple retook the top market valuation.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 28 July 2026 480 words · ~2 min

The campus behind the proposed guarantee sits on federal land in Piketon, Ohio, where the Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant once enriched uranium for national security programs. The U.S. Department of Energy announced the underlying partnership in March: SB Energy, the SoftBank subsidiary developing the project, is leasing the site, funding accelerated environmental cleanup, and building 10 gigawatts of new power generation alongside the computing campus, with development starting this year on 189 acres, a projected 10,000 construction jobs, and more than 2,000 permanent positions. Reuters, relaying reporting by The Wall Street Journal's Anissa Gardizy, Amrith Ramkumar and Corrie Driebusch, adds that the federal government controls the site's power and that Japan has pledged $33 billion for a natural gas plant there under the recent trade deal. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick helps determine access to the facility, and the first phase, about 800 megawatts, is due in 2028. The Energy Department frames the project under the administration's executive orders on AI, energy and data-center permitting, federal accommodation running opposite the year-long freeze New York put on permits for new hyperscale data centers.

The same account bounds what Nvidia's guarantee would cover: the campus lease and its debt financing, and not the chips inside, which sit in a separate discussion about Nvidia financing up to $350 billion of OpenAI's chip purchases. For OpenAI, leasing the campus would begin a move toward controlling its own infrastructure instead of renting from Microsoft, Amazon and Oracle.

The nearest precedent in the circular-financing structure runs far smaller. The Information reported that Google has agreed to backstop up to $44 billion of third-party data-center lease payments, up from $6.5 billion in September, with Anthropic among the tenants; The Next Web's account describes the guarantees as a sales tool for Google's TPUs, since cheaper debt lets developers build the projects that buy its chips, and cites Moody's analysts warning that the accounting liability is unlikely to reflect plausible future scenarios.

Credit markets repriced Nvidia within a day. Bloomberg counted more than $750 billion in prospective Nvidia deals once the backstop talks joined the $500 billion-plus SK Group partnership, which Nvidia's own release describes as letters of intent covering a 2-gigawatt SK Telecom AI cloud on Vera Rubin hardware and HBM4 memory co-development with SK hynix, first facility due in 2027. The Next Web reports that Nvidia's five-year credit default swap spread hit a record 82 basis points on Monday, its biggest single-day intraday jump since the contract began actively trading in November 2025, while shares fell nearly 5 percent and Apple reclaimed the position of world's most valuable company; the IMF and the Bank for International Settlements have both flagged circular AI financing as a systemic risk. Jensen Huang called the circularity charge "ridiculous". Gary Tan of Allspring Global Investments told Bloomberg that "capital is increasingly being used to fund future AI customers and infrastructure deployments".
