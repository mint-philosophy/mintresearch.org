Astra’s SRE-Bench result: 99.2% within four attempts

OpenAI’s launch evaluation measures understanding compiled software without source code, with 88.0% success on a single attempt.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 6 September 2026 317 words · ~2 min

OpenAI reported that GPT-6 Astra solved 88.0% of SRE-Bench challenges in one attempt and 99.2% within four attempts. These were results from its September 3 launch, highlighted by Chris (@ChrisGPT) on September 5. The benchmark tests reverse engineering: understanding what compiled software does when its original source code is unavailable.

Jeremy Spence of Columbia University and colleagues introduced the benchmark in their August 11 arXiv paper, The Next Challenge for Agentic Cybersecurity: A Realistic, Contamination-Free Reverse Engineering Benchmark. The team includes Vals AI researcher Nikil Ravi. Experts wrote 19 private programs from scratch, then produced 262 executable variants with different compilation settings and protections against analysis. Keeping the programs private is intended to prevent models from recognizing familiar public software and using remembered information to bypass the work of understanding it.

OpenAI's system card counts a challenge as fully solved only when the agent satisfies all six objectives. The four-attempt measure uses independent trials and credits the best outcome; token consumption includes all four trials. The 99.2% figure measures how many challenges Astra could solve when given repeated attempts. Astra's one-attempt score also exceeds GPT-5.6 Sol's four-attempt score in OpenAI's comparison.

Spence and colleagues designed tasks with outcomes that can be checked automatically. In the malware scenario, an agent must analyze a harmless simulated infection and create a cleanup tool that removes its effects while preserving benign data. Defenders investigating malware, firmware or proprietary applications often need to establish exactly what an executable does before deciding how to respond. The benchmark measures that program understanding separately from exploit development. Its authors identify the small number of underlying programs as a limitation on the breadth of the evaluation.

The benchmark team says it worked with OpenAI on the Astra evaluation and has begun exploring harder settings through further pilot evaluations. Its stated aim is to keep extending the benchmark as agents' reverse-engineering capabilities improve.
