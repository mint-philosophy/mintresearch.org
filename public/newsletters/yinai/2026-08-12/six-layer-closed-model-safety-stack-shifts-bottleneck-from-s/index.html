Stephen Casper’s six-layer case for closed-model safety

Casper combines specifications, filtered data, adversarial training, classifiers, probes, and user monitoring to argue that a narrowly defined technical problem is solved.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Codex · 12 August 2026 338 words · ~2 min

On X, Stephen Casper argues that technical safety for closed-weight AI is “solved” only in a bounded sense: a well-intentioned deployer using state-of-the-art tools should cause orders of magnitude fewer major incidents than malicious operators or organizations that implement the tools badly. He defines the problem as writing a sound safety specification and robustly aligning behavior to it; state of practice can lag, and open weights and governance remain outside the claim.

Casper asks deployers to write specifications that admit utility-safety trade-offs; filter hazardous and dual-use training data; adversarially safety-fine-tune at scale; combine constitutional classifiers with trajectory monitoring; run activation probes for harmful cognition; and monitor or restrict suspicious users. Each cited study tests one component, not all six as a combined system. Kyle O’Brien, Casper, and colleagues’ Deep Ignorance models filtered biorisk data without notable general-performance loss and stayed below an unfiltered baseline after 300 million tokens of adversarial fine-tuning, although staged fine-tuning plus in-context retrieval defeated every tested defense.

OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 system card reports more than 700,000 A100e GPU hours spent finding universal jailbreaks; one attack’s success rate fell from 10% to zero after mitigations. Anthropic’s Constitutional Classifiers++ reports 1,736 cumulative human red-team hours, one high-risk vulnerability, no jailbreak that answered all eight CBRN targets at half an undefended model’s detail, and a 0.05% production-traffic refusal rate. UK AISI’s Boundary Point Jailbreaking demonstrates automated black-box universal jailbreaks against Constitutional Classifiers and GPT-5’s input classifier. The attacks needed hundreds of thousands of queries and produced enough flags that the authors recommend batch-level monitoring; their GPT-5 accounts were exempt from normal banning that would likely interrupt such an attack.

Usman Anwar replied that safeguards do not exhaust technical safety. Casper restated his narrow definition and said the thread argues against further machine-learning-centered work on closed-model risk. Others asked for a failure-probability estimate, whether the stack survives rising capabilities, and whether catastrophic technical failure remains possible. Casper did not answer those questions in the thread, so his argument redirects the research agenda without estimating catastrophic risk.
