Thinking Machines releases Inkling, an open-weights base built for fine-tuning

The lab pitches its first open-weights model, a 975B-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts, as a customization base; in a launch demo, Inkling fine-tunes itself into a lipogram model in about 27 minutes.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 15 July 2026 478 words · ~2 min

In “Introducing Inkling,” published on July 15, Thinking Machines Lab announced its first open-weights model, the newest such release tracked here since Z.ai's GLM-5.2 on June 19. Inkling is a mixture-of-experts transformer with 975 billion total parameters, 41 billion active per token, and a context window reaching 1 million tokens, pretrained from scratch on 45 trillion tokens of text, images, audio, and video. “Inkling is not the strongest overall model available today, open or closed,” the announcement says; the lab instead offers the weights as a base for others to customize, citing multimodal breadth, adjustable reasoning effort, and fine-tuning on its Tinker platform.

The announcement's centerpiece has Inkling retrain itself. Given the goal of becoming a lipogram model that never uses the letter “e,” Inkling drafted the plan, generated its own evaluation and synthetic training data, ran a 96-step job on Tinker across three epochs, and loaded the new weights, closing the loop in about 27 minutes. Asked afterward what a team should do when it ships a large model, the retrained version answered without a single “e”: “party, thank staff, post a summary, watch for bugs, fix faults fast.”

The lab's technical write-up lists several departures from the common recipe. The mixture-of-experts layout follows DeepSeek-V3, with 256 routed experts, two shared, and six of the routed firing per token. Inkling swaps rotary position embeddings for relative positional embeddings, which the lab says extrapolate better to long sequences, and interleaves sliding-window and global attention at a 5-to-1 ratio. For multimodality it drops the usual encoder, feeding audio as dMel spectrograms and images as 40-by-40-pixel patches processed jointly with text. Training used a combined Muon-and-Adam optimizer and more than 30 million reinforcement-learning rollouts, over which a held-out reasoning reward rose from 0.264 to 0.356.

The benchmark table in the announcement shows Inkling competitive among open models and short of the leaders. Evaluated at reasoning effort 0.99, it reaches 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified, 63.8% on Terminal Bench 2.1, and 29.7% on text-only Humanity's Last Exam, well below closed systems such as Claude Fable 5, which posts 95.0% on SWE-bench. The lab presses its case on efficiency: as the effort setting climbs, Inkling matches Nemotron 3 Ultra on Terminal Bench while generating about a third as many tokens. Inkling's numbers come from the lab's own harness; competitors' scores are self-reported.

Thinking Machines is distributing Inkling broadly. Full weights sit on Hugging Face as both the original checkpoint and an NVFP4 version for NVIDIA Blackwell chips; fine-tuning is live on Tinker at a temporary 50% discount, and inference runs through TogetherAI, Fireworks, Modal, Databricks, and Baseten. A 276-billion-parameter Inkling-Small, with 12 billion active, is previewed but still in testing. On X, Jack Morris called Inkling the only open-weight model trained without distilling from OpenAI or Anthropic; the announcement adds that post-training was bootstrapped on synthetic data from open models including Kimi K2.5.
