New York pauses new hyperscale data centers as the AI backlash reaches Democratic primaries

Kathy Hochul's Executive Order 62 makes New York the first state to pause new hyperscale data centers; David Weigel reports that Sanders-aligned progressives are working to make AI industry PACs as toxic in Democratic primaries as AIPAC became.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 15 July 2026 391 words · ~2 min

Tracked here since June 20 as a patchwork of local moratoriums and permit disputes, the effort to slow AI data centers reached the state level on July 14, when Gov. Kathy Hochul signed Executive Order No. 62. Her office said that for up to a year, the Department of Environmental Conservation will not issue discretionary permits that are not already deemed complete. During the pause the state will draft a Generic Environmental Impact Statement on energy demand, water use, and air quality, alongside a Community Investment Framework covering local infrastructure, prevailing wages, and workforce development. Hochul said New York would "lead the way in creating the strongest standards in the nation." Fortune noted the state "has not been a destination for the biggest hyperscale data centers."

In a Semafor exclusive published the same day, David Weigel reported that Sanders-aligned progressives want to turn the AI industry into their next AIPAC: brand its campaign PACs as villains, then discredit any Democrat who accepts their money, the way earlier primaries made pro-Israel backing a liability. In North Carolina, Nida Allam, whose 2022 primary against Rep. Valerie Foushee centered on Foushee's AIPAC support, is challenging her again over data centers. In Wisconsin, socialist state Rep. Francesca Hong calls herself "the only one in this race that supports a one-year moratorium" on new hyperscale construction; a February Marquette Law School poll found 70% of Wisconsinites and 85% of Democrats saying the costs of data centers outweigh the benefits. In Arizona's Fourth District, environmental activist Kai Newkirk is challenging Rep. Greg Stanton, who backs charging operators more for energy; Newkirk says Stanton "takes tons of corporate PAC money from the same big tech interests."

The International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers endorsed Michigan candidate Matt Maasdam, warning that data-center bans eliminate union construction jobs; Senate hopeful Abdul El-Sayed and Sunrise Movement co-founder William Lawrence both campaign on stopping the centers, and rival Bridget Brink declined to endorse a pause. Sunrise president Denae Ávila-Dickson told Semafor that developers "will give $2 million to a candidate who's going to let them do that." Faiz Shakir, an adviser to Sen. Bernie Sanders, cast the divide as "fight oligarchy" against centrist "public-private partnerships." Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez proposed the federal Artificial Intelligence Data Center Moratorium Act in March; New York's order is the first statewide pause to be enacted.
