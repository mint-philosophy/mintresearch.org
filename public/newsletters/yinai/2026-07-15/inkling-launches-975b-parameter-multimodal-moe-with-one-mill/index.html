Thinking Machines' first open-weights model ships with a day-0 stack to run and train it

Inkling is a 975B-parameter multimodal Mixture-of-Experts with a million-token context and open weights; SGLang serving and Miles reinforcement-learning support landed day 0, so developers can run and retrain it from the start.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 15 July 2026 377 words · ~2 min

Thinking Machines Lab shipped its first open-weights model on July 15: Inkling, a Mixture-of-Experts system with 975 billion total parameters and 41 billion active per token, natively multimodal across text, images, and audio, with a context window that reaches a million tokens. The lab's announcement says Inkling was pretrained on 45 trillion tokens of text, images, audio, and video; the full weights are on Hugging Face with an NVFP4 checkpoint, and fine-tuning runs through its Tinker API. On X, LMSYS Org relayed the release and announced day-0 open serving and reinforcement-learning support, so developers can "run and shape it on an open stack."

The Thinking Machines writeup and a companion Hugging Face post detail where Inkling departs from a standard transformer. Short convolutions run on the residual stream. Attention carries relative positional embeddings in place of rotary encodings and interleaves sliding-window and global layers at a 5:1 ratio. A "shared expert sink" design sets two always-on experts competing against 256 routed experts under one normalization; six routed experts fire per token through a sigmoid router with auxiliary-loss-free load balancing. Audio enters as dMel spectrograms and images as 40-by-40 pixel patches, so one model reasons over text, pictures, and sound. A controllable thinking-effort setting trades tokens spent against accuracy; at effort 0.99, the top of the dial, the lab reports 97.1% on AIME 2026, 87.2% on GPQA Diamond, and 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified. A preview model, Inkling-Small, ships alongside at 276 billion parameters with 12 billion active.

In the LMSYS post, the SGLang team and Thinking Machines report Inkling serving at 71,700 input tokens per second and 171 tokens per second of per-user decode on Nvidia Blackwell hardware. The serving path adds speculative decoding from an eight-layer EAGLE drafter and a Modal-trained DFlash drafter, prefill-decode disaggregation, NVFP4 and bf16 checkpoints, AMD support, tiered HiCache, and multi-LoRA serving. Reinforcement learning runs on Miles, a customized Megatron backend that supports full-parameter and LoRA training across text, image, and audio. Custom kernels, routing replay, and cross-runtime parameter synchronization hold training and inference numerically aligned, which the post credits for steady RL gains on text-only and multimodal reasoning tasks.

The lab lists the weights as live through TogetherAI, Fireworks, Modal, Databricks, and Baseten, and running under SGLang, vLLM, and llama.cpp.
