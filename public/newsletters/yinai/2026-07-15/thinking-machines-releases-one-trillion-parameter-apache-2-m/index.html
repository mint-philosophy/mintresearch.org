Thinking Machines' Inkling, an American entry in the open trillion-parameter field

The July 15 release runs 975 billion parameters, 41 billion of them active per token, under Apache 2.0, and takes text, images, and audio natively. It beats fellow American model Nemotron 3 Ultra on the published benchmarks, trails GLM 5.2 and Kimi K2.6, and is pitched as a base to build on.

The Minty Newsroom · Edited by Fable · 15 July 2026 383 words · ~2 min

Tracked here since Meituan opened LongCat 2.0 on July 1, the trillion-parameter open-weights field gained a major American entry on July 15. Thinking Machines introduced Inkling in a blog post: a 975-billion-parameter sparse model that activates 41 billion parameters per token. The Hugging Face model card lists the license as Apache 2.0 and describes a 66-layer decoder-only transformer that routes each token to 6 of 256 experts plus 2 shared, with native input across text, images, and audio. The blog reports pretraining on 45 trillion tokens of text, images, audio, and video, carried out on NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems. On Bluesky, Nathan Lambert relayed the release and called Inkling the "new best American model."

The blog's benchmark table, run at the model's maximum thinking-effort setting, puts Inkling ahead of Nemotron 3 Ultra, the other American model in the comparison: 46.0% against 37.4% on Humanity's Last Exam with tools, and 77.6% against 70.7% on SWEBench Verified. The leading Chinese open models still sit above it on agentic and multimodal work. GLM 5.2 scores 80.0% on SWEBench Verified and 54.7% on Humanity's Last Exam, and on the MMMU Pro multimodal test Kimi K2.6 reaches 79.0% to Inkling's 73.5%. On the AIME 2026 math benchmark, Inkling, Kimi, and GLM finish within three points of one another: Inkling at 97.1%, Kimi at 96.4%, GLM at 99.2%. Thinking Machines writes that "Inkling is not the strongest overall model available today, open or closed," and pitches the model as an open-weights base for customization.

Thinking Machines has Inkling on its Tinker fine-tuning platform, and Hugging Face carries the full weights as both the original checkpoint and an NVFP4 checkpoint; listed deployment partners include Together AI, Fireworks, Databricks, and Baseten. Activating 41 billion of 975 billion parameters keeps inference cheap for anyone with the memory to hold the full weights, and the Apache 2.0 terms leave them free to fine-tune it. The company also previewed Inkling-Small, a 276-billion-parameter model with 12 billion active that, per the blog, matches or exceeds the full-size model on many benchmarks at under a third its size; its full weights follow once testing is complete. Post-training ran reinforcement learning over more than 30 million rollouts, and the blog credits a hybrid optimizer: Muon for the large weight matrices, Adam for the rest.
